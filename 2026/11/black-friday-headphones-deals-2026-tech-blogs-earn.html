Black Friday 2026 falls on November 27, and the headphone deals are already live. The Sony WH-1000XM6, the flagship noise-cancelling headphone most buyers start their search with, was seen at $348 on Amazon on November 20, 2026, against a list price of $459.99. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) were seen at $369 against a $449 list price on the same day. The Apple AirPods Max 2 were seen at $419 against a $549 list price. And the value play of the whole event, the standard Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen), was seen at $169 in Sandstone against a $359 list price, more than half off. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs from November 19 to November 30, 2026, and Best Buy's doorbusters run from November 19 to November 28, so the sale is a twelve-day window, and for tech bloggers it is one of the highest-traffic windows of the year. This guide covers the verified deals and the playbook for turning headphone deal searches into ad revenue before the window closes.

Why Headphones Are the Black Friday Category Tech Blogs Can't Ignore

Every Black Friday has a hero category, the one shoppers search for first and compare longest. In 2026, headphones are that category. Almost every major model got a meaningful update this year, and the updates pushed last year's flagships down the price ladder at exactly the right moment. Apple finally updated the AirPods Max, releasing the AirPods Max 2 in March 2026 with the H2 chip and up to 20 hours of battery life. Sony's WH-1000XM6 arrived with a new QN3 processor, twelve microphones, LDAC support, up to 30 hours of battery with noise cancelling on, and a folding design. Bose refreshed its QuietComfort line with a second generation of both the standard and Ultra models. When three brands refresh their flagships in the same year, the deal tables practically write themselves, and readers arrive with a very specific question: which one should I buy, and is this price real?

Advertisement

That question is the whole opportunity for a tech blog. A shopper searching for "Sony WH-1000XM6 Black Friday deal" is not browsing. They have a wallet open and a decision to make within minutes. They want a current price, a comparison against the obvious alternative, and a reason to trust the page in front of them. A blog that publishes the verified price, names the retailer, dates the sighting, and explains the trade-off in plain language earns the click, the dwell time, and the return visit. The ad revenue follows the attention.

The timing matters as much as the content. The deals started before the day itself, and by Black Friday many of the best prices will already have been live for a week. The category also splits cleanly into buyer types, and each type searches differently: the commuter wants the best noise cancelling, the iPhone owner wants the best AirPods deal, and the budget buyer wants the cheapest good pair. One post that answers all of those searches can rank for a dozen queries at once.

The 2026 Flagship Battle: Sony, Bose and Apple

The top of the market is a three-way fight, and all three flagships have been seen well below list price before Black Friday itself. Here is where each one stands, with prices verified in the days before publication.

Sony WH-1000XM6: The Best Pair for Most People

The Sony WH-1000XM6 is the default recommendation in almost every 2026 buying guide, and the deal makes the case stronger. It was seen at $348 on Amazon on November 20, 2026, against a list price of $459.99, a saving of nearly $112. Best Buy and Sony direct had it at $378 in a price check on November 17, 2026, so the Amazon sighting undercut the stores by $30. The headphone itself is Sony's most complete flagship: the QN3 processor, twelve microphones feeding the noise cancelling, LDAC high-resolution audio support, up to 30 hours of battery life with noise cancelling switched on, and a folding design that finally makes the flagship easy to pack for a flight. For a blogger, the XM6 is the anchor of the whole post: the model with the most search volume and the clearest price story.

Sony WH-1000XM5: The Rival in Sony's Own Lineup

The Sony WH-1000XM5 was the flagship until the XM6 arrived, and its price is now the best argument against buying the newest model. It was listed at $229.99 at Sony direct and Best Buy in the November 17, 2026 price check, and some colors had been seen as low as $186 in early November. That is roughly $120 less than the XM6's seen price, and for many buyers the difference buys nothing they will notice on a commute or a flight. The XM5 still has excellent noise cancelling, strong battery life, and the comfortable fit that made it the default recommendation for two years. This is the comparison that makes a deal post useful instead of decorative. A reader choosing between $348 and $229.99 needs someone to say plainly what the extra money buys: a newer processor, more microphones, a folding design, and a small step up in noise cancelling. For a buyer who wants great noise cancelling for the office and the train, the XM5 at $229.99 is the smarter purchase.

Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen): The Silence King

Bose has owned the "best noise cancelling" conversation for years, and the QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) keep that crown. They were seen at $369 on Amazon on November 20, 2026, against a list price of $449, and other listings had them around $360 in early November. It offers up to 30 hours of battery life with noise cancelling on, aptX Adaptive support, lossless audio over USB-C, and a Cinema Mode for movies. If the reader's priority is silence above everything else, this is the pair to recommend, and the $369 price is the lowest it has been seen this season. Put the Ultra (2nd Gen) next to the Sony XM6 and the AirPods Max 2 in your comparison table, and let the reader pick by priority: Sony for the best all-rounder, Bose for the strongest noise cancelling, Apple for the ecosystem.

Apple AirPods Max 2 and AirPods Pro 3: The iPhone Buyer's Choice

Apple's audio lineup finally feels current again. The AirPods Max 2, announced in March 2026, moved to the H2 chip, which brought better active noise cancellation, Adaptive Audio, Live Translation, and up to 20 hours of battery life. It was seen at $419 on Amazon on November 20, 2026, against a list price of $549, a saving of $130. Walmart had it at $449 in Midnight, so Amazon's $419 was the price to beat when this guide was written. The AirPods Pro 3 are the more important Apple deal for most readers, because they are the earbuds most iPhone owners will actually buy. Walmart had them at $179, and Best Buy listed them at $179.99. At that price they are the obvious recommendation for anyone with an iPhone, and they belong in every headphone deal post even though they are earbuds rather than headphones, because the searcher typing "AirPods Black Friday deal" does not care about the distinction. For bloggers, the Apple section pulls in the enormous Apple deal audience, which spikes every Black Friday.

The Value Tier: Where the Real Bargains Hide

The flagships get the headlines, but the value tier is where Black Friday does its best work. These are the headphones that were already good value at full price and are now genuinely cheap, and they are the models your readers will buy in the largest numbers.

Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen): The Deal of the Event

The standard Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen) are the value play of the entire Black Friday event. Bose and Best Buy listed them at $199 against a list price of $359, and Amazon had the Sandstone color seen at $169 on November 20, 2026, which is 53 percent below list. This is a current-generation headphone from the brand most associated with noise cancelling, at a price that used to buy only the budget tier. It weighs 230 grams, runs up to 24 hours with noise cancelling on, and supports lossless audio over USB-C. For a reader who wants the Bose name and Bose quiet without the Ultra price, this is the recommendation. When a deal is this strong, say so plainly.

Sony's Budget Pair: WH-CH720N at $88

The Sony WH-CH720N was listed at $88 at Best Buy in the November 17, 2026 price check, and it is the cheapest credible noise-cancelling headphone in this year's sale. The newer WH-CH730N, with up to 50 hours of battery with noise cancelling on, a 207-gram weight, Bluetooth 6.0 and multipoint connection, sits in the $99 to $119 range depending on the retailer. These are the headphones for the student, the first-time buyer, and the reader who wants noise cancelling for the library or the office without spending flagship money. They will not match the XM6, and your post should say that honestly, but at $88 the WH-CH720N is an easy recommendation, and it is the model most likely to sell out first.

Beats, Sennheiser, Soundcore and Samsung: The Rest of the Table

The rest of the table fills out the comparison and catches the searches the flagships miss. The Beats Studio Pro was seen at $139.95 on Amazon against a list price of $299.99, the Beats Solo 4 at $99 against a list price of $199.95, the Beats Studio Buds+ at $89.95 against a list price of $169.95, and the Powerbeats Pro 2 at $179.95 against a list price of $249.99. The Sennheiser Momentum 4 was seen at $179.95 on Amazon on November 20, 2026, against a list price of $299.99, and it is the pick for the reader who cares about battery life above all else. The Samsung Galaxy Buds4 Pro were seen at $179.99 against a list price of $249.99, and they are the natural recommendation for Samsung phone owners in the same way the AirPods Pro 3 are the recommendation for iPhone owners. The Soundcore Liberty 5 Pro earbuds were seen at $129.99, the Liberty 5 at $75.99 against a list price of $129.99, and the Soundcore Space One over-ear pair sits under $100 with adaptive noise cancelling and LDAC support. Put all of these in one comparison table with four columns: model, best seen price, list price, and best for. Update the table every morning during Black Friday Week.

How to Read a Headphone Deal Like a Pro

Not every price that looks like a deal is one, and your readers need you to explain the difference. The first rule is to compare against the street price, not the list price. A headphone listed at $459.99 that has been selling at $378 for a month is not 24 percent off when it drops to $348. It is $30 off its real price, which is still a good deal, but it is not the bargain the headline implies. The honest framing is the one your readers will reward: state the list price, state the recent street price, and state the seen price with the date. Three numbers, no exaggeration. The second rule is to watch for color-specific pricing. The Bose QuietComfort at $169 was the Sandstone color, the Sony XM6 at $378 was the Black version, and the AirPods Max 2 at $449 at Walmart was Midnight. Name the color in your post. It costs one sentence and saves a reader's trust. Finally, remember that prices change hourly during this event. Every price in this guide is a dated sighting, checked against retailer listings on November 20, 2026, or earlier in the week as noted. Deal prices can change several times a day, so check the current price before you buy belongs near the top, right under the first price table, where the reader will actually see it.

The Blogger Playbook: Turning Audio Deals Into Ad Revenue

Knowing the deals is half the job. The other half is publishing them in a way that earns traffic and revenue, and the playbook for headphone deals is the same playbook that works for every Black Friday category. It starts with publishing early. The deals went live on November 19. A post published on November 20 has a week to gather clicks, earn links, and climb the rankings before Black Friday itself, while a post published on November 26 competes with every other late post in the most crowded search window of the year. Early beats perfect. Publish the verified prices you have, then update the post every morning. Google rewards the page that changes, because a changing page is a live page.

The second move is the comparison table. Put the flagship table near the top of the post, above the fold if you can, with the model, the best seen price, the list price, and the best-for label. Readers decide in seconds whether your page answers their question, and a table answers it faster than any paragraph. The third move is keyword targeting. The queries that matter pair a model name with a deal word: "Sony WH-1000XM6 Black Friday deal", "Bose QuietComfort Ultra Black Friday price", "AirPods Max 2 Black Friday", "best noise cancelling headphones Black Friday 2026". Use the exact model names in your headings, and use the phrase "Black Friday" in the title, the first paragraph, and at least two headings. Natural writing that names the products correctly will rank better than a page that repeats the keyword until it reads like a robot wrote it.

The fourth move is internal linking. A reader who came for the XM6 price may also want a VPN for streaming on the flight where they will use the headphones, or AI tools to edit the podcast they will listen to on them. Link to your related coverage naturally, in the flow of the article, not in a block of links at the bottom that nobody clicks. Our guides to Black Friday VPN deals and Black Friday AI tools deals sit naturally alongside a headphone post, because the same reader is shopping for all three. Our Black Friday gaming deals guide reaches the reader who wants a headset that works for games as well as music. Every internal link is a second page view, and a second page view is a second set of ad impressions.

The fifth move is the update rhythm. During Black Friday Week, update the post every morning and say so at the top: "Prices checked November 23, 2026." A dated freshness line tells the reader the prices are current, and it tells Google the page is maintained. When a price drops, move it to the top of the table. When a deal sells out, mark it sold out instead of deleting it, because a sold-out deal that you flagged honestly is proof that your prices were real. When a new deal appears, add it the same day. The sixth move is monetization itself. Display ads earn from the page views, referral and affiliate links earn from the purchases, and the email capture earns for months afterwards, because a reader who trusted your headphone prices will trust your next deal guide too. Put the email capture below the comparison table, not in a popup that blocks the prices.

Which Headphones Should You Actually Recommend?

After all the prices and tables, the reader wants a straight answer. For most people, buy the Sony WH-1000XM6 at $348 if you can still find it at that price. It is the best all-rounder and the pair that will still feel current in two years. For the reader whose priority is silence above everything else, buy the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) at $369. For the iPhone owner, buy the AirPods Pro 3 at $179, and consider the AirPods Max 2 at $419 only if you specifically want over-ear Apple headphones. For the reader on a budget, buy the Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen) at $199, or at $169 in Sandstone if that color is still available, because it is a current-generation flagship-family headphone at a budget price. For the reader with under $100, buy the Sony WH-CH720N at $88, or the Soundcore Space One under $100 if you want the longest battery life in the budget tier. Those five recommendations cover every buyer in the market.

After Black Friday: Cyber Monday and the Gift-Guide Window

Black Friday ends on November 27, but the sale does not. Cyber Monday follows on November 30, and the headphone deals historically carry over, with a few models dropping further and a few selling out entirely. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs through November 30, which means the same listings stay live for the whole window. Your job as a blogger is to keep updating the same post through Cyber Monday, then to pivot the same content into a gift guide. "Best headphones to gift this Christmas" is a search that starts the day after Cyber Monday and runs through December 24, and it is the same article with a new title and a new introduction. The deal post you publish today is not a one-week asset. It is the foundation of three months of content, and the bloggers who plan that far ahead are the ones whose traffic does not collapse on December 1.
