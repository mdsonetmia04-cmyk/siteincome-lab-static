Black Friday 2026 is five days away, and the biggest deals of the year are already live. Apple's own four-day shopping event starts on Friday, November 27, with gift cards worth up to $300, while Amazon is already on day four of a twelve-day Black Friday sale with the AirPods Pro 3 down to $169, the Apple Watch SE 3 at $179, and the 14-inch M6 MacBook Pro at $1,799. For shoppers, this is the cheapest week of the year to buy Apple gear. For tech bloggers, it is the most profitable week of the year to publish. This post breaks down every deal that matters and shows exactly how publishers turn Apple week search traffic into ad revenue.

Why Apple Week Is the Biggest Traffic Week of the Year for Tech Blogs

Every November, the same pattern repeats. Millions of people who never read a tech blog all year suddenly search for one thing: the lowest price on the Apple product they have been waiting to buy. They search "AirPods Pro 3 Black Friday deal" and "MacBook Air Black Friday 2026" and "Apple Watch SE 3 sale" — and the blogs that published early, with real prices and honest comparisons, collect that traffic. Apple products are the perfect deal-season topic: demand is enormous, prices barely move all year, and the discount window is short. A reader who finds your post on November 23 may click three or four of your pages before the weekend is over.

The traffic is also unusually valuable. Shoppers are in buying mode, which means they click more ads, stay longer on comparison pages, and return to the same page several times to check whether a price has dropped further. A single well-ranked Apple deals post can earn more in one week than a dozen ordinary posts earn in a quarter. Black Friday falls on November 27, 2026, Amazon was already on day four of a twelve-day Black Friday event by November 22, and CNN Underscored's deals team shopped 147 early sales to find the 98 best deals worth buying now. The blogs that win this week are the ones that published before the rush and update their prices every day.

Apple's Own Black Friday Event: Gift Cards Up to $300

Apple announced its 2026 Black Friday event on November 19, and it follows Apple's usual formula: no direct discounts, but a gift card with the purchase of an eligible product. The event runs from November 27 at 12 a.m. Pacific Time through November 30 at 11:59 p.m. Pacific Time, online and in Apple Stores. This year the maximum gift card is $300, up from $250 in previous years — but the structure matters more than the headline number.

A 16-inch MacBook Pro with an M5 Pro or M5 Max chip earns the full $300 gift card. A 14-inch MacBook Pro with the same chips earns $250, and a 13-inch or 15-inch MacBook Air with the M5 chip earns $200. An iPad Pro earns $125, an iPad Air earns $100, and a Mac mini earns $100. The iPhone Air earns $100, the iPhone 16 and iPhone 17 earn $75, and the iPhone 17e earns $50. The Apple Watch Ultra 4 earns $100, while the Apple Watch Series 12 and Apple Watch SE 3 earn $50 each. AirPods Pro 3 earn a $50 gift card, AirPods Max 2 earn $75, and the AirPods 5 earn $25. Beats 360 headphones earn $75. For the first time ever, Apple is also offering a $25 gift card with select third-party accessories from Anker, Belkin, Mophie, Nimble, Rains, and Twelve South.

There is one catch every shopper should understand. The gift card cannot be used to reduce the price of the product you are buying. You pay full price, and the gift card is valid only for a future purchase from Apple — hardware, software, subscriptions like Apple Music and Apple TV, App Store apps, or iCloud+ storage. That makes Apple's event a good deal if you were already planning to buy two Apple products, and a weak deal if you only wanted one. A $50 gift card on a $249 pair of AirPods Pro 3 sounds nice until you realize Amazon is selling the same earbuds for $169 right now — $80 off in real money. Publishers who explain this difference honestly earn the trust that turns one-time deal hunters into repeat readers.

What Apple Left Out of Its Own Event

Just as important is what is not included. Apple's newest products are excluded from the gift-card promotion, including the iPhone 18 Pro, the iPhone 18 Pro Max, the iPhone Duo, the MacBook Neo, the latest iPad mini, the Apple TV 4K, the iMac, and the Mac Studio. If a reader wants the very latest iPhone, Apple's own store will not give them a gift card for it — but Amazon is offering up to $1,200 off the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Duo, according to MacRumors' deal tracking. That gap between Apple's official event and the real street prices is exactly where a good deals blog earns its keep.

The Real Discounts Are at Amazon — and They Are Already Live

Amazon's Black Friday Week is a twelve-day event, and by November 22 it had already reached day four, with new price drops landing every morning. The headline prices as of November 22: AirPods Pro 3 are $169, down from $249. AirPods Max 2 are $419, down from $549. The Apple Watch SE 3 starts at $179, down from $249. The Apple Watch Series 12 is $329 for the 42mm model, down from $399, and $379 for the 46mm model, down from $449. Beats 360 headphones, brand new this year, are $249, down from $349. The AirTag 2 is $23, down from $29. The 13-inch M5 MacBook Air is $1,049, down from $1,299, the entry-level 14-inch M6 MacBook Pro is $1,799, down from $1,999, and the MacBook Neo is $649, down from $699. These are real, verified prices from deal trackers, not list prices with a fake discount attached.

AirPods Deals: The $169 AirPods Pro 3 Leads the Pack

AirPods are always the single biggest Apple deal category, and 2026 is no exception. The AirPods Pro 3 at $169 is the deal of the week so far: $80 off the $249 list price, the lowest price the earbuds have reached, and a discount on a product that was released recently enough that most buyers have not owned it yet. That price may hold through Black Friday itself, which means the Black Friday price is live now, five days early — and waiting gains you nothing.

The AirPods Max 2 at $419, down from $549, is the second-biggest audio deal: $130 off Apple's premium over-ear headphones. The Beats 360 at $249, down from $349, is a $100 discount on a brand-new product — new headphones almost never go on sale this quickly. Lead with the AirPods Pro 3: it has the highest search volume and the clearest saving, and audio deals convert better than any other Apple category, because a reader shopping for earbuds makes a fast, low-risk decision.

Apple Watch Deals: SE 3 at $179, Series 12 at $329

The Apple Watch SE 3 at $179 is the best value in Apple's entire Black Friday lineup: down from $249, $70 off, and the first discount deal trackers have recorded on this model all season. For a reader who wants an Apple Watch without paying for the most expensive model, this is the obvious pick — and for a blogger, the obvious headline after AirPods. The SE 3 gives you the core Apple Watch experience at a price that finally feels like a genuine sale rather than a token cut.

The Apple Watch Series 12 is $329 for the 42mm GPS model, down from $399, and $379 for the 46mm GPS model, down from $449. The Apple Watch Ultra 4 is not discounted as deeply at Amazon, but it earns a $100 gift card at the Apple Store — one of the few cases where the Apple Store wins, and a good deals post says that plainly. Smartwatch deals work well for publishers because the decision is comparison-heavy: readers want to know whether the SE 3 is enough and whether the Series 12 is worth the extra $150.

Mac Deals: MacBook Air at $1,049, MacBook Pro at $1,799, MacBook Neo at $649

Mac deals are where the biggest dollar savings live. The 13-inch M5 MacBook Air at $1,049, down from $1,299, is $250 off — the kind of round-number saving that makes a great headline. The 14-inch M6 MacBook Pro with 16GB of RAM and 1TB of storage is $1,799 at Amazon, down from $1,999. The MacBook Neo, Apple's most affordable laptop, is $649, down from $699, a $50 discount that brings a brand-new Mac under $650 for the first time.

The Mac category is also where Apple's gift-card event and Amazon's price cuts diverge most sharply. A 13-inch MacBook Air bought at the Apple Store earns a $200 gift card but costs full price; the same machine at Amazon costs $250 less in cash. Cash beats a gift card almost every time. Bloggers who do this simple math in public give readers a reason to trust them, and trusted readers come back for the next sale.

iPhone Deals: Up to $1,200 Off the Newest Models

iPhone deals are the most searched Apple deals of Black Friday, and this year the headline is that the newest models are discounted at all. MacRumors' deal tracking lists the iPhone 18 Pro and the iPhone Duo at up to $1,200 off — an extraordinary discount on phones Apple itself excluded from its gift-card event. The exact saving depends on the model, the storage, and the carrier terms, which is why a good iPhone deals post must be specific: name the model, name the price, name the condition. Vague iPhone coverage is worse than no iPhone coverage.

The older models have their own logic. The iPhone 17 and iPhone 16 earn $75 gift cards at the Apple Store, the iPhone Air earns $100, and the iPhone 17e earns $50 — but Amazon and the carriers will almost always beat those values with direct price cuts or trade-in offers. One warning: iPhone prices change faster than any other Apple category during deal week, and carrier terms make some headline prices misleading. A "$1,200 off" price that requires a specific carrier plan and a trade-in is not the same as $1,200 off the phone. Readers forgive a price that changed; they do not forgive a price that was never real.

iPad, Beats and Accessories: The Quiet Winners

The quiet winners of Apple week are the categories nobody writes about first. The iPad Air earns a $100 gift card at the Apple Store and the iPad Pro earns $125, while Amazon's early discounts on the new OLED iPad mini start at $669. Beats headphones are having an unusually strong year: the Beats 360 at $249 is $100 off, the Beats Studio Pro has been spotted at $140, down from $300, and the Beats Pill and Beats Solo 4 earn gift cards at the Apple Store. The AirTag 2 at $23, down from $29, is the cheapest Apple product on sale this week and the easiest impulse buy.

Accessories deserve a section in every Apple deals post, not a footnote. They are cheap, easy to buy, and convert at high rates. Apple's first-ever $25 gift card on third-party accessories from Anker, Belkin, Mophie, Nimble, Rains, and Twelve South is also a story in itself: Apple has never included other brands in its Black Friday event before, and that change tells readers how competitive this year's sale has become.

How Tech Blogs Turn Apple Week Into Ad Revenue

Now the part that matters most for this blog's readers: how does a deals post actually make money? First, the post ranks for high-volume searches like "AirPods Pro 3 Black Friday deal" and "MacBook Air Black Friday 2026", bringing in thousands of visitors in a few days. Second, those visitors see display ads — banners, native ads, and popunders — and every thousand views earns the publisher money. Third, internal links to related coverage multiply the pageviews each visitor generates. Fourth, daily updates keep the post ranking while competing posts go stale. That is the whole business model, and Apple week is when it pays best.

Ad placement matters more during deal week than at any other time of year. Readers arrive, scan for the price they want, and leave — so the ads that earn are the ones they actually see in the first screen. A 728x90 banner near the top, a 300x250 unit beside the first deal table, and a native unit inside the article all outperform ads buried at the bottom. Our own site follows this logic: the 300x250 and 160x600 units sit where deal hunters look, and the popunder fires once per visit. The publishers who earn most are not the ones with the most ads; they are the ones whose ads sit where the reader's eyes already are.

The format of the post matters too. Deal roundups that rank share the same structure: a short opening that names the best deal in the first two sentences, a list of prices with the saving shown in dollars, a short section per product category, and a clear statement of when the prices were last checked. Google's November 2026 core update rewarded pages that demonstrate real expertise and fresh information: a price list from last week is worse than useless, and a post that updates daily will outrank a post that was better written but abandoned. Comparison content is the second engine of deal-week revenue: a reader who searches "AirPods Pro 3 vs AirPods Max 2 Black Friday" is closer to buying than a reader who searches "Black Friday deals", and comparison pages earn more per visitor because the reader stays longer and clicks more.

The Publisher Playbook: Publish Early, Update Daily, Link Everything

The publishers who win Black Friday follow the same playbook every year. Publish the roundup at least a week before Black Friday, when the first early deals appear, so the page has time to rank before the search peak. Update the prices every morning during deal week, and say so on the page — "Prices checked November 23, 2026" is a trust signal for readers and a freshness signal for Google. Link every product section to your related coverage: the laptop roundup links to the smartphone roundup, the smartphone roundup links to the Cyber Monday post, and everything links back to the main deals hub. Internal links cost nothing to add, and they are the difference between a visitor who reads one page and a visitor who reads four.

Timing the updates is a discipline. Amazon drops new prices in the morning, and the blogs that update by midday capture the afternoon search wave. A price that changed at 9 a.m. and is still wrong on your page at 9 p.m. is a reader you have lost, probably forever. The best deal blogs treat the week like a newsroom: one person watches the prices, one person updates the page, and the page itself carries a visible "last updated" note.

Mistakes That Cost Bloggers Money During Deal Season

The most expensive mistake is publishing prices you never verified. A "$1,200 off iPhone" headline that turns out to require a carrier plan and a trade-in will cost you readers, and a price that was never real will cost you your ranking when readers bounce in seconds. Verify every price against a live retailer page or a reputable deal tracker before you publish it, and remove any deal the moment it expires. A dead deal on a live page is a signal to Google that the page is stale, and to readers that the blog cannot be trusted.

The second mistake is writing for the search engine instead of the shopper. Keyword-stuffed headings and ten paragraphs of filler before the first price will not rank and will not convert. Readers came for a price: give it to them in the first screen, then give them the context that helps them decide. The third mistake is forgetting the days after Black Friday. Cyber Monday on November 30 is a second peak, and the publishers who keep updating their posts through the weekend capture it. A post that stops updating on Friday night leaves money on the table for the entire weekend.

Finally, do not overload the page with ads during deal week. A page that is hard to read is a page readers abandon, and an abandoned page earns nothing. One popunder per visit, a banner near the top, a rectangle beside the content, and a native unit inside the article is enough. The goal is a page that loads fast, shows the price immediately, and earns from the traffic it keeps.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 is the best week of the year to buy Apple gear, and the best week of the year to publish about it. The AirPods Pro 3 at $169, the Apple Watch SE 3 at $179, the MacBook Air at $1,049, and the iPhone 18 Pro at up to $1,200 off are real prices, live now, five days before Black Friday itself. Apple's own event offers gift cards up to $300 from November 27 through November 30, but for most shoppers Amazon's cash discounts are the better deal — and saying so honestly is what makes a deals blog worth reading. For publishers, the lesson is the same as every year: publish early, update daily, link everything, and respect the reader's time. Do that, and Apple week will pay for the quiet months that follow.
