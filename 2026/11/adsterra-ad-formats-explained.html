Adsterra Ad Formats Explained: Popunder, Social Bar, Native, Banners, Direct Link




Getting approved on Adsterra takes ten minutes. The harder decision comes right after: which ad formats do you actually run? Adsterra offers seven, and they range from nearly invisible to "your readers will complain." Pick wrong and you either leave money on the table or annoy the audience you worked to build.

This guide walks through every format — what your visitor actually experiences, how intrusive it is, what it's best for — and ends with the starter setup I'd recommend for a new blog. Everything below is checked against Adsterra's official pages and help docs (November 2026).

Now the detail on each.

1. Popunder — the flagship (and the most controversial)

What it is: When a visitor clicks anywhere on your page, a full ad page opens in a new browser tab behind the current window. The visitor doesn't see it immediately — they find it later when they close or switch tabs. Because the page fully loads in the background, popunders get essentially 100% viewability, which is why advertisers pay a premium for them.

Why publishers love it: It's consistently Adsterra's highest-earning format. Third-party publisher reports put Tier-1 popunder CPMs anywhere from $3 to $18 per 1,000 impressions (these are community-reported ranges, not official numbers — your actual rate depends on GEO, niche, and traffic quality). It also needs no ad slot: there's no banner box to design around, and it works on every page automatically.

The honest downside: Popunders are the format readers complain about most. Open three tabs of popunders from one site and you'll never visit that site again — and your visitors feel the same. Adsterra itself advises against placing more than one popunder code on the same page, since multiple codes can conflict. It's also desktop-skewed: on mobile browsers, popunders behave less reliably and pay less.

Use it when: you have desktop-heavy traffic and want maximum revenue per visitor. Skip or delay it when: your blog is brand-new and you're still building reader trust — nothing kills return visits faster than surprise tabs.

2. Social Bar — Adsterra's exclusive format

What it is: Social Bar is Adsterra's proprietary format — you won't find it on AdSense or most other networks. One line of code generates a rotation of interactive widgets that appear within your page: chat-head icons, animated notification-style messages, small custom banners, even interstitial-style creatives. Only one creative shows at a time, so the page never looks cluttered.

Why it matters: Two big claims from Adsterra's own documentation: it reaches 100% of your traffic (no opt-in, no subscriptions, works on iOS where classic web push is restricted), and it delivers up to 30× higher click-through rates than traditional web push notifications. Adsterra also says that by 2026, about 87% of its publishers had tried Social Bar — treat both numbers as the company's own marketing, but the format's popularity among publishers is real and widely reported.

The honest downside: "Less intrusive than popunder" doesn't mean invisible. Chat heads and animated widgets still compete for attention with your content, and on a text-heavy blog they can feel gimmicky. Over-customization is also a trap: the 20+ templates are battle-tested, and beginners do better picking a default skin than designing their own.

Use it when: you have mobile traffic (Social Bar is OS-friendly and works great on small screens) and want strong earnings without a popunder's reputation cost. It's the best single-format compromise on the network.

3. In-Page Push — notifications without the opt-in

What it is: In-Page Push mimics a push notification — a small message box styled like an OS or messaging-app notification — but it renders inside the webpage, not at the OS level. That distinction matters: classic web push requires the visitor to subscribe first, which means you only monetize the small fraction who opted in. In-Page Push monetizes everyone currently browsing, with no subscription step.

Why publishers use it: Adsterra's benchmarks put In-Page Push CTRs at 15–33% (their numbers, for advertisers, but the format's engagement is genuinely high). It's fully customizable — Android, Windows, iOS, or macOS notification styles — and works on all browsers including iOS.

The honest downside: It looks like a system notification, which is exactly why it gets clicks — and exactly why some visitors find it deceptive. There's a fine line between "native-feeling" and "tricking people," and push-style creatives walk it. If your blog's brand is trust (tutorials, reviews, education), heavy In-Page Push can undermine it.

Use it when: your niche is offer-friendly (VPN, antivirus, utilities, games, downloads — Adsterra's own guide lists these as top converters for In-Page Push). Think twice when: your readers came for your expertise and would notice the trick.

4. Native — the polite format

What it is: Native ads are styled to blend with your site's content — a headline, a thumbnail, a short description that looks like another article card in your feed or sidebar. The reader can tell it's an ad (it's labeled), but it doesn't interrupt reading.

Why publishers use it: Adsterra claims native formats can increase CTRs up to 8× compared to regular display ads (company claim — the direction is right even if the multiplier is optimistic). More importantly, native is the format least likely to annoy anyone. It's the ad format your readers will complain about least, which has real value when you're building an audience.

The honest downside: "Blends in" cuts both ways — blending too well into an editorial blog can feel sneaky to sharp readers, and regulators in the US and EU increasingly require clear ad labeling (Adsterra's units are labeled, but keep it that way). Revenue per impression is also lower than popunder or Social Bar; native is a volume game.

Use it when: you're starting out and reader trust matters more than squeezing every cent. It pairs well with almost anything.

5. Display Banners — the classic

What it is: The traditional rectangular ad units — 728×90 leaderboard, 300×250 medium rectangle, 160×600 skyscraper, 320×50 mobile banner. You generate a code per size, paste it into a gadget or theme slot, and it renders a standard banner.

Why publishers use it: Familiarity. Readers have seen banners for 25 years; they know what they are and mostly ignore them. That "banner blindness" is also the downside — but for a new blog, a 300×250 in the sidebar is predictable, inoffensive revenue. Banners are also the easiest format to run alongside AdSense later, since they're the least likely to violate anyone's policies.

The honest downside: Lowest CPMs of the visible formats — publisher reports commonly put banner CPMs at $0.30–$2 (again, community ranges, GEO-dependent). You need real traffic volume for banners to matter, and too many banners make a blog look like a coupon site from 2009.

Use it when: you want a safe, familiar baseline — especially in the sidebar or below the header. One or two well-placed banners beat five desperate ones.

6. Direct Link (Smartlink) — the invisible format

What it is: A single smart URL. There's no ad unit at all — you attach it to any clickable element: a text link, a button, an image, a "Download" call-to-action. When clicked, it routes the visitor to whichever advertiser campaign currently bids highest for that visitor's device, country, and OS.

Why publishers use it: Zero layout impact and zero reader annoyance until the moment of the click — and even then, the visitor chose to click. It's also the only format that works with no website at all: social media traffic, Telegram channels, YouTube descriptions. Adsterra's own Blogspot guide calls Smartlink "the easiest way of getting profit from ads" for exactly this reason.

The honest downside: Revenue per click is modest (publisher reports often cite $0.50–$3 effective CPM-equivalent), and it only earns when someone clicks — no passive impression income. Slapping Smartlinks on misleading "Download" buttons is also the fastest way to destroy trust and get flagged for deceptive practices.

Use it when: you have a natural call-to-action (a tool recommendation, a download, a resource link) or traffic without a website. Don't disguise it as something it isn't.

7. Interstitial — the full-screen interruption

What it is: A full-screen (or large overlay) ad shown at natural transition points — page load, or when navigating between pages. Maximum visibility, maximum interruption.

Why publishers use it: The visual impact is unmatched, and for short-session traffic (landing pages, download flows) the CPMs are solid — publisher reports put interstitials around $2–$8 (community ranges).

The honest downside: It's the format most likely to make a visitor bounce before reading a word. Google has also penalized intrusive interstitials on mobile in search rankings historically — a full-screen ad on a blog post is a bad trade for organic traffic. Use sparingly or not at all on content pages.

Use it when: download/utility flows where the visitor expects a step before the file. Avoid it on: article pages you want ranking on Google.

How to actually get your ad codes

The process is the same for every format, and Adsterra's own guide confirms it takes under five minutes:

  1. Sign up as an Adsterra publisher (or log in), and add your website under Websites → ADD WEBSITE — URL, category, and your first ad unit.
  2. Wait for the site status to flip from Pending to Approved (usually ~10 minutes).
  3. Click All codes → Get code for the format you want. Each code is unique to one placement — two banners means generating two codes.
  4. Paste the code into your site per the format's instructions (gadget for banners, theme for popunder, link URL for Smartlink).

My recommended starter setup (new blogs)

If I were launching a blog today, here's the exact order I'd add formats:

  1. Start with ONE format: Native or Social Bar. Both earn without punishing your readers. Native if your brand is editorial trust; Social Bar if your traffic is mobile-heavy.
  2. Add a single 300×250 banner in the sidebar once you have steady traffic — predictable baseline income.
  3. Test Popunder last, and only after you have returning readers and real traffic data. Watch bounce rate and return-visitor numbers for two weeks after enabling it. If they dip, the extra CPM isn't worth it.
  4. Never enable everything on day one. Adsterra's own tips warn against oversaturating pages — ad overload raises churn, and churn kills the traffic your earnings depend on.

Honest pros and cons of Adsterra's format lineup

Pros

  • Real variety: seven formats covering every intrusiveness level, from invisible (Smartlink) to aggressive (popunder) — you choose your trade-off instead of taking what you're given.
  • No traffic minimum to start, and formats like Smartlink monetize even social traffic with no website.
  • Formats stack: Social Bar is designed to run alongside your existing placements without replacing them.
  • Per-format codes with clear placement instructions, so you're not guessing where scripts go.

Cons

  • The two highest earners (popunder, interstitial) are also the two most likely to annoy readers — the revenue/UX trade-off is real and permanent.
  • Published CPM ranges are community-reported, not official — Adsterra doesn't publish a rate card, so you're testing blind until your own data comes in.
  • Push-style formats walk an ethical line — notification-mimicking creatives convert partly because they look like system messages.
  • More choice means more ways to misconfigure — one popunder code per page, one code per placement; beginners do break this.

FAQ

Which Adsterra ad format pays the most? Popunder, consistently — publisher communities report the highest CPMs for it, especially on Tier-1 desktop traffic. Social Bar is usually second. But "pays the most per impression" isn't "earns you the most": if popunders drive readers away, your total revenue can drop.

What is the difference between Social Bar and In-Page Push? In-Page Push is a single creative type: notification-style messages inside the page. Social Bar is Adsterra's bigger wrapper format — one line of code that rotates In-Page Push creatives plus chat heads, custom widgets, icons, and interstitial-style units. Think of In-Page Push as one instrument and Social Bar as the band.

Can I use multiple Adsterra formats on the same page? Yes — and Adsterra designs for it (Social Bar explicitly runs alongside other placements). But follow two rules from their own guidance: never more than one popunder code per page, and never generate one code and reuse it in two placements — each code is unique to one spot.

Which format is best for beginners? One low-friction format: Native or Social Bar. Both monetize from day one without the reader-backlash risk of popunders or interstitials. Add a sidebar banner once traffic is steady.

Do Adsterra formats work on Blogspot? Yes — Adsterra publishes an official Blogspot setup guide. Banners and native go in via Layout → Add a Gadget → HTML/JavaScript; popunder and other site-wide scripts go in Theme → Edit HTML. Smartlink needs no website changes at all.
