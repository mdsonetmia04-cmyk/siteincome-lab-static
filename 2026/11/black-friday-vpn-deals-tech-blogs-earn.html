Black Friday VPN Deals 2026: How Tech Blogs Earn From the Year's Biggest Software Sale

November 13, 20268 min readBy Sonet Mia

Black Friday is the cheapest time of year to buy a VPN — and in 2026, the sales started early. NordVPN's campaign has been running since October 21, ExpressVPN's early-access sale is live, and Private Internet Access (PIA), Surfshark, and Proton VPN all show sale prices on their own pages more than two weeks before Black Friday on Friday, November 27. The headline numbers are dramatic: up to 78% off at NordVPN, up to 80% off at ExpressVPN, and $2.03 a month at PIA.

For tech bloggers, VPN deal season is the most profitable software content of the year. VPN and software advertisers pay the highest rates in display advertising — the VPN/software category is the largest single share of popunder advertising volume, and US rates for VPN audiences are among the best any blog niche earns. A tech blog that covers VPN deals well earns twice: once from the deal traffic itself, and again because that traffic is worth more per pageview than almost any other audience.

The Black Friday VPN Deals Live Right Now

All prices below were read from the providers' own pages or from deal trackers that checked them on November 11–12, 2026. Prices exclude tax, and the "per month" figure is the prepaid total divided by the term — not a monthly payment. Always re-check before publishing, because ExpressVPN and NordVPN both run phased deals that get cheaper closer to Black Friday:

  • NordVPN — up to 78% off + 3 extra months. Basic plan $3.49 a month on a 2-year plan (27 months, $94.23 upfront), dropping to $2.99 a month from November 24 to December 1. Campaign ends December 9. Renewal: $139.08 a year.
  • ExpressVPN — up to 80% off + 4 bonus months. Basic plan $2.99 a month on a 2-year plan (28 months, $83.72 upfront), with announced phases dropping to $2.49 a month from November 25 to December 2. Renewal: $99.95 a year.
  • Private Internet Access (PIA) — $2.03 a month. Three years plus four extra months (40 months, about $81.20 upfront). The lowest sticker price of the season — and the most stable renewal, staying near the sale price.
  • Surfshark — from €2.49 a month before VAT on a 2-year plan plus 3 months. Prices vary by visitor location.
  • Proton VPN — Plus from $2.99 on a one-year plan. Offer runs through November 30.
  • Mullvad — no sale. It never runs discounts, which is itself useful information for a comparison post.

Two honest notes that most deal roundups bury: first, the discount percentage in the headline usually covers multiple tiers and should not be read as a verified saving against a tracked historical price. Second, the renewal price is the real cost — NordVPN's Complete tier, for example, renews at roughly four to five times its sale price, while PIA's renewal stays close to the deal price. A blog that explains renewals earns more trust than one that copies headlines.

Why VPN Content Pays Tech Blogs More Than Other Tech Content

VPN is not just another software category. For a monetized blog, it is the best-paying clean niche there is:

  1. Advertisers pay premium rates for VPN audiences. People researching VPNs are privacy-conscious, tech-savvy, and ready to spend — exactly the audience software and security advertisers bid highest to reach. VPN/software is the largest category in popunder advertising and pays top rates in Social Bar and native formats too.
  2. The audience is global and Tier-1 heavy. VPN searches come disproportionately from the US, UK, Canada, and Western Europe — the geographies that pay the most per thousand ad impressions. The same pageview that earns cents from some regions earns several times more from these.
  3. Deal season concentrates the demand. VPN buyers wait all year for Black Friday. The same search volume that trickles through October arrives in a flood between November 13 and December 9, when the last campaigns end. A blog with deal content ready before the flood earns the peak; a blog that publishes during it competes with every major outlet at once.
  4. The content stays useful after the sale. "Which VPN is best for streaming?" and "Is a free VPN safe?" earn traffic all year. A deal post published in November becomes an evergreen comparison post in January with one update.

The VPN Deal-Season Playbook for Tech Blogs

Publish now, update at every phase change. NordVPN's price drops on November 24. ExpressVPN's phases change on November 17 and 25. Each phase change is a new wave of searches — and a reason to update your post's timestamp. One living deal post, updated at every phase, outranks a pile of stale roundups.

Compare the total cost, not the percentage. The comparison readers actually need is a table: provider, sale price, months included, total upfront payment, renewal price, money-back period. That table is the post. Everything else — speed claims, server counts, streaming promises — is secondary during deal season, because the reader's question is "what will I actually pay?"

Warn readers about renewals — it builds the audience. Most VPN deal posts hide the renewal price. Saying it plainly — "NordVPN Basic renews at $139.08 a year; PIA renews near its sale price" — does two things: it protects your readers, and it makes your blog the one they trust enough to return to. Trust is the asset; one deal post is just the entry point.

Answer the questions behind the deal. Deal-season readers also search: "do I need a VPN?", "is NordVPN safe?", "can I use a VPN for Netflix?", "free VPN vs paid VPN." Each question is a supporting post that links to the deal post — and each one earns traffic long after Black Friday ends.

Cover the whole window, not one day. The VPN deal season runs from late October (NordVPN's start) through December 9 (NordVPN's end), with Cyber Monday on November 30 and a second wave of "Cyber Monday VPN deals" searches in between. Three weeks of content, not one day of it.

Link deal content to monetization content. Many VPN deal readers run websites of their own — and VPN audiences overlap heavily with the publisher audience. Monetizing VPN Deal Pages

VPN deal pages earn more per pageview than almost any other tech content, because the audience is exactly who advertisers want. The setup that works:

  • Start with Native Banner and Social Bar. Both earn well on comparison and deal pages without driving readers away. A deal page's job is to keep the reader comparing — the ad formats should not interrupt that.
  • Skip popunder on deal pages. It pays the most per impression, but it costs reader trust, and trust is the entire business model of a deal blog. Save the aggressive formats for pages where the reader has already got what they came for.
  • Place ads at the natural pauses. Below the comparison table, between provider sections, and after the renewal warning — where the reader stops to think, not where they are trying to read a price.

The Honest Math of VPN Deal-Season Earnings

VPN deal content is the highest-earning clean content a tech blog can publish — with two reality checks:

  • The spike is seasonal. VPN deal searches peak between mid-November and early December, then fall back. The blogs that keep earning are the ones whose deal readers find evergreen VPN guides, comparison posts, and tech content worth returning for.
  • Competition is fierce. Every major tech outlet covers VPN deals. A small blog wins not by out-listing them, but by out-helping them: the renewal table, the total-cost comparison, and the honest answer to "which one should I buy?" are what a big outlet's roundup usually skips.
