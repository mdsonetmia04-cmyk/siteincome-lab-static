Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, November 27, and the software deals are already live. Adobe Creative Cloud Pro, which normally costs $69.99 a month, dropped to $34.97 a month for the first year last Black Friday. Microsoft 365 Personal is $99.99 a year direct from Microsoft, but retailers sold it for under $50 last year. Password managers, cloud storage, WordPress plugins and design tools are all cutting prices by 40 to 90 percent this week. And here is the part most shoppers miss: software is the one Black Friday category where the discount is almost never a permanent price cut. It is a first-year price. The deal that looks like it saves you $420 over twelve months quietly renews at full price in year two, and the companies selling it are counting on you forgetting that. For readers, this guide lists every real software deal that is live right now, with the actual prices, the renewal prices, and the catches nobody puts in the headline. For bloggers, it shows why software deals are one of the easiest kinds of Black Friday traffic to win, and exactly how to turn that traffic into ad revenue.

The Software Deals That Are Live Right Now

Every price in this section was checked against live deal roundups on November 22 and 23, 2026. Prices change fast during deal week, so treat this as a snapshot of what is live today, and always confirm the price on the vendor's own checkout page before you subscribe. That rule applies to readers and to bloggers alike: a deals post with a wrong price loses trust faster than any other kind of post, and a reader who catches one wrong price will not believe the other forty.

Adobe Creative Cloud Pro: The Headline Deal of the Season

Adobe Creative Cloud Pro is the single biggest software deal of Black Friday 2026, and it is the one every tech blog should lead with. Adobe lists Creative Cloud Pro at $69.99 a month on an annual plan billed monthly, which works out to $839.88 over a year. Last Black Friday, new subscribers paid $34.97 a month for the first year, or $419.64 over the year, a saving of about $420. That is the largest cash saving on any software deal this season, and it is the reason Adobe's deal tops nearly every Black Friday software roundup published this week. The plan includes Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro and more than twenty other apps, plus Adobe Firefly, so it covers the whole creative workflow in one subscription.

Students get an even better price: $15.99 a month for the first year until November 27, $4 below the usual student rate of $19.99 a month, renewing at $39.99 a month from year two. For everyone else the test is simple: buy below $34.97 a month for the first year, and set a calendar reminder for the renewal date, because the plan renews at $69.99 a month after twelve months.

Microsoft 365 Personal: The Retailer Deal to Watch

Microsoft 365 Personal costs $99.99 a year direct from Microsoft, or $9.99 a month, which works out to $119.88 a year. Annual billing already saves about $20, but the real Black Friday saving comes from retailers rather than from Microsoft itself. Last year, retailer bundles sold Microsoft 365 Personal for around $45.99, less than half the direct price, and the same pattern is expected this year, with Microsoft's own sale window running from November 19 to November 28. The Personal plan covers one person, with the desktop and web versions of Word, Excel and PowerPoint, 1 TB of cloud storage and Copilot features. The Family plan covers up to six people at $12.99 a month, and Premium is $19.99 a month, but the Personal plan is the one that gets the deepest retailer discounts, and it is the one most deal hunters will actually buy.

Password Managers and Security Tools: 50 to 70 Percent Off

Security software is the most discounted category in the whole Black Friday software sale, and the deals are the most honest ones. Norton 360 and Bitdefender Total Security are both running 50 to 70 percent off multi-device plans this year, which is a big cut on products that renew every year anyway. 1Password Individual is $2.99 a month for the first twelve months, and last Black Friday it dropped to $1.99 a month, so anyone buying now should check whether this year's price has matched that yet before they commit. NordPass is up to 58 percent off two-year Individual and Family plans, plus three months free, which works out to $1.39 a month on the Individual two-year plan.

These deals make sense even if you do not need the product today, because a password manager or antivirus is a renewal, not a new purchase: you will pay for it next year regardless, so 50 to 70 percent off is a guaranteed saving. The same logic applies to VPNs: NordVPN is up to 78 percent off two-year plans plus three extra months, from $2.99 a month, and Surfshark is up to 88 percent off, from $2.49 a month. We covered them in our Black Friday VPN deals guide.

Cloud Storage: Lifetime Plans Are the Real Story

Cloud storage is where Black Friday 2026 has its most interesting deals, because lifetime plans turn a subscription into a one-time purchase. pCloud is offering up to 60 percent off lifetime plans, including a 3-in-1 lifetime bundle with 3 TB of storage, pCloud Encryption and pCloud Pass for a one-time payment of $579, running from November 13 to November 28. Icedrive is offering 80 percent off the first year of annual Pro plans, or 1 TB of lifetime storage for $299, through December 5. Sync.com is running three years for the price of one on annual plans, starting November 23 and running to December 5.

Internxt has the most aggressive discount: 87 percent off from November 2 to 22, then 90 percent off from November 23 to 29, continuing through Cyber Week to December 4. A lifetime plan at these prices pays for itself in two to three years compared with a monthly subscription, and it never renews at a higher price, because there is no renewal at all. That is the one category in the whole sale where the renewal-price trap does not apply.

WordPress and SEO Tools: The Blogger's Own Shopping List

For bloggers, the most useful software deals are the ones that pay for the blog itself. WP Rocket is up to 60 percent off a three-year license, or 40 percent off a one-year license, and the deal ends on November 27 or 28, earlier than most, so it is the one deal this week with a genuine deadline. Cloudways is 80 percent off for the first two months on all plans and add-ons with the code BFCM802, capped at $200 off per month. LifterLMS is 60 percent off the first year of any new bundle with the code BLACKFRIDAY26, through November 30. Scalenut is 65 percent off every annual plan, plus triple limits, with a plan listed at $59 a month dropping to $21 a month billed yearly.

These deals turn a Black Friday purchase into a business expense that pays for itself: a caching plugin that speeds up your pages improves both search rankings and ad viewability, and a hosting discount lowers the cost of the site that earns the revenue. Because your readers are other bloggers, a post about WordPress tools converts better than almost any other software category. AI Tools: The Deals That Are Not Deals

AI tools deserve their own section, because the pattern here is different from every other software category. Some AI tools are genuinely on sale. Some are not on sale at all, and the roundups that list them as Black Friday deals are really just listing their ordinary prices. ChatGPT Plus is $20 a month, and there is no discount: it was $20 a month last Black Friday too. Claude Pro is $20 a month, or $17 a month on annual billing, and again there is no Black Friday discount. Notion Plus is $10 a month on annual billing, or $12 a month billed monthly, and it does not run a sale. These are the three most popular AI and productivity tools on the internet, and none of them discounts for Black Friday, because none of them needs to.

Jasper starts at $59 a month on a yearly plan, Descript at $16 a month, and Canva Pro at $144 a year. The Renewal-Price Trap: The One Number That Matters Most

Every software deal this week has two prices, and only one of them is in the headline. The first price is the one you pay today. The second price is the one you pay next year, and it is almost always the full list price. Adobe Creative Cloud Pro is the clearest example: $34.97 a month for the first year, then $69.99 a month. Microsoft 365 Personal from a retailer is roughly $46 for the first year, then $99.99 a year. 1Password is $1.99 or $2.99 a month for the first twelve months, then back to its standard rate. This is not a scam, and it is not hidden: the renewal price is printed on the checkout page, in the terms, and usually in a footnote on the sale page itself. But it is the number most shoppers never read, and it is the number that decides whether a deal was actually a good one.

For readers, the rule is simple: before you buy, find the renewal price and ask whether you would still buy the product at that price. If the answer is yes, the Black Friday price is a genuine saving. If not, you are renting a product for a year at a discount and then paying full price for something you do not really want. Judge every deal on its two-year cost, not its one-year saving. Adobe at $34.97 a month for year one and $69.99 for year two costs $1,259.52 over two years, against $1,679.76 at full price: still a real saving of about $420, and still the best software deal of the season, just not the 50 percent saving the headline promises, because the headline only counts the first year.

Why Software Deals Are the Easiest Black Friday Traffic to Win

For bloggers, software deals are the easiest Black Friday traffic to win, and the reasons are structural rather than lucky. The first reason is intent. A person searching for "Adobe Creative Cloud Black Friday deal" is not browsing: they have their card in their hand and they are looking for one number, the price. That is the highest-intent search in the whole Black Friday season, higher than "best laptop deals" and far higher than "Black Friday sales". A post that answers that search with the real price, the renewal price and the end date will get the click, and the reader will stay on the page because the page has the one thing they came for.

The second reason is competition. The biggest deal sites cover laptops, televisions and smartphones with teams updating those pages every hour, but almost nobody covers software deals with the same intensity. A small tech blog can outrank a giant deal site on a software keyword simply by being more specific: the giant site lists fifty deals on one page, while your post covers one category in depth, with real prices, real renewal prices and real end dates. Google rewards that specificity, and so do readers.

The third reason is timing. Software deals go live earlier than hardware deals: Internxt started on November 2, pCloud on November 13, Microsoft's window on November 19. A software deals post published on November 23 has had four days to get indexed before the big hardware deals launch on November 27, so it is already ranking when the search volume spikes on Black Friday morning. Publish early, update the prices daily, and Google sees a living page rather than a stale one.

How to Structure a Software Deals Post That Ranks

The structure of a deals post is as important as the deals themselves, because search engines and readers both judge a page in the first ten seconds. Start with the price in the opening paragraph. The reader searched for a price, so give them the price before anything else: Adobe is $34.97 a month for the first year, Microsoft 365 is under $50 at retailers, Surfshark is $2.49 a month. If the opening paragraph does not contain a number, the reader will hit the back button and click the next result, and that bounce tells Google your page did not answer the query.

After the opening, organize the deals by category, not by brand, and give each category its own heading. Readers think in categories: they want a photo editor, a password manager, cloud storage or a WordPress plugin, and they want to compare the options inside that category. Put the price, the discount, the renewal price and the end date in the first two sentences of each section, and put the biggest saving first, because the first deal on the page decides whether the reader keeps scrolling.

End every deal section with the catch: the renewal price, the plan limitation, the end date, or the fact that the deal is only for new subscribers. Readers trust a post that tells them the catch, because it proves the writer actually checked the offer. That trust turns a one-time visitor into a returning reader, and it is what makes a deals post rank year after year: the same post, updated with new prices, can rank for the same keyword every November.

The Monetization Playbook: Turning Deal Traffic Into Ad Revenue

Deal traffic is valuable because it is high-intent, but it is fragile, because it disappears the week after Black Friday. The playbook has two parts, and they both happen in the same week. The first is the ad inventory: a banner above the fold, a 300x250 unit after the second paragraph next to the first deal, and another unit around the forty percent mark. Readers who arrive from a deal search are in a hurry, so the ads need to be visible in the first screen, not buried at the bottom where nobody scrolls.

The second part is internal linking. A reader who came for the Adobe deal might also need a password manager, or cloud storage, or a VPN. Every deal section should link to the matching guide on your own site, because every internal link is another page view, another ad impression, and another chance to keep the reader inside your site instead of sending them back to Google. This post links to the VPN guide, the hosting guide and the AI tools guide for exactly that reason, and each of those posts links back to the others, so a reader who arrives on any one of them can travel through the whole Black Friday series without ever leaving the site.

A Day-by-Day Plan for Deal Week

Deal week rewards bloggers who publish in a sequence. Monday, November 23, is the day for the big category guides: software, VPNs, hosting and AI tools, because the early deals are already live. Tuesday and Wednesday are for updating those posts and publishing the smaller guides. Thursday, November 26, is Thanksgiving in the United States: the quietest day for publishing, but the busiest for reading, as millions of Americans plan what to buy the next morning. A post updated on Thursday evening is the one they will find on Friday morning.

Common Mistakes That Kill Software Deals Posts

The most common mistake is copying prices from another roundup instead of checking the vendor's own page. Roundups get prices wrong, they go stale within hours, and they copy each other's errors, so a price that is wrong in one roundup is wrong in twenty of them by the end of the day. Check the vendor's page, take a screenshot, and note the date you checked. The second mistake is hiding the renewal price, or leaving it out entirely. Readers find it anyway, usually in the comments, and a post that hid the renewal price loses the reader's trust permanently. The third mistake is listing tools that are not actually on sale. ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro and Notion Plus do not discount for Black Friday, and a post that lists them as deals is a post that was written without checking, and readers can tell.

The fourth mistake is publishing too late: a post published on Black Friday morning is competing with pages that have ranked for a week. The fifth is the opposite: publishing in October with last year's prices and never updating. A stale price is worse than no price, because it sends a reader to a checkout where the price is different. Publish early, update daily, and treat the post as a living page for the whole week.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 is the best week of the year to buy software, and the best week of the year to write about it. The deals are real: Adobe at $34.97 a month for the first year, Microsoft 365 for under $50 at retailers, security tools at 50 to 70 percent off, lifetime cloud storage that never renews, and WordPress tools at up to 65 percent off. But every deal has a second price, the renewal price, and the shoppers who save the most this week are the ones who read it before they buy. For bloggers, the play is just as clear: publish early, lead with the price, organize by category, tell the reader the catch, and keep the page alive for next year. The traffic peaks on Friday, November 27, and it will be gone by Tuesday, but the rankings and the readers you earn this week will still be there next November.
