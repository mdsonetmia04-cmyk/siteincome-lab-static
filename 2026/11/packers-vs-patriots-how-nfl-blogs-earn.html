Packers vs Patriots: How NFL Blogs Earn with Adsterra in 2026

November 9, 20263 min readBy Sonet Mia

Packers vs Patriots is one of the most searched games in America today — more than 1 million searches in the last 24 hours. When a game draws that much attention, thousands of fans are searching for previews, scores, and analysis at the same time.

Sports blogs that cover these games earn money from that attention. This post explains exactly how: the Adsterra setup that NFL bloggers use, the ad formats that work best for football traffic, and the realistic numbers behind it.

Why Football Blogs Earn Well

Football traffic is valuable to advertisers for a simple reason: it is large, regular, and passionate. The NFL season runs for months, with big games every week. A blog that covers one team gets a fresh traffic wave every single week — not just once.

That repeat traffic is what makes a sports blog a real income source instead of a one-time spike.

Step 2: Use Ad Formats That Fit Football Readers

Not every ad format works for sports content. The two that fit best:

  • Native Banner — looks like part of the article, so fans notice it without feeling interrupted. Place one inside the post and one on the homepage.
  • Social Bar — a small notification-style ad that stays visible while fans scroll through a long recap. It earns well on mobile, where most football fans read.

Avoid covering your content with popups. Fans who cannot read the article will leave, and a visitor who leaves in five seconds earns you nothing.

Step 3: Understand the Earnings Math

Display ads pay per 1,000 views (CPM). Here is an honest example, not a promise:

  • A game-day post brings 50,000 views.
  • At a $2 CPM, that post earns around $100.
  • Cover 10 big games in a season, and the same math adds up to around $1,000 from those posts alone — before counting your regular season content.

Your real CPM depends on your visitors' countries (USA and Europe pay the most), your ad placement, and the season. Traffic from America and Europe is exactly what advertisers pay a premium for, which is why sports blogs targeting US readers do well.

Step 4: Publish Before the Traffic Arrives

Timing is everything in sports blogging:

  • Previews: publish 1–2 days before the game.
  • Recaps: publish within an hour after the final whistle.
  • Evergreen guides: team histories, stadium guides, and "best players" lists earn traffic all year.

A post published after the game is over misses the biggest wave. Plan your content around the fixture list, not around your free time.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting for "enough traffic" before monetizing. Set up ads from day one — even small traffic earns something, and you learn what works.
  • Writing only scores. Scores are everywhere. Analysis, opinion, and useful guides are what make readers come back to your blog instead of ESPN.
  • Forgetting mobile. Most football searches happen on phones. Test every post on your own phone before you publish.
