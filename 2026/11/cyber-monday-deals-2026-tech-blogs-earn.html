Cyber Monday Deals 2026: How Tech Blogs Turn the Year's Biggest Software Sale Into Ad Revenue

November 21, 202616 min readBy Sonet Mia

Cyber Monday 2026 falls on Monday, November 30, and it is the single biggest online shopping day of the year in the United States. Millions of people who spent the Thanksgiving weekend window-shopping will sit down at their desks and start buying, and a huge share of them will start by typing a search like "Cyber Monday VPN deals" or "best Cyber Monday software deals" into Google.

That search box is where tech blogs make their money. Every deal-hunting search is a reader with a wallet open, and the blogs that answer those searches with honest, up-to-date deal roundups earn from display ads, affiliate commissions, and ad networks all at once. This post explains what Cyber Monday is, which deals readers are actually searching for in 2026, and how a small tech blog can turn one week of deal season into real revenue.

What Cyber Monday Actually Is (and Why It Matters to Bloggers)

Cyber Monday is the Monday after Thanksgiving in the United States, a day invented to push holiday shopping online. The term was coined by Ellen Davis of the National Retail Federation, in a press release issued on November 28, 2005. The idea came from a simple observation: every year, on the Monday after Thanksgiving, online sales jumped, because people were back at work on fast office internet connections, buying the things they had looked at over the weekend.

The first Cyber Monday brought in about $486 million in online sales, up 26 percent from the year before. Growth was fast after that. Online spending passed $1 billion in a single day in 2010, hit $7.9 billion in 2018 according to Adobe Analytics, reached $9.4 billion in 2019, and then $10.7 billion in 2020, which was the biggest online shopping day in American history at the time. What started as one Monday has now stretched into a full week of deals, often called Cyber Week, and many brands keep their discounts running into early December.

For a blogger, the important part is not the history. It is the search behavior. Deal season creates the most predictable traffic spike of the year for tech content. People search for the same things every November: VPN deals, antivirus deals, password manager deals, laptop deals, and software discounts. The searches start weeks before the day itself, peak on the Monday, and stay strong for days afterward. A blog post that is published early and updated through the week can collect traffic for a month, not a day.

There is another reason Cyber Monday matters more than most other trending topics. The readers are buyers, not browsers. A person searching for a VPN deal in late November is usually ready to pay within minutes. That makes the traffic far more valuable than the same number of visitors on an ordinary how-to article. Advertisers know this, and they pay more to reach these readers, which is why ad rates climb through the fourth quarter of the year.

The 2026 Calendar: When the Money Happens

Black Friday 2026 is Friday, November 27. Cyber Monday 2026 is Monday, November 30. But the deals do not wait for those dates. Most VPN and software brands launched their holiday discounts in mid-October, and many run them until the first week of December.

NordVPN, for example, is running its Black Friday promotion from October 15 to December 10, with a separate Cyber Monday window from December 2 to December 10. Surfshark's promotion runs from October 30 to December 1. Proton VPN's runs from October 26 to December 1. ExpressVPN's Cyber Monday window runs into early December as well. The pattern is the same across the industry: the sale starts early, the deepest discounts land on the big days, and the offers linger for a few days after, catching the shoppers who missed the rush.

That calendar is good news for a small blog. You do not need to publish on Cyber Monday itself. In fact, publishing on the day is too late. The smart move is to publish your deal roundup two to three weeks early, when the first discounts go live and competition for the keywords is still low, and then update the prices and offers every day through the sale. Google rewards pages that are already indexed and already earning clicks when the search wave arrives.

One more timing detail matters. Deal pages that go stale hurt you. A reader who lands on a post with last year's prices will leave in seconds, and Google notices. If you publish a Cyber Monday post, commit to updating it at least once a day during Cyber Week, and put the update date near the top so readers can see the prices are fresh.

VPN deals dominate Cyber Monday searches for tech blogs, and the 2026 offers are the deepest of the year. Surfshark is close behind, with up to 87 to 88 percent off two-year plans plus three months free, from $2.19 a month on its cheapest tier. Its promotion runs from October 30 to December 1. Two things are worth saying about these prices. First, the exact monthly figure depends on the plan and the length of the subscription, and different deal roundups quote slightly different starting prices, so always check the provider's own checkout page before you publish a number. Second, the prices above are the promotional prices for the first term. Most VPNs renew at the regular rate after the first two years, and an honest post says so. Readers trust a blog that tells them about the renewal price. They do not trust a blog that hides it.

VPNs are not the only software deals readers want. Password managers and security bundles are the second-biggest category. NordPass is offering 53 percent off its two-year plan at $1.39 a month, plus three months free, from October 21 to December 9. Coveron, the identity-protection service, is offering 64 percent off its two-year Full Protection plan at $9.99 a month through November 30. Antivirus bundles, cloud storage, and password managers all run similar discounts in the same week, which means one well-organized deals post can serve several different searches at once.

There is also a practical point for bloggers. You do not need to join every VPN affiliate program to write about these deals. A post can earn from display ads alone, because the traffic is valuable and the ad rates are high in late November. Affiliate links are a bonus on top. If you do use affiliate links, say so clearly. A short disclosure near the top of the post is enough, and it keeps you honest with both your readers and Google.

How Tech Blogs Actually Earn From Cyber Monday

There are three income streams on a deal-season post, and they stack. The first is display advertising. When a reader opens your Cyber Monday roundup, the ad units on the page start earning. Networks like Adsterra pay per thousand impressions, and the rates are higher in the fourth quarter because advertisers are competing for holiday shoppers. A post that gets 10,000 views in Cyber Week can earn several times what the same views would earn in a quiet month.

The second stream is affiliate commissions. When a reader clicks your link to a VPN deal and buys, you earn a percentage of the sale. VPN affiliate programs are among the most generous in tech, and the products practically sell themselves during Cyber Monday. But affiliate income depends on trust. A reader who believes your prices and your opinions will click. A reader who suspects you copied a table from another site will not.

The third stream is the one most new bloggers miss: email. A Cyber Monday post is a chance to grow a mailing list, because deal hunters are happy to trade an email address for a price alert. A simple signup box that says "I will email you if the price drops further" can add hundreds of subscribers in a week. Those subscribers are worth more than the week's ad revenue, because you can email them again for the next sale.

7 Ways to Turn Cyber Monday Searches Into Revenue

Knowing the deals is not enough. The blogs that earn the most from Cyber Monday do a few specific things differently. Here are seven of them, in the order that matters most.

1. Publish Early, Then Update Every Day

The biggest mistake is waiting until Cyber Monday to publish. By then, the big sites have owned the keywords for weeks. Publish your roundup as soon as the first discounts go live, which in 2026 means mid-October, and update the prices daily. Each update is a signal to Google that the page is fresh, and each update gives you a reason to share the post again. A post that says "Updated November 21, 2026" at the top converts better than a post with no date at all, because deal hunters check the date before they trust the prices.

2. Lead With a Comparison Table

Readers who search for deals want a fast answer. Put a simple table near the top of the post: provider, discount, monthly price, free months, and the date the offer ends. A reader should be able to compare five VPNs in ten seconds without scrolling through paragraphs. The table also helps you rank, because Google often shows comparison tables in its results. After the table, go deeper with a short paragraph on each deal, so readers who want detail can keep reading.

3. Answer the Question Behind the Search

Most deal searches are really questions. "Best Cyber Monday VPN deals" means "which VPN should I buy at the lowest price?" "NordVPN vs Surfshark Black Friday" means "which one is better for me?" Write the post as an answer, not a list. Say who each deal suits: the cheapest option for a student, the best value for a family, the fastest for streaming. When your post answers the real question, readers stay longer, and longer visits mean more ad impressions and more clicks.

4. Use Real Prices, Checked the Same Day

Nothing kills a deals post faster than a wrong price. If your post says $2.99 a month and the checkout page says $4.99, the reader leaves and does not come back. Check every price on the day you publish, and check again each morning during Cyber Week. If a deal ends, remove it or mark it as expired. A post with five correct deals earns more than a post with fifteen deals, half of which are wrong.

5. Link Your Deal Posts Together

One deal post should never stand alone. Link it to your other seasonal posts, and link those posts back to it. A reader who lands on your Cyber Monday roundup might also want your Black Friday VPN deals post, and a reader on that post will want the Cyber Monday update. Internal links keep readers on your site longer, spread traffic across more pages, and help Google understand that your site covers deal season properly. 6. Capture the Email Before the Sale Ends

Most Cyber Monday readers will never come back. They came for a deal, they bought it, and they are gone. The only way to keep them is email. Add a simple box to the post offering a price-drop alert or a "best deals of the week" email. You do not need a fancy setup. A short line under the comparison table works: "Want me to email you if any of these prices drop further? Leave your email here." Even a small list built during Cyber Week becomes the audience for your next sale, and the next one after that.

7. Write for Readers, Not for Robots

It is tempting to stuff a deals post with keywords. Do not. Google has become very good at recognizing pages written for search engines instead of people, and readers are even better. Use the keywords naturally: "Cyber Monday VPN deals" in the title, in one heading, and a few times in the text where it fits. Write short paragraphs, use plain language, and say what you actually think about each deal. A natural tone is not just nicer to read. It is now a ranking factor, because Google measures whether readers stay on the page or bounce straight back to the results.

The Mistakes That Kill Deal-Season Earnings

A few mistakes show up on small blogs every November, and they cost real money. The first is copying another site's deal table without checking it. Prices change daily during Cyber Week, and a copied table is usually out of date within hours. The second is hiding the affiliate disclosure. Readers can tell when every link earns you money, and hiding it destroys trust. A clear disclosure near the top costs you nothing and protects you with readers and with regulators.

The third mistake is overloading the page with ads. A deals post with a popup, a sticky banner, and three auto-playing video ads will earn less than a clean page, because readers leave. Use two or three well-placed units: one at the top, one beside the comparison table, and one below the content. The fourth mistake is publishing and forgetting. A Cyber Monday post that is never updated will rank for a day and then fade. The blogs that win are the ones that treat the post as a living page for the whole month.

The fifth mistake is chasing every deal. A post that lists forty VPNs, twenty antivirus tools, and a dozen laptop deals is not helpful. It is noise. Pick the ten or twelve deals your readers actually want, write a real paragraph about each one, and leave the rest out. Fewer, better deals convert more readers than a wall of links.

A Simple Cyber Monday Content Plan for a Small Blog

If you run a small blog, you do not need a big team to win deal season. You need a plan and a calendar. Three weeks before Cyber Monday, publish your main deals roundup with the early offers, and publish one comparison post, such as two leading VPNs head to head. Two weeks before, update the roundup with the new discounts and publish a short post about the best software deals beyond VPNs, like password managers and antivirus bundles.

One week before, update everything again, and add a post that answers a specific question readers are asking, such as whether the Black Friday or the Cyber Monday price is better. On the day itself, update the roundup in the morning, share it everywhere you have an audience, and check the prices once more in the evening. The day after, publish a short follow-up: which deals are still live, and which ones ended. That follow-up catches the late shoppers and keeps the traffic going for another week.

Keep the plan small enough to finish. A small blog that publishes three good, updated deal posts will earn more than a small blog that publishes ten rushed ones. Quality is the advantage small blogs have. The big sites publish fast, but they rarely check every price by hand. You can.
