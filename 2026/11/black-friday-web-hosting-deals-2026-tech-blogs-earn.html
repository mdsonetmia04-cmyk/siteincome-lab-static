Every year, the same week decides who makes real money from a tech blog and who just watches the traffic go by. Black Friday falls on November 27, 2026, and Cyber Monday follows on November 30, and in the days around them, millions of people search for one thing above almost everything else in the tech world: cheaper web hosting. They are starting blogs, moving websites, and launching stores. The hosts know it, so they cut prices harder than at any other point in the year. Hostinger is advertising up to 85% off its Black Friday sale, running from October 19 to December 14, and Hosting.com is advertising up to 89% off with shared hosting from $1.49 a month. For a tech blogger, that combination of motivated buyers and deep discounts is the most profitable content window of the year, and this guide shows you exactly how to turn it into revenue.

Why Black Friday Hosting Week Is a Blogger's Biggest Payday

Web hosting is unusual among Black Friday products. A television is a one-time purchase, but hosting is a subscription that renews every year — which is exactly why hosts can afford to discount it so aggressively. When a host sells a plan at 85% off, it is not losing money. It is buying a customer who will keep paying for years. That simple fact shapes everything about Black Friday in the hosting world, and it is why the deals are so deep and the affiliate commissions are so generous.

Advertisement

For bloggers, the opportunity comes from both sides at once. On the reader side, search volume for hosting deals explodes in the final two weeks of November. People who would never normally search for "best web hosting" suddenly type "Black Friday hosting deals" into Google, because they know the discounts only exist for a few days. The intent behind those searches is as close to a purchase as search traffic ever gets: the reader already wants hosting, already has a budget in mind, and is comparing prices right now.

On the revenue side, the timing could not be better. The fourth quarter is when advertisers spend the most money of the entire year, and display ad rates follow that spending. A tech blog that earns a steady rate from its ads in October will typically earn noticeably more per thousand visitors in late November, because advertisers are bidding against each other for the same shopping audience. So the same article that earns affiliate commissions from hosting signups also earns more from every ad impression it generates. Very few niches get both effects at once, and hosting is one of them.

The Best Black Friday Web Hosting Deals of 2026

Before you write a single word of your deal roundup, you need to know which offers are real and which numbers you can safely quote. Every price and discount below comes from the vendors' own Black Friday pages or from deal roundups that checked those pages directly in November 2026. Prices vary by country and by plan length, and the final price at checkout is the one that counts — but these are the headline figures your readers will be comparing.

Hostinger: Up to 85% Off, October 19 to December 14

Hostinger is the name most deal roundups put first, and for good reason. Its own Black Friday page advertises up to 85% off, with the sale running from October 19 to December 14, 2026. That long window matters: you can publish early, rank early, and still send readers to a live discount on Black Friday itself. Hostinger is the classic recommendation for a reader starting their first blog, because the entry price is low, the setup is genuinely beginner-friendly, and the plans include the basics a new site needs — a free domain on longer plans, free SSL, and one-click WordPress installation.

The deal gets more interesting on the longer plans. Hostinger's four-year plans come with extra AI credits for its AI website builder, which lets a reader build a site by describing it in plain language instead of wrestling with a theme. That is a real selling point for the audience that buys hosting on Black Friday: people who want a website but do not want to learn web design to get one. When you write about Hostinger, lead with the price, but close with the experience — the reader is not buying server space, they are buying the feeling that their site will be live by tonight.

One honest note belongs in every Hostinger recommendation: the headline discount applies to the first term, and renewal pricing is higher. That is true of almost every host on this list, and your readers will find out eventually. Telling them upfront costs you nothing and buys you trust.

Hosting.com: Up to 89% Off, Shared Hosting From $1.49 a Month

Hosting.com is running the deepest headline discount of the season at up to 89% off, with shared hosting listed from $1.49 a month. For a reader comparing plans on price alone, that is the number that stops the scroll. It is also the deal most likely to be quoted in "cheapest hosting" searches, which are some of the highest-volume queries of the whole Black Friday period. If your blog covers budget hosting at all, Hosting.com belongs in your roundup.

The angle for Hosting.com is different from Hostinger's. Hostinger sells the beginner experience; Hosting.com sells the price. Your article should say that plainly. A reader who wants the cheapest possible way to get a WordPress site online will be happy here. A reader who wants managed hosting or premium support should look at the higher-tier plans or a different host entirely. Honest framing like that does not reduce conversions — it increases them, because the readers who do buy are the ones the product actually fits.

There is a practical point worth making, too. A $1.49-a-month starting price only exists on a multi-year plan. If a reader buys one year, the monthly figure is higher. Spell that out. The fastest way to lose a reader's trust is to quote a price they cannot reproduce at checkout.

The Companion Deals: WordPress Tools, Email, and VPNs

WordPress Tools: Rank Math and the SEO Stack

Rank Math, one of the most popular WordPress SEO plugins, is running a Black Friday banner advertising up to 40% off. That belongs in your hosting content for a simple reason: the reader who just bought hosting is about to install WordPress, and the first plugin most guides recommend is an SEO plugin. Link it to your broader SEO content, and remind readers that the Google November 2026 core update — which we covered in our guide to what bloggers must do before Black Friday — makes on-page SEO more important than ever. A site that starts with proper SEO from day one has a head start over every site that adds it later.

The same logic applies to the other tools in the WordPress ecosystem. A "what to install after you buy hosting" checklist is one of the highest-converting article formats in the entire niche. The reader is in buying mode, they trust you because your hosting advice was honest, and every item on the checklist solves a problem they are about to have. Write that checklist as a separate article, link it from your hosting roundup, and you have two ranking pages instead of one.

VPNs: The Cross-Sell Every Hosting Roundup Misses

Here is the deal most hosting roundups leave on the table: the same reader who buys hosting on Black Friday is very likely to buy a VPN in the same week. They are setting up a website, which means they are thinking about security and privacy. The VPN discounts this year are among the deepest in any category. Surfshark is advertising up to 90% off two-year plans plus four months free, from $1.59 a month. NordVPN is offering up to 70% off two-year plans plus four months free, from $2.88 a month. Proton VPN is at 75% off a two-year subscription at $2.49 a month, valid until December 1, 2026, and ExpressVPN is advertising up to 80% off plus four months free.

How to Compare Hosting Deals Without Getting Burned

The biggest mistake a tech blogger can make during Black Friday is also the easiest: copying the headline discount into an article without explaining what it actually means. Your readers are smart, and the ones who feel misled do not come back. The bloggers who earn the most from hosting season are the ones whose readers trust them, and trust is built by explaining the fine print before the reader discovers it at checkout.

Renewal Pricing Is the Real Price

Almost every hosting discount is an introductory price. You pay the discounted rate for the first term — one year, two years, or four years — and then the plan renews at the regular rate. A plan advertised at 85% off might renew at three or four times the price you paid in year one. That does not make it a bad deal. It makes it a deal with a time limit, and your article should say so clearly. Tell your readers to check the renewal price on the pricing page before they buy, and to set a calendar reminder for the renewal date. That one paragraph of honest advice is worth more for your long-term earnings than any sales trick.

The term length matters just as much. The deepest discounts always require the longest commitment. A four-year plan at 85% off looks unbeatable until you realize you are paying four years upfront. For a reader who is sure about their project, that is a great deal. For a reader who is testing an idea, a one-year plan at a smaller discount is the smarter buy. Your job is to help the reader match the plan to their situation, not to push the plan with the biggest number on it.

What Actually Matters in a Hosting Plan

Price is the first thing readers compare, but it should not be the only thing. Four features matter more than the discount percentage. First, the free SSL certificate — every modern site needs one, and a host that charges extra for it is charging you for something that should be free. Second, backups: does the plan include automatic daily backups, or will the reader pay extra? Third, the free domain: many hosts include a free domain for the first year on longer plans.

The Deals That Expire First

Not all Black Friday deals end on the same day, and your content should reflect that. Proton VPN's 75% off ends December 1, and Hostinger's sale runs until December 14. The practical advice for your readers is simple: if a deal expires on December 1, decide before December 1. And the practical advice for your blog is equally simple: update your articles when a deal expires. A roundup that still advertises a dead discount in mid-December loses readers and rankings. Cyber Monday falls on November 30, 2026, and many hosts extend or refresh their offers for it — we covered the second wave in our Cyber Monday deals guide.

The Blogger's Playbook: Turning Hosting Deals Into Revenue

Knowing the deals is only half the job. The other half is publishing the right articles at the right time, in the right order, so that Google finds them before the search peak arrives. Hosting content has a cruel timing problem: the articles that earn the most are the ones published weeks before Black Friday, but the deals they describe are only confirmed days before it. The bloggers who solve that problem are the ones who make the money.

Publish Early, Update Often

The winning pattern is simple. Publish your hosting roundup two to three weeks before Black Friday with the deals you can already confirm — Hostinger's sale, for example, opened on October 19, which means it was confirmable a full five weeks early. Then update the article as new deals are confirmed, and update it again on Black Friday morning with any last-minute offers. Google rewards pages that are updated regularly, and readers trust pages with a recent update date. An article first published in early November and updated three times before Black Friday will outrank an article published on Black Friday itself, almost every time.

The Four Article Formats That Convert

Not every hosting article should be a roundup. Four formats consistently earn more than the rest, and a smart blog publishes all four across the season. The first is the roundup itself — "Best Black Friday Web Hosting Deals 2026" — which captures the broadest searches. The second is the comparison: "Hostinger vs Hosting.com: Which Black Friday Deal Is Actually Better?" Comparisons capture readers who have already narrowed their choice to two options, and those readers convert at a much higher rate, because they are one decision away from buying.

The third format is the tutorial: "How to Start a Blog With Hostinger in 2026" or "How to Move Your WordPress Site to Cheaper Hosting." The fourth format is the "best for" article: "Best Hosting for a Food Blog," "Best Hosting for a Small Business," "Best Cheap Hosting for Students." These target specific audiences, face less competition in search results, and convert well because the reader feels the article was written for them.

Affiliate Commissions vs Display Ads: Where the Real Money Is

Every hosting article earns money in two ways, and understanding the difference changes how you write. Display ads pay you per thousand visitors — the more traffic, the more ad revenue, regardless of whether anyone buys anything. Affiliate commissions pay you per sale — fewer events, but each one is worth far more than a thousand ad impressions. During Black Friday, both go up at the same time: traffic rises because everyone is searching for deals, and the share of visitors who buy rises because the discounts are real.

For a small blog, the affiliate side matters more. A single hosting sale through your link can be worth more than a whole month of display ads at low traffic levels. That is why hosting content is worth the effort even before your traffic is large. You do not need a hundred thousand visitors to earn from Black Friday hosting content. You need a few hundred visitors who are ready to buy, and an article that helps them choose honestly. That is an achievable goal for a blog of almost any size.

The smartest approach is to treat the two income streams as partners, not rivals. Write the article for the reader first — honest comparisons, verified prices, clear advice — and let both revenue streams benefit from the trust that creates. An article written only for commissions reads like a sales pitch, and readers can tell. An article written for the reader earns commissions anyway, because the reader trusts the recommendation.

Mistakes That Cost Bloggers Money Every Black Friday

Most of the money lost during Black Friday is not lost to competition. It is lost to avoidable mistakes, and the same ones happen every year. The first is publishing too late. An article published on Black Friday morning has almost no chance of ranking for Black Friday searches, because Google needs time to find it, index it, and decide it deserves to rank. The articles that win the peak were published weeks earlier and updated through it. If you are reading this and have not published your hosting roundup yet, publish it today — late is bad, but never is worse.

The second mistake is copying prices without checking them. Hosting prices change during the sale, discounts end early, and codes expire. An article that quotes a dead price loses the reader at the exact moment they were ready to buy. Check every price in your article on the day you publish, and check again on Black Friday morning. It takes ten minutes and it is the difference between a commission and a bounced visitor.

The third mistake is writing for the search engine instead of the reader. Keyword-stuffed articles with the same phrase repeated in every heading used to rank. They do not anymore — and even when they do, they do not convert, because a reader who feels sold to does not buy. Write the article you would want to read if you were buying hosting today: clear prices, honest trade-offs, and a recommendation you would give a friend.

What If You Missed Black Friday?

It happens every year: a blogger plans to publish, life gets in the way, and suddenly it is December. The good news is that the hosting deals do not all end on Black Friday. Hostinger's sale runs until December 14, MailerLite's discount runs until December 18, and Carrd's offer runs until December 6. Cyber Monday on November 30 is a second peak in its own right, and many hosts extend their best prices through the first week of December. A "last chance" article published on December 1 can still earn well, because it captures the readers who meant to buy on Friday and ran out of time. The season is a window, not a single day.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 is the best week of the year to buy web hosting, and the best week of the year to write about it. Hostinger is offering up to 85% off until December 14, Hosting.com is advertising up to 89% off from $1.49 a month, and the companion VPN deals mean the reader who buys hosting this week can also secure their connection at the lowest prices of the year. For a tech blogger, that is a rare combination: a product readers genuinely want, discounts deep enough to be worth writing about, and two income streams — affiliate commissions and display ads — rising at the same time. Publish your roundup now, compare the deals honestly, explain the renewal prices your readers need to know, and follow up with the tutorials that turn a one-time buyer into a long-term reader. The sale ends in December. The audience you build from it can last for years.
