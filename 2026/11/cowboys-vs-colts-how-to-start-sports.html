Cowboys vs Colts: How to Start a Sports Blog and Monetize It in 2026

November 9, 2026

Cowboys vs Colts is trending with more than 1 million searches in America today. Games like this are a reminder of how much attention sports content gets — and how much of that attention turns into money for the blogs that cover it.

If you have ever thought about starting a sports blog, this is the practical guide: pick a niche, set it up free, plan your content around the season, and monetize it from the start.

Step 1: Pick One Team or One Sport

The biggest beginner mistake is covering "all sports." Big sites already do that, and you cannot compete with them. Pick something narrow:

  • One NFL team (for example, the Cowboys or the Colts)
  • One league (NFL, NBA, or a football league)
  • One angle (game analysis, fantasy football, or team history)

A narrow blog ranks faster, because Google learns what your blog is about. A blog about everything ranks for nothing.

Step 2: Set Up a Free Blog

You do not need money to start. Blogger (Blogspot) is free, owned by Google, and takes ten minutes to set up:

  1. Create the blog and choose a clear name (your team or sport in the name helps).
  2. Pick a clean, fast, mobile-friendly theme.
  3. Write your About page and a short description of what the blog covers.
  4. Publish your first 5–10 posts before you worry about traffic.

A professional-looking blog also gets approved faster by ad networks.

Step 3: Plan Content Around the Fixture List

Sports blogging is the easiest niche to plan, because the schedule is public months in advance. Your content calendar writes itself:

  • Before each game: a preview post (key players, recent form, what to watch).
  • After each game: a recap post within an hour of the final whistle.
  • Between games: player profiles, team history, and "best of" lists that earn traffic all year.
  • Off-season: draft news, transfers, and season predictions keep the blog alive when there are no games.

Publish previews 1–2 days early. A preview posted on game day is already too late — the big sites have taken the traffic.

Step 4: Monetize from Day One

Do not wait for "enough traffic." Set up monetization when you launch:

  1. Get approved on Adsterra — it accepts small and new blogs. Follow our guide: How to Get Approved on Adsterra.
  2. Place a Native Banner inside your posts and a Social Bar that stays visible on mobile, where most fans read.
  3. Compare networks before you commit — Adsterra vs AdSense explains which suits a new blog.

Visitors from the USA and Europe earn the highest ad rates, so write in English and target American and European readers from the start.

Step 5: Bring Traffic from Everywhere

Google alone is slow for a new blog. Send traffic from every free channel:

  • Pinterest — game graphics and infographics travel well there.
  • Facebook groups — team fan groups are full of your exact readers.
  • Quora — answer football questions and link back to your detailed posts.
  • X (Twitter) — live reactions during games bring instant visitors.

One viral post in a fan group can bring more visitors than a month of Google traffic for a new blog.

A Realistic Timeline

Be honest with yourself: a sports blog does not earn in week one. A realistic path looks like this:

  • Month 1: set up, publish 10–15 posts, get Adsterra approved.
  • Month 2–3: publish every game preview and recap, start seeing Google traffic.
  • Month 4–6: steady weekly traffic, first real ad earnings, and a clear picture of which posts earn best.

Bloggers who quit in month one never see month four. Consistency is the whole game.
