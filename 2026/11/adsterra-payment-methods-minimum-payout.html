Adsterra Payment Methods & Minimum Payout Explained (2026)

The number one question new publishers ask after "how do I get approved" is "how — and when — do I actually get paid?" Adsterra's headline number is attractive: a $5 minimum payout, the lowest threshold of any major ad network. But that $5 comes with fine print — different minimums per method, a two-week hold, and a payout calendar that can make your first payment arrive later than you'd expect.

In this guide I'll lay out the complete official payout table (checked against Adsterra's help center, October 2026), explain how the NET15 schedule really works, and give you the honest pros and cons.

The complete Adsterra payout table (2026)

Here are all seven payout rails, exactly as Adsterra's help center lists them:

Payout method Minimum payout Delivery time Fee
Paxum $5 1–2 business days $1
WebMoney $5 1–2 business days 1% (WMZ) / 2% (WMT)
PayPal (via Hyperwallet) $25 1–2 business days $0.65
Local bank transfer (40+ currencies) $25 Up to 5 business days $7 + exchange rate
Crypto — Tether / Bitcoin / USDC $100 1–2 business days 1% + network fee
Wire transfer $1,000 Up to 5 business days $50 (USD) / $0 (EUR)

Three things jump out immediately:

  1. The "$5 minimum" only applies to Paxum and WebMoney. Pick PayPal and your real threshold is $25; pick wire and it's $1,000. Choose your method before counting down to your first payment.
  2. Fees eat small payouts. A $1 fee on a $5 Paxum withdrawal is 20%. That's still better than waiting months to reach a $100 threshold elsewhere — but go in with open eyes.
  3. Crypto has a geographic catch. Tether and Bitcoin payouts are marked "not available in the EU and EEA" in Adsterra's own payout table. (USDC has wider availability.) If you're in Europe, plan around PayPal, local bank transfer, or wire instead.

How the payout schedule actually works (NET15, twice monthly)

Adsterra pays on a NET15, twice-monthly schedule. Here's what that means in practice:

  • Payout windows: the 1st–2nd and the 16th–17th of each month, between 9:00 AM and 6:00 PM GMT. If a date falls on a weekend or holiday, it rolls to the next business day.
  • It's automatic. Once your balance hits your method's minimum and your payout details are filled in, Adsterra schedules the payment itself — no manual withdrawal request needed.
  • The 2-week hold. After you reach the minimum, there's a two-week hold period while Adsterra verifies the traffic behind the earnings. Only then is the payout released into the next window.

Now the part that surprises new publishers: hitting the minimum doesn't mean money this month. If your balance crosses $5 on, say, the 3rd of the month, the hold plus the window calendar means your payment lands in the following month's early window. The gap between earning and banking can stretch past two weeks — which doesn't quite match the "fast payouts" branding you'll see in reviews.

Two more operational details worth knowing: fill in your payout details early — your information must be complete before the payout period, or the cycle skips you. And payment fields lock around payout dates (roughly three days either side of each window), so change wallet addresses well in advance or the edit waits a full cycle.

Which payout method should you choose?

Just starting out → Paxum ($5). It's the fastest path to a first payout, which matters psychologically: cashing out $5 in month one proves the system works. Paxum accounts are free to open and widely used by affiliates.

Want PayPal → $25 via Hyperwallet. Processed through Adsterra's payment partner (not a direct PayPal transfer); the $0.65 fee is negligible, but $25 takes ~5x longer to reach than Paxum's $5.

Outside the US/EU banking comfort zone → local bank transfer ($25). Payouts in 40+ local currencies remove the old $1,000 wire barrier. The $7 + exchange fee stings on small amounts, so let the balance build first.

Crypto-friendly → $100 minimum. Fine if you're already in crypto — but remember the EU/EEA restriction on Tether/Bitcoin rails, plus 1% + network fee.

Earning serious money → wire transfer ($1,000). The $50 USD fee hurts, but EUR wires are fee-free, and at four-figure monthly earnings it becomes background noise. Requires KYC (see below).

KYC: what wire and bank-transfer users must know

For most methods (Paxum, WebMoney, PayPal, crypto), no identity verification is needed. But wire transfer and local bank transfer trigger mandatory KYC: upload a passport or national ID in English (plus sign an agreement for wire). Verification usually clears fast — but do it before you need the money. Operating as a company? There's a separate KYB process.

This is standard anti-fraud practice, not a red flag — but publishers who skip it and then wonder why their $1,000 wire never arrives are a recurring theme in complaint threads.

Honest pros and cons

Pros

  • $5 minimum via Paxum/WebMoney — the lowest cash-out floor of any major ad network. Beginners can realistically see money in month one.
  • Automatic, twice-monthly payouts — no withdrawal requests, no chasing support for routine payments.
  • Seven payout rails, including 40+ local currencies and crypto — far more choice than AdSense's bank-transfer-or-nothing model.
  • Paper trail: every completed payout generates a downloadable PDF invoice in your dashboard's Payout history (12 months filterable) — better than trusting strangers' screenshots.

Cons

  • The NET15 + hold lag. "Biweekly payouts" sounds fast, but the two-week hold plus window timing means your first payment can take 4–6 weeks from your first dollar earned.
  • Wire transfer is effectively off-limits for small publishers — $1,000 minimum plus a $50 USD fee.
  • Crypto restrictions: Tether and Bitcoin payouts aren't available in the EU/EEA.
  • Fees on small amounts — $1 on a $5 Paxum payout, $7 + exchange on local bank transfers.
  • Mixed reputation: 3.2/5 on Trustpilot across ~960 reviews, with a visible minority reporting payout disputes or withheld balances near thresholds. Read the terms, keep your own records, and never treat one network as your only income stream.
  • Payment fields lock for ~3 days around each payout window — bad timing on an edit costs you a full cycle.

FAQ

What is the minimum payout on Adsterra? $5 via Paxum or WebMoney — the lowest threshold. It's $25 for PayPal (via Hyperwallet) and local bank transfer, $100 for crypto, and $1,000 for wire transfer. The "$5 minimum" marketing line only applies to the first two methods.

How often does Adsterra pay? Twice a month, on the 1st–2nd and 16th–17th (GMT), on NET15 terms — meaning you're paid for earnings from roughly 15+ days earlier. Payments are automatic once you hit your method's minimum.

I reached $5 but haven't been paid. Why? Three usual causes: (1) the two-week hold hasn't elapsed, (2) your payout details aren't fully filled in, or (3) you crossed the threshold just after a window closed — you're queued for the next one. Check the Payouts tab for the scheduled date.

Does Adsterra require ID verification (KYC)? Only for wire transfer and local bank transfer payouts — you'll upload a passport or national ID in English (plus sign an agreement for wire). Paxum, WebMoney, PayPal, and crypto payouts don't require KYC.

Can I get paid in cryptocurrency? Yes — Tether, Bitcoin, and USDC from $100 at 1% + network fee. But Tether and Bitcoin rails aren't available in the EU and EEA.

Is Adsterra legit with payments? It's operated since 2013 and pays thousands of publishers on the biweekly schedule above — but its Trustpilot score is a mixed 3.2/5, with some publishers reporting disputes. My take: the payout system itself is real and documented; the complaints cluster around threshold timing, traffic-quality holds, and support responsiveness. Keep records and diversify.
