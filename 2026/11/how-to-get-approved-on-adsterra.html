How to Get Approved on Adsterra as a Publisher (Step-by-Step, 2026)

Getting approved on Adsterra is one of the fastest ways to start monetizing a website — most publishers are approved in about 10 minutes, and there is no minimum traffic requirement. That makes it a realistic option for brand-new blogs that Google AdSense would reject for "insufficient content" or low traffic.

In this guide, I'll walk you through the exact publisher signup process step by step, what Adsterra actually checks during review, and how to avoid the few things that get applications flagged. Everything here is based on Adsterra's current official documentation (updated October 2026).

Why Adsterra approval is easier than AdSense

Before the steps, here's why this keyword exists at all — publishers search "how to get approved on adsterra" because the bar is genuinely lower than the alternatives:

  • No traffic minimum. Adsterra accepts publishers of all sizes. A blog with 50 visitors a day can get approved; AdSense would likely reject it.
  • ~10-minute review. Adsterra's moderation is famously fast. You can go from signup to placing ad code on the same day.
  • No website required for Smartlink. If you have social traffic but no site yet, the Direct Link (Smartlink) format lets you monetize without adding a website at all.

The trade-off, honestly: approval is easy because Adsterra polices traffic quality instead of site size. Bot traffic or incentivized traffic is prohibited and will get you banned — the easy entry is not a license to send junk traffic.

Step-by-step: signing up as an Adsterra publisher

Step 1 — Go to the publisher signup page

Head to adsterra.com, click Sign Up in the top menu, then choose Get Started as a Publisher. (You can also sign up directly through my referral link — it costs you nothing extra and supports this blog.) Make sure you pick the publisher path, not the advertiser path — they are separate account types.

Step 2 — Enter your details

On the first page you'll be asked for:

  • A valid email address — note: if you already have an Adsterra advertiser or affiliate account, you must use a different email for the publisher account.
  • Your full name.
  • A password — at least 6 characters, including letters, numbers, and special symbols. Adsterra checks your password against known data leaks; if it has appeared in a leak, you'll get an error and need to choose a different one (you get 10 attempts, then a 15-minute wait).
  • A messenger and your messenger account (e.g. Telegram, Skype, WhatsApp) — this is how their team contacts you if anything comes up.
  • Your country of residence.

Double-check the email — everything downstream (verification, payout notifications, ad codes) goes there.

Step 3 — Verify your email

After clicking Sign Up, check your inbox for an email from the Adsterra Team titled "Complete your registration as a Publisher." Click Confirm e-mail. This usually takes you straight to the sign-in page; occasionally you'll need to re-enter your login and password.

Done — you're officially an Adsterra publisher. But you're not earning yet: your account is created, your website still needs approval.

Step 4 — Add your website

In the publisher dashboard, go to the Websites section and click Add Website. You'll submit your site's URL, pick the right category for it, and choose which ad formats you want to use. Take the category seriously — miscategorized sites get flagged in review.

Step 5 — Pass the website review (~10 minutes)

Adsterra reviews the submitted site. In most cases this takes about 10 minutes. You can watch the status on your account page; once it flips to Approved, you'll get a notification.

Step 6 — Get your ad code and place it

After approval, Adsterra sends a service email containing your ad code script (click View Code Scripts right in the email), or you can grab it anytime from the dashboard: Websites page → click your site → Get code. Paste the script where Adsterra's instructions tell you to — placement varies by format.

That's the whole process. Signup to earning can genuinely happen within an hour if your site is ready.

What Adsterra actually checks (and what gets you rejected)

Adsterra is flexible on size but strict on legitimacy. Based on their published requirements, these are the things that matter:

  1. Real human traffic. This is the big one. Bot traffic and incentivized traffic (paying or tricking people to click) are explicitly prohibited. No traffic minimum — but the traffic you do have must be genuine.
  2. A real website with real content. There's no published page-count rule, but a parked domain or a one-page placeholder with no content will not pass. Have a functioning site with actual posts before you submit.
  3. Correct site category. Submitting a tech blog under the wrong vertical looks sloppy and can trigger a manual review.
  4. Prohibited content. Adsterra bars adult content under its current terms. Don't submit a site in a banned category.
  5. Accurate account details. Your name, country, and contact info should be real — these tie into payment verification later.

If your site is declined, fix the specific issue and resubmit rather than opening a second account with a different email — duplicate accounts create more problems than they solve.

FAQ

How long does Adsterra approval take? About 10 minutes for the website review in most cases, per Adsterra's own documentation. The account signup itself is instant after email verification.

Is there a traffic minimum to join Adsterra? No. Adsterra states there are no minimum traffic requirements for publishers and accepts publishers of all sizes — as long as traffic is real and human.

Can I join Adsterra without a website? Yes — the Direct Link (Smartlink) format is designed for monetizing traffic without adding a website. Website-based formats (banners, popunder, etc.) require an approved site.

Why was my Adsterra application rejected? The most common reasons are non-human or incentivized traffic, a site with no real content, a prohibited content category (e.g. adult), or inaccurate account details. Fix the issue and resubmit.
