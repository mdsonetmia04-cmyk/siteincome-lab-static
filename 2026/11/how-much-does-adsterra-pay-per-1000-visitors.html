What "CPM" actually means

CPM stands for cost per mille — the price an advertiser pays for 1,000 ad impressions. If your popunder has a $2 CPM, every 1,000 times it loads earns you $2. That definition already explains most of the confusion around Adsterra rates.

Three distinctions matter. Visitors are not impressions. One visitor can generate zero impressions (ad blocker, bounced before the ad loaded) or several (popunder + Social Bar + a native banner on the same visit). "Per 1,000 visitors" and "per 1,000 impressions" are different numbers, and articles that swap them are comparing apples to oranges. CPM is not eCPM or RPM. Your dashboard also shows eCPM — earnings per 1,000 impressions across all formats — and publishers talk about RPM, earnings per 1,000 pageviews. A site with a $2 popunder CPM and a $1 Social Bar CPM might show a blended eCPM between the two, and an RPM lower still, because not every pageview produces an ad impression. CPM is a bid, not a price. Adsterra runs a real-time auction: advertisers bid for your visitors, and the winning bid becomes your CPM. A US finance reader in December attracts high bids; a Tier-3 entertainment visitor in January attracts low ones. Nobody sets your CPM; the market does, visit by visit.

What actually moves your CPM

Seven factors, roughly in order of impact:

  1. GEO mix. The single biggest lever. A blog whose readers are 70% US/UK earns multiples of one whose readers are 70% Tier-3 — same content, same formats. Check your analytics: if your traffic is mostly Tier-3, your CPM ceiling is low no matter what you optimize.
  2. Niche. Finance and VPN readers attract high bidders; entertainment readers don't.
  3. Format mix. Popunder and Social Bar pay the most; banners pay the least. Running two or three formats together usually beats running one — but each additional format costs you some reader goodwill.
  4. Device. In Adsterra's own published examples, iOS and desktop Windows consistently out-earn Android on the same GEO. A mobile-heavy audience earns less per visitor than a desktop-heavy one.
  5. Traffic quality. Bounce rate, session duration, and bot traffic all feed Adsterra's anti-fraud scoring. Bought traffic, traffic-exchange visits, and bot-heavy sources don't just earn low CPMs — impressions can be filtered out entirely, or your account flagged. Real readers, real sessions.
  6. Placement and frequency. A popunder firing on every pageview earns more impressions — and annoys more readers — than one capped at once per visitor per day. Placement is a trade-off between today's CPM and tomorrow's returning visitors.
  7. Seasonality. Advertisers spend heaviest in Q4 (Black Friday through Christmas) — publisher tests report roughly +30% CPMs in Q4 — and lightest in January, when budgets reset. Weekdays (Tuesday–Thursday) also tend to outbid weekends. If your CPM drops in January, it is probably the calendar, not your site.

FAQ

What is a good Adsterra CPM? For most niches, $3–$5 is good and $8–$15 is excellent — the top end is mostly finance and crypto sites with Tier-1 traffic (Onlyalok, April 2026). Below $1 usually means your traffic is mostly Tier-3, your niche is low-value, or you're running only banners — all fixable, but by different levers.

Does Adsterra pay per click or per 1,000 views? Both, depending on format. Popunder, Social Bar, native, and banners pay per 1,000 impressions (CPM). Smartlink / Direct Link pays per click or per action (CPC/CPA) — there is no fixed CPM for it at all.

Why is my CPM $0.30 when others report $5? Almost always one of three things: your traffic is mostly Tier-3, your niche has low advertiser demand, or you're running only low-paying formats. Check your dashboard's GEO breakdown first — if 80% of your impressions are Tier-3, a $0.30–$1 CPM is normal, not broken.

Which country pays the highest CPM on Adsterra? In Adsterra's own published examples, the US, UK, Germany, and Japan top the list — Germany social traffic on Windows shows the single highest example at $47. But that is one specific combination, not an average; a typical US-heavy site sees far lower blended rates.

Does Adsterra pay more in December? Yes — advertiser spending peaks in Q4, and publisher tests report roughly 30% higher CPMs in Q4 than the yearly average. January is the weakest month, when budgets reset. Plan your expectations around the calendar.
