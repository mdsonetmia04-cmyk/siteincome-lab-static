Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, November 27, and this year the gaming deals are stranger than ever. The biggest game in history, Grand Theft Auto 6, launched just eight days earlier on November 19 and is still sitting at its full price of $79.99. The PlayStation 5 has no official discount at all. The Xbox Series X has no official discount either. And yet, somehow, this is still the biggest week of the year for gaming blogs — because Nintendo is cutting the price of the Switch 2, Sony is slashing controllers and games, Microsoft is discounting more than 2,000 Xbox games by up to 80 percent, and millions of shoppers are searching for the best deal on every one of them. For readers, this guide breaks down every real gaming deal that is live right now. For bloggers, it shows exactly how to turn that search traffic into ad revenue.

Why 2026 Is the Weirdest Black Friday in Gaming History

Every Black Friday has a pattern, and for years the pattern in gaming was simple: the consoles got cheaper, the games got cheaper, and the blogs that published the best roundups collected the traffic. In 2026 that pattern broke. Consoles actually got more expensive during the year. The Xbox Series X now lists at $799.99, and the PS5 Pro lists at $899.99. Sony and Microsoft both raised prices in 2026, and neither company is willing to cut the price of its flagship console for Black Friday. Sony confirmed there are no discounts on new PS5 consoles this year, and Microsoft's Black Friday announcement covers games, controllers and accessories, but not the Xbox Series X or Series S.

Advertisement

That sounds like bad news for deal hunters, but it is actually the opposite. When the console itself does not go on sale, the money moves somewhere else: into bundles, controllers, headsets, games and accessories. Sony's official Black Friday deals are live now, and they include $20 off the DualSense controller, $30 off the DualSense Edge, $100 off the PlayStation VR2 and up to 33 percent off PlayStation Plus for new members, running through November 30. Microsoft's sale runs from November 19 through December 2 with up to 80 percent off more than 2,000 games. Nintendo, meanwhile, is the only console maker cutting a console price this year, with the Switch 2 Mario Kart World bundle down to $499 from $549 between November 22 and December 5.

The Console Deals That Actually Exist

The Nintendo Switch 2 is $469 at Woot, down from its $499.99 list price, with a limit of one per customer while stocks last. That is a rare discount on the console itself, and it is the best deal live right now for anyone who wants the hardware alone. The bigger value, though, is the bundle. The Nintendo Switch 2 plus Mario Kart World bundle is $499, down from $549, live at Amazon and other major retailers through December 5, and it includes a three-month Nintendo Switch Online membership. A second bundle, the Switch 2 with Nintendo Switch Sports Resort, is $529, a saving of $27 over buying the pieces separately. There is even a Switch 2 plus The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time bundle at Walmart for $607, down from $639.

Sony's answer to the console question is different. There is no discount on a new PS5 or PS5 Pro, and last year Sony took $100 off every model, so shoppers waiting for a repeat will be disappointed. What Sony is doing instead is refurbished hardware. A certified refurbished PS5 Digital Edition is $499 at PlayStation Direct, down from $599, and the disc version is $549, down from $649. These are Sony-refurbished units, not new consoles, and the offer runs from November 16 through December 21. PlayStation Direct is also offering an extra $50 off a new PS5 when you trade in an old console over the same period. For a buyer who does not mind a refurbished unit, that $100 saving is the closest thing to a PS5 console discount this year.

Microsoft is not cutting console prices at all. The Xbox Series X lists at $799.99, and the 2026 Xbox Black Friday Sale, which runs from November 19 at 9 p.m. Pacific Time through December 2, includes no console price cut. Last year Microsoft's own store did not cut console prices either, so this is consistent, but it means Xbox shoppers should focus their money on games and accessories, where the discounts are genuinely deep.

PlayStation Deals: Controllers, PS VR2, PS Plus and Games

Sony's official Black Friday sale began on November 16, and it is the most generous PlayStation sale in years once you look past the console. The DualSense wireless controller is $54.99, down from $74.99, a $20 saving available at Amazon, Best Buy, GameStop, PlayStation Direct, Target and Walmart. The premium DualSense Edge is $169, down from $199, a $30 saving. Those two prices alone will drive an enormous amount of search traffic, because controllers are the single most-searched PlayStation accessory every Black Friday, and this year the discount is real and widely available.

The PlayStation VR2 is $100 off, which makes it one of the biggest single-item savings in Sony's sale. PlayStation Plus is up to 33 percent off for new members through November 30. That subscription discount matters more than it looks, because a new PS Plus member is a reader who will come back to your blog every time a monthly games announcement drops. If your post converts a reader into a PS Plus subscriber, that reader has a reason to return to your site twelve times a year.

Then there are the games, and this is where the PlayStation Store sale does its heaviest work. The store sale runs from November 4 through November 30 with up to 75 percent off thousands of PS5, PS4 and PS VR2 games, expansions and downloadable content. The standout prices this year include Ghost of Yotei at $34.99, down from $69.99, at PlayStation Direct, and Resident Evil Requiem at $39.99, down from $69.99, at Amazon. Elden Ring is $19.99 at Amazon. Avatar: Frontiers of Pandora, the From the Ashes Edition, is $14.97. This year's sports games are $29.99 across the board: College Football 27, EA Sports FC 27 and NBA 2K27 are all $29.99 at Amazon, more than half off their launch prices.

Xbox Deals: 2,000 Games and No Console Cut

Microsoft's Black Friday sale is the biggest game sale of the year by sheer volume. More than 2,000 games are discounted by up to 80 percent, running from November 19 through December 2. That is a larger catalogue than Sony's sale and a longer window, and it covers everything from this year's sports titles to older games that rarely go on sale.

The hardware story on Xbox is about controllers and headsets. Xbox wireless controllers start at $42.99, with the Carbon Black controller at $42.99 at Amazon, Best Buy, GameStop, Target and Walmart, and the Pulse Red and Shock Blue controllers at $49. The Xbox wireless headset in Carbon Black is $107.99. The Xbox Design Lab is offering up to 15 percent off custom controllers with free engraving from November 19 through December 2, which is a genuinely useful deal for anyone buying a gift.

The game prices are just as strong as PlayStation's. Forza Horizon 6 is $42 at Amazon, Best Buy, GameStop, Target and Walmart. The sports games match Sony's prices: College Football 27, EA Sports FC 27 and NBA 2K27 are $29.99. That price parity matters for bloggers, because it means you can write one comparison section that serves both PlayStation and Xbox readers, and both audiences will find it useful.

Nintendo Switch 2: The Only Console With a Real Discount

Nintendo is the star of Black Friday 2026, and the reason is simple: it is the only company actually cutting the price of a current console. The Switch 2 launched this year, and demand is still high, so a $30 discount on the console itself and a $50 discount on the Mario Kart World bundle are the most newsworthy deals of the entire sale season.

The numbers are worth repeating because they are the numbers readers are searching for. The standalone Switch 2 is $469 at Woot, down from $499.99. The Mario Kart World bundle is $499, down from $549, from November 22 through December 5 at Amazon, Best Buy, GameStop, Target, Walmart and the Nintendo Store. The Sports Resort bundle is $529. The Ocarina of Time bundle is $607 at Walmart, down from $639. The Nintendo eShop sale runs through December 2, with discounts on digital games across the Switch and Switch 2 libraries.

Nintendo's retail deals started on Sunday, November 22, which means the Switch 2 story is fresh right now. For a blog publishing today, this is the most time-sensitive section of the entire post, and it deserves the most prominent placement. Put the Switch 2 prices near the top, update them daily, and say plainly when a deal is gone. Readers reward that honesty with return visits, and return visits are the foundation of ad revenue.

PC Gaming Deals: Monitors, Keyboards and Capture Cards

Console deals get the headlines, but PC gaming deals are where the biggest individual savings live, and they are the easiest section for a blog to monetize, because every item is a specific product with a specific price and a specific buyer.

The headline PC deal this year is the Lenovo Legion Tower 5 with an RTX 5070 Ti graphics card and a Ryzen 7 7800X3D processor, priced at $2,499.99. That is a premium machine at a premium price, but it is exactly the kind of product that deal-season readers research for days before buying, which means they read long articles, compare specifications and come back to the same page repeatedly. A blog post that covers that machine honestly, with its real price and its real specifications, earns far more ad impressions than a post that lists ten cheap accessories.

Monitors are the other big PC story. Dell, HP, Lenovo and Newegg all have OLED gaming monitors starting from $279.99 this Black Friday. That price point matters because OLED monitors were a luxury item two years ago, and now they are within reach of a mid-range buyer. A reader who has been waiting for an OLED monitor to drop below $300 is a reader with a very specific search query, and a blog that answers it with the real price and the real retailer wins that reader.

The 8BitDo Retro 87 mechanical keyboard is also discounted. The ASUS ROG Pelta wireless gaming headset is $64.99, down from $129.99, a saving of almost exactly half. The AVerMedia Live Gamer Ultra 2.1 capture card, the device streamers use to record console gameplay in high quality, is $229.99 at Amazon through November 30. Epomaker's Black Friday and Cyber Monday sale runs through December 3, with 15 to 45 percent off keyboards and up to 50 percent off keycaps, switches and accessories.

GTA 6 Changes Everything — Except Its Price

No Black Friday gaming guide is complete without Grand Theft Auto 6, and this year the story is what is not happening. GTA 6 launched on November 19, eight days before Black Friday, and it is still full price at $79.99. There is no discount, no bundle and no official Black Friday offer, and that is exactly why it matters so much for gaming blogs.

GTA 6 is the biggest entertainment launch in history. Our own launch-week coverage broke down how gaming blogs turn the release into ad revenue, and the numbers are still climbing. The game dominates every gaming search trend, and every Black Friday shopper who buys a console this week is buying it to play GTA 6. That means the searches overlap: "PS5 Black Friday deal" and "GTA 6 PS5 bundle" and "best console to play GTA 6" are all the same reader at different stages of the same decision. A blog that covers GTA 6 alongside the Black Friday deals captures that reader twice.

For bloggers, GTA 6 is the traffic engine that powers the whole week. A post about Black Friday gaming deals that mentions GTA 6 in its opening paragraph, its headings and its conclusion will rank for GTA-related searches that a pure deals post never touches. How Gaming Blogs Actually Turn These Deals Into Ad Revenue

So far this post has been about the deals. Now it is about the money, because that is the reason this blog exists. Gaming blogs do not earn from the deals themselves. They earn from the traffic the deals bring, and from the ads that traffic sees. Here is how that works in practice, step by step.

Advertisement

First, search volume explodes. In a normal week, a gaming blog might get a few hundred visits a day from Google. In Black Friday week, a single well-ranked deals post can get thousands of visits a day, because millions of people are searching for the same prices at the same time. That traffic is worth more than normal traffic, because the readers are in buying mode. They stay longer, they read more carefully, and they click more ads. A reader who is comparing three headsets is a reader who will scroll past three ad units without thinking about it.

Second, the money is in the update, not the publish. A deals post published on November 22 and never touched again will earn for two days and then fade, because its prices will be wrong by November 25 and Google will notice. A deals post updated every morning with the new prices, the sold-out items and the new bundles will keep its ranking through Cyber Monday on November 30 and beyond. Every update is a new reason for Google to crawl the page, and every crawl is a new chance to rank higher. The bloggers who treat a deals post as a living document earn five to ten times more from it than the bloggers who treat it as a one-time publication.

Third, internal links multiply the value of every visitor. How to Spot a Fake Black Friday Gaming Deal

Not every discount this week is real, and the fake ones are the biggest threat to a gaming blog's credibility. Here is how to tell the difference, and how to protect your readers.

The most common trick is the inflated original price. A headset listed at "$129.99, now $64.99" is a real deal only if the headset actually sold at $129.99 before the sale. The ASUS ROG Pelta at $64.99, down from $129.99, is a genuine halving, because that was its real price. But many retailers raise the "was" price in November so the discount looks bigger. Before you publish a price, check what the product cost in October. If you cannot verify the original price, do not quote it. Quote the sale price only, and say what the product costs, not what the retailer claims it used to cost.

The second trick is the bundle that costs more than its parts. A console bundle that includes a game you do not want, a controller you already own and a subscription you will never use is not a deal, even if the headline price looks lower than the console alone. Always add up the pieces. The Switch 2 Mario Kart World bundle at $499 is a real deal because the console alone is $499.99 and the game is included. A bundle that charges you $549 for a console and a game you could buy separately for $520 is not.

For bloggers, covering fake deals is as valuable as covering real ones. A post titled "PS5 Black Friday deal: is it real?" will rank for the same searches as the fake deal itself, and it will earn the trust of every reader who was about to be fooled. Trust is the asset that compounds. A reader who trusts your prices in November will trust your reviews in January, and will come back in February, March and April, long after the deal season is over.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 is the strangest gaming sale in years, and that is exactly what makes it the best one for gaming blogs. There is no PS5 discount and no Xbox console discount, but there is a $469 Switch 2 at Woot, a $499 Mario Kart World bundle, a $54.99 DualSense controller, a $100 saving on the PlayStation VR2, more than 2,000 Xbox games at up to 80 percent off, a PlayStation Store sale through November 30 with up to 75 percent off, and OLED monitors from $279.99. GTA 6 is full price at $79.99 and will stay that way, and that is fine, because the game is the reason everyone is shopping in the first place. For readers, the advice is simple: buy the Switch 2 bundle while it lasts, buy the controllers and games that are actually discounted, and do not wait for a console discount that is not coming. For bloggers, the advice is simpler still: publish early, list the real prices, update every day, link to your other posts, and let the traffic do the rest. The deals end on December 5. The audience you earn this week does not have to.
