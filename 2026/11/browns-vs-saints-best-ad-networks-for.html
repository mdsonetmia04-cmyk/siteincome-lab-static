Browns vs Saints: Best Ad Networks for Sports Blogs in 2026

November 9, 20264 min readBy Sonet Mia

Browns vs Saints is trending with more than 500,000 searches in America today. Every big NFL game sends the same message to bloggers: sports traffic is huge, regular, and valuable.

But traffic alone pays nothing. The ad network you choose decides how much of that traffic turns into money. This post compares the best ad networks for sports blogs in 2026 — what each one is good at, who it accepts, and which one fits a new blog.

Why Sports Traffic Pays Well

Advertisers pay more for sports readers than for most other topics, for three reasons:

  1. Volume — big games bring thousands of visitors in hours.
  2. Regularity — the season schedule guarantees fresh traffic every week.
  3. Engagement — fans read longer and visit more pages than casual readers.

A sports blog with 50,000 monthly visitors can earn more than a general blog with twice the traffic, because the audience is more valuable to advertisers.

1. Adsterra — Best for New Sports Blogs

Adsterra is the most beginner-friendly option for sports blogs. It approves small and new websites, offers many ad formats (Native Banner, Social Bar, Banner, Popunder), and pays weekly or biweekly depending on your settings. Its approval process is fast, and it works well with Blogspot blogs.

Best for: new blogs, small traffic, bloggers who want to start earning immediately. Our approval guide: How to Get Approved on Adsterra.

2. Google AdSense — Highest Standards, Strictest Rules

AdSense is the best-known network, and it can pay well for US traffic. But it is strict: your blog needs original content, a clean design, privacy pages, and usually some traffic history before approval. Many new sports blogs get rejected on the first try.

Best for: established blogs with steady traffic and original content. See the full comparison: Adsterra vs AdSense.

3. PropellerAds and Monetag — Alternatives Worth Knowing

PropellerAds and Monetag (formerly PropellerAds' sister platform) accept small publishers and offer push notifications, popunders, and native ads. They can earn well on high-volume sports traffic, but their aggressive formats can annoy readers if overused. Use them carefully — one format at a time — and watch your bounce rate.

Best for: blogs with viral, high-volume traffic that want extra formats beyond banners.

4. Media.net — Contextual Ads for US Traffic

Media.net (powered by Yahoo and Bing) serves contextual text ads that fit well with article content. It works best for blogs with mostly US and UK traffic and clean, text-heavy articles. Approval is stricter than Adsterra, and earnings depend heavily on your visitors' location.

Best for: blogs with strong US/UK readership and long-form articles.

How to Choose the Right Network

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. How much traffic do I have? Under 10,000 monthly visitors — start with Adsterra. Over 50,000 — test AdSense or Media.net as well.
  2. Where are my readers? US and European traffic earns the most everywhere. If your readers are mostly from one country, pick the network that pays best for that country.
  3. What formats fit my content? Sports readers are on mobile during games. Native banners and social bars fit that behaviour better than heavy popups.

Many successful sports bloggers use two networks: Adsterra for instant monetization from day one, and AdSense added later once traffic grows. There is no rule that says you must pick only one.

The Formats That Earn Most on Sports Blogs

  • Native Banner inside the article — readers see it while reading the recap.
  • Social Bar on mobile — stays visible during long game threads.
  • Banner 728x90 at the top of the post — the classic leaderboard, best for desktop readers.

Place ads where fans actually look: inside the article and at the top of the post. Ads hidden at the bottom of the page earn almost nothing.

The Bottom Line

Browns vs Saints shows the opportunity: half a million searches for one game. The bloggers who earn from that traffic are not the ones with the biggest sites — they are the ones with the right ad network set up before the traffic arrives. If your blog is new, start with Adsterra, add a second network when your traffic grows, and let every game work for you.
