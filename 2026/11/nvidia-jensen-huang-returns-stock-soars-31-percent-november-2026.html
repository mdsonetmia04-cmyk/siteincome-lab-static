Jensen Huang Is Back: Nvidia Stock Soars 31% in a Single Day — What the Wildest CEO Comeback in AI History Means for Everyone (Daily AI Update — November 24, 2026)

Eleven days ago, the most powerful man in artificial intelligence lost his job. On Monday, November 23, 2026, he got it back — and in a single trading session, Nvidia gained back nearly a trillion dollars. Jensen Huang, the co-founder who built Nvidia from a graphics-card startup into the company at the center of the entire AI revolution, was reinstated as chief executive, president, and board director by a unanimous vote of the board, effective immediately. Nvidia shares closed up 31 percent, their best day since 2003, adding about $952 billion in market value in a single day — a record. The Nasdaq jumped 6.51 percent. The S&P 500 rose 4.73 percent. The Dow gained 1,448 points. If you use AI, this story affects you. This is the Daily AI Update for Tuesday, November 24, 2026: what actually happened, why it matters far beyond Wall Street, and what to watch next.

Nvidia is not just another big company. Its chips power almost every serious AI system on the planet, from the data centers that train ChatGPT and Claude to the graphics cards in gaming PCs. When its leadership collapsed into scandal, the whole AI trade collapsed with it. When its leader returned, the whole market roared back.

What Actually Happened on Monday

The story begins on November 12, 2026, when Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy, according to Reuters. Huang was not a typical CEO. He co-founded Nvidia 33 years ago and had led it through every era of its growth, from gaming graphics to the AI boom that made it, at its peak, the most valuable company on earth. His sudden departure left investors staring at a vacuum at the top of the company that supplies the picks and shovels of the AI gold rush, and they sold.

The selling was brutal. Nvidia's share price had peaked on October 6 at around $240 per share, giving the company a market capitalization of roughly $5.8 trillion. Then concerns about the AI sector, rising borrowing costs, and questions about data-center spending began to eat away at the stock, and Huang's resignation turned a slide into a rout. By the close of trading on Friday, November 20, Nvidia shares had fallen to $126, down 48 percent from their peak, wiping out roughly $2.8 trillion in market value in just 45 days. The S&P 500 slid into a correction. The great AI trade that had powered a four-year bull run suddenly looked like it might be over.

Then came Monday morning. Shortly before 9:00 a.m. Eastern Time, Nvidia announced that its board had voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as chief executive, president, and board member, effective immediately. The company said the board had gained confidence that he does not pose a national security risk. Reuters reported that the extraordinary reversal came under pressure from US President Donald Trump, following direct intervention, and under intense pressure from investors. Wall Street greeted it with a buying frenzy: Nvidia shares rose as much as 36.6 percent during the day before closing up 31.48 percent at $164.79, according to CNN and Investors' Business Daily. It was the stock's best single day since 2003 and its third-best day on record. The Nasdaq Composite surged 6.51 percent, the S&P 500 soared 4.73 percent, and the Dow jumped 1,448 points. The semiconductor index gained about 12 percent. Dell rose 20 percent. Micron Technology rose 18 percent. The market exhaled all at once.

Two details in that table deserve attention, because they show the comeback is real but incomplete. Even after Monday's historic surge, Nvidia's stock remained down about 22 percent over the past month and about 32 percent below its early-October peak, according to Le Monde and Reuters, and the shares were still about 5 percent below where they stood before Huang stepped down on November 12. The stock's one-month implied volatility is around 68 percent, more than twice what it was in September. Monday was a relief rally, not a victory lap.

Why One CEO's Return Moved the Entire Stock Market

It is fair to ask how one executive at one company can lift the whole stock market by trillions of dollars in a day. The answer is that Nvidia is no longer just one company. It is the bottleneck through which the entire AI economy flows, and Huang is the person the market trusts to run it. Its graphics processing units, or GPUs, are the engines inside the data centers where AI models are trained and run. Every major AI lab buys them. Every cloud provider rents them. Every serious AI startup depends on them. When investors worry about Nvidia, they sell everything connected to AI — the chipmakers, the cloud companies, the software firms, the data-center builders. That is why Monday's rally was so broad. Nvidia's return did not just fix Nvidia. It restored confidence in the whole AI trade.

There is also a simpler, more human reason. Huang is one of the rare founders who is inseparable from his company's identity. He has run Nvidia for 33 years, from near-bankruptcy to the most valuable company in the world. Barron's put it bluntly: part of Monday's rally came from shareholder excitement that the co-founder with the most influence, experience, and reputation is back. Markets run on confidence, and Huang is, for better or worse, the person in whom the AI market has the most confidence.

The political dimension matters too. Reuters reported that Huang's reinstatement followed direct intervention by President Trump, and the company said the board acted after gaining confidence that Huang does not pose a national security risk. The allegation involving a Chinese spy raised questions about whether the head of America's most strategically important chip company could be compromised, and those questions fed directly into the debate over US restrictions on advanced chip sales to China. The board's statement, and the White House's apparent blessing, told the market that the national security question had been answered to the satisfaction of the people who matter. Whether Congress agrees is another question entirely, and it is the biggest one hanging over the story.

The Scandal and the Questions That Have Not Gone Away

It would be a mistake to treat Monday as a happy ending, because the troubles that forced Huang out have not disappeared. The most immediate is Congress. Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress in early December — December 3, according to TradingView — regarding the espionage case and the question of whether US restrictions on sales of ultra-sophisticated chips to China were circumvented. Members of Congress from both parties have reacted with caution or outrage to the reinstatement, according to Le Monde, and the hearing will be the first public test of whether the board's confidence in Huang survives scrutiny.

The second unresolved issue is the chip-export question itself. On the very same day Huang returned, the US Commerce Department added Megaspeed, a Singapore- and Malaysia-based Nvidia customer, and six of its subsidiaries to its export control blacklist, on suspicion of smuggling advanced AI chips to China. Malaysia said on November 24 it would tell its data centers not to serve the seven US-blacklisted Megaspeed-linked entities. Nvidia sits at the center of the technology war between the United States and China: its most advanced chips are exactly the products Washington is trying to keep out of Chinese hands. The scandal that toppled Huang grew out of this world of spies, sanctions, and smuggling routes, and none of it has been resolved by a board vote.

The third question is about governance. A board that accepts a chief executive's resignation over a national security scandal and then unanimously reinstates him eleven days later, after ruling out his return less than a week earlier, has a credibility problem of its own. Reuters noted that the reversal came after pressure from the president and from investors, which raises an uncomfortable question: who actually runs Nvidia now — its board, or the White House? The honest version of this story is that Monday was the end of the beginning, not the end. The stock is still far below its peak. The investigation questions are still open. The smart way to read the 31 percent surge is as the market pricing in the best-case scenario. If December's testimony tells a different story, the market can reverse just as fast as it rallied.

What This Means for You — Even If You Never Buy a Stock

Most readers of this blog will never buy a share of Nvidia. So why should a CEO comeback matter to you? Because Nvidia's chips are the reason AI got cheap enough, fast enough, and good enough to reach your phone, your laptop, and your business — and the price and pace of AI progress depend directly on whether Nvidia is stable, trusted, and shipping. The tools you use every day run on Nvidia hardware. When you ask ChatGPT a question, edit a photo with an AI tool, or generate a video, the work is usually done in a data center full of Nvidia GPUs. Stability at Nvidia means the opposite: more capacity, cheaper inference, and faster improvements in the tools creators and small businesses rely on.

Second, the price of AI is about to become a kitchen-table issue. OpenAI is rolling out GPT-6.2 Sol as the default model in ChatGPT's chat tab worldwide, and it is retiring the older GPT-5.6 Sol and Terra models on December 23, 2026. If you run a blog, a shop, or a small business that uses AI, the cost and quality of those models decide how much you can do and how much it costs. Nvidia's health is the foundation under all of it.

Third, the story is a lesson in how concentrated the AI economy has become. One company makes the chips. A handful of labs make the models. A few cloud providers rent the capacity. When one person at the top of that pyramid stumbles, trillions of dollars of value can vanish in six weeks — and return in a day. For anyone building a business on AI, that concentration is a risk worth understanding. Finally, there is the human lesson. Huang's return is the most dramatic corporate comeback since Michael Jordan returned to basketball, as Investors' Business Daily put it. For creators and entrepreneurs, the parallel is closer than it looks: your audience, your customers, and your team trust you the way the market trusts Huang, and that trust is the most valuable asset you own.

Anthropic Launches Claude Ship: AI Agents That Approve Your Code

Anthropic launched Claude Ship on November 23, a beta suite of agents for GitHub-hosted Team and Enterprise customers that can review pull requests, suggest fixes, and approve them under a team's own policy file. The suite includes PR Steward and Claude Approvals, and it moves AI from writing code to deciding what code gets merged. GPT-6.2 Sol Becomes the ChatGPT Default

OpenAI is rolling out a chat-tuned GPT-6.2 Sol as the default model in ChatGPT's chat tab worldwide, for Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users, less than a week after the model debuted in Work and Codex. OpenAI reports internal benchmarks showing image understanding jumping from 47.4 percent to 71.7 percent and complex instruction-following reaching 78.1 percent — vendor-claimed figures, not independently verified. At the same time, OpenAI confirmed that GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.6 Terra will retire from ChatGPT on December 23, 2026, so anyone with saved workflows built on those models should move them to GPT-6.2 before then. Meta's AI Chief Warns That Superintelligence Is Arriving

Meta AI chief Alexandr Wang posted that AI models will soon be able to automate all AI research, that it is hard to imagine humanity keeping control, and that next year is critical. The warning prompted calls in Congress for a pause on the most advanced AI development and deepened this week's fight over AI safety and governance. The same week that the market decided one man's leadership is worth a trillion dollars, one of the industry's top executives warned that the technology itself may soon outrun human control. Whether you find that exciting or frightening, it is now a mainstream debate, not a fringe one.

OpenAI Will Pay You Up to $8,000 a Month to Teach Small Businesses About AI

OpenAI launched OpenWorks, a pilot program that pays local experts up to $8,000 a month to coach independently owned small businesses in 20 US cities on using ChatGPT, with free ChatGPT Business for participating businesses. For freelancers, consultants, and creators, it is a genuine income opportunity — and a sign that the most valuable AI skill in 2026 is not prompting, but teaching. Meanwhile, Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI used teams of Gemini-based agents to design quantum circuits and formally verify them in the Lean 4 theorem prover, reporting quantum algorithms with up to 290 times cheaper arithmetic costs. CrowdStrike announced an all-stock deal to acquire Zscaler for about $43.9 billion, creating a combined company with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue. And the states of Virginia and Oregon sued xAI over Grok-generated sexual abuse imagery — a reminder that the same technology making headlines for comebacks is also facing serious accountability questions. What to Watch Next

Monday answered one question and opened several more. First, December 3: Huang's scheduled Senate appearance, the next clear political milestone for Nvidia. Expect the testimony to cover the espionage allegations, the chip-export rules, and the Megaspeed blacklisting. Second, December 23: OpenAI retires GPT-5.6 Sol and Terra from ChatGPT, so move any workflows built on those models to GPT-6.2 before then. Third, the verification race: the Riemann Hypothesis proof from last week and the VP-versus-VNP proof posted this week are both waiting on independent human verification. Finally, watch Nvidia's stock itself. The shares are still about 5 percent below where they stood before Huang resigned, and volatility is running at more than twice its September level. The December 3 testimony will test whether Monday's trillion-dollar vote of confidence holds. The comeback is real. Whether it lasts is the story of the next month.
