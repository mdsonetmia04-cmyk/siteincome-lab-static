How to Monetize a Blogspot Blog in 2026 (AdSense + Adsterra Setup)

Can you actually make money from a free Blogspot blog? Yes — and I can say that with unusual confidence, because you're reading this on one. SiteIncome Lab runs on Blogspot, and this post documents the two real monetization routes available on the platform: Google AdSense through Blogger's built-in Earnings tab, and Adsterra through ad codes you place yourself.

One is strict and slow; the other is fast and flexible. Here's the honest setup guide for both, the real requirements nobody prints on the signup page, and how I'd combine them.

Why Blogspot is a legitimate place to start

The "free blog = not serious" attitude is outdated. Here's what Blogspot actually gives you in 2026:

  • Free hosting on Google's infrastructure — fast, secure (HTTPS by default), zero server bills.
  • A free blogspot.com address — no, the subdomain doesn't block monetization. Adsterra officially accepts Blogspot sites, and AdSense approves them too. (A custom domain helps branding long-term, but it's not a day-one requirement.)
  • Built-in AdSense integration — Blogger and AdSense are both Google products, so the connection is a dashboard flow, not a code-pasting puzzle.
  • Full HTML/JavaScript access — unlike some free platforms, Blogger lets you paste any third-party ad code via gadgets or theme editing.

The real limitations: less design flexibility than WordPress, fewer SEO plugins, and you're building on rented land — Google can change the rules. But for going from zero to first ad dollar, the price (free) is hard to argue with.

Route 1: AdSense via Blogger's Earnings tab

AdSense is the built-in, official route — and the one with the highest bar.

The setup (once you're ready)

  1. In Blogger, select your blog, then click Earnings in the left menu.
  2. Click Create AdSense account (or "Sign up for AdSense").
  3. Fill in the form with your Google account details, payment country, and phone verification, then submit.
  4. Wait for Google's review — typically days to a few weeks.
  5. Once approved, place ads via Layout → Blog Posts → Show Ads Between Posts (in-article) or Layout → Add a Gadget → AdSense (sidebar units). Note: the AdSense gadget only becomes usable after your blog is approved for showing Google ads.

The requirements nobody prints clearly

Google doesn't publish a checklist that says "20 posts and you're in." What it does require: a site you control, content in a supported language, and compliance with the AdSense program policies and publisher terms. In practice, the publisher community has converged on a preparation checklist that actually gets approvals:

  • 15–20 substantial, original articles (700–1,000+ words each) — thin or copied content is the #1 rejection reason.
  • About, Contact, and Privacy Policy pages — AdSense reviewers look for a real publication, not an anonymous shell. (This blog has About and Contact; a Privacy Policy page is next on my list for exactly this reason.)
  • A clean, mobile-friendly theme with working navigation — most of your readers are on phones.
  • Some real traffic — there's no official minimum, but a blog with zero visitors looks unfinished.

Two things worth knowing: there's no official "your blog must be 6 months old" rule — that myth survives in old tutorials, though a consistent publishing history does help. And a Blogger-connected AdSense account is a hosted account: it covers your Blogspot/blogger sites, not every website you'll ever own.

The honest truth about AdSense approval

AdSense rejects most first-time applications. Common reasons: "insufficient content," policy violations, or navigation that looks unfinished. Rejection isn't permanent — fix what's flagged and reapply — but if you need revenue this month, AdSense is the slow lane. That's why route 2 exists.

Route 2: Adsterra on Blogspot (step by step)

Adsterra officially supports Blogspot and even publishes its own setup guide for it. Approval takes ~10 minutes with no traffic minimum — the polar opposite of AdSense's review.

Step 1 — Add your blog to Adsterra

  1. Sign up as an Adsterra publisher (or log in).
  2. In the left menu, open the Websites tab and click ADD WEBSITE.
  3. Enter your blog's URL (your *.blogspot.com address works fine), pick the closest category (choose Other if nothing fits), and select your first Ad Unit — each ad code is unique to one format.
  4. Click the green ADD button. Your site shows Pending until review finishes (~10 minutes), then flips to Approved.

Full signup walkthrough: How to Get Approved on Adsterra as a Publisher (Step-by-Step, 2026).

Step 2 — Generate your ad code

After approval, go to All codes → Get code for the format you want. Copy the code. Remember: each code is unique to one placement — two banners means generating two codes. Which format to pick first? My take: Adsterra Ad Formats Explained: Popunder, Social Bar, Native, Banners, Direct Link.

Step 3a — Place banners/native via Layout gadgets (no HTML editing)

For ad units that sit in a visible slot (sidebar banner, header banner, native unit):

  1. In Blogger, go to Layout.
  2. Click Add a Gadget in the section where you want the ad (sidebar, header, footer).
  3. Choose HTML/JavaScript from the gadget list.
  4. Paste your Adsterra code into the Content field, click Save.
  5. Drag and drop the gadget to position it, then Save arrangement.

That's it — the ad renders on every page using that layout section.

Step 3b — Place popunder/site-wide scripts via the theme

Popunder code isn't attached to any visible element — it fires on click anywhere — so a gadget slot is the wrong tool. For site-wide scripts:

  1. In Blogger, go to Theme → Edit HTML.
  2. Paste the script just before the closing </body> tag (or </head> — follow the placement note Adsterra shows when you generate the code; it tells you exactly where each format goes).
  3. Save the theme.

Back up your theme first (Theme → Backup/Restore → Download theme). One misplaced character in theme HTML can break your layout, and a backup turns a disaster into a two-minute restore.

Step 3c — The zero-code option: Direct Link

For a text link, button, or image, you don't touch the theme at all — just use the Smartlink URL Adsterra generates as the link's destination. It also works outside your blog: social bios, video descriptions, anywhere you can paste a link. Practical tips that save beginners real pain

  • Don't overload ads. Adsterra's own guidance warns that oversaturated pages drive churn — and churn kills the traffic your earnings depend on. Start with one or two placements.
  • One popunder code per page, max. Multiple popunder codes on the same page can conflict — this is straight from Adsterra's Blogspot guide.
  • Preview after every placement. Open the live page and check that ads don't overlap content or navigation buttons. Poor placement tanks both UX and CPM.
  • Check mobile. Most Blogspot traffic is mobile. A 728×90 leaderboard that looks fine on desktop can break a phone layout — use responsive sizes or the 320×50 mobile banner.
  • Traffic first, optimization second. No ad format earns on zero visitors. Your first 90 days should be ~80% content, ~20% monetization setup.
  • Never click your own ads — on AdSense this gets accounts banned; on any network, invalid traffic gets earnings clawed back. And never buy bot traffic: it's the fastest route to a ban everywhere.

Honest pros and cons of monetizing on Blogspot

Pros

  • Truly free start: hosting, HTTPS, and a subdomain cost nothing — your only investment is content.
  • Two real revenue paths: AdSense's built-in flow plus any third-party network via gadgets/theme code.
  • Adsterra officially supports Blogspot with a published setup guide — no hacks or workarounds needed.
  • Fast iteration: gadget-based placements let you test formats without touching code.

Cons

  • AdSense's bar is genuinely high — most new Blogspot blogs get rejected first try; budget weeks, not days.
  • Rented land: Google controls the platform, the subdomain, and can change policies — a custom domain + self-hosting is the long-term hedge.
  • Limited design/SEO tooling versus WordPress — you work within Blogger's template system.
  • "Free blog" perception — some advertisers and readers take custom domains more seriously; it matters at the margins, not at the start.

FAQ

Can you monetize a free Blogspot blog? Yes. You can run AdSense (via Blogger's Earnings tab, after approval), third-party ad networks like Adsterra (via HTML/JavaScript gadgets or theme code), and affiliate links — as long as you follow each service's rules and Blogger's content policy. The free subdomain doesn't block monetization by itself.

How do I add Adsterra ads to my Blogspot blog? Add your blog in Adsterra (Websites → ADD WEBSITE), wait for approval, generate the ad code, then in Blogger go to Layout → Add a Gadget → HTML/JavaScript and paste the code — or Theme → Edit HTML for site-wide scripts like popunder.

What are the requirements for AdSense on Blogger? Google requires a site you control, content in a supported language, and policy compliance. In practice, successful applicants typically have 15–20 original articles, About/Contact/Privacy pages, a mobile-friendly theme, and real traffic. There's no official minimum post count or 6-month age rule — those are community observations, not Google policy.

Is AdSense or Adsterra better for a new Blogspot blog? Adsterra first: approval in ~10 minutes, no traffic minimum, earnings from day one. AdSense later: stricter and slower, but higher trust and typically better rates for display inventory once approved. Running both — AdSense banners plus one Adsterra format — is the common endgame.

Do I need a custom domain to earn from Blogspot? No. Both AdSense and Adsterra accept *.blogspot.com addresses. A custom domain (~$12/year) helps branding and long-term asset value, but it's an upgrade, not a prerequisite.

Why aren't my ads showing after I paste the code? Usual suspects: the site is still Pending approval in Adsterra (wait for Approved status); an ad blocker in your own browser; the gadget wasn't saved with "Save arrangement" in Layout; or for theme edits, the script landed in the wrong place — re-check Adsterra's placement note for that format.
