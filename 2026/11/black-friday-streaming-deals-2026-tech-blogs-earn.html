Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, November 27, and the streaming deals are already live. Paramount+ is down to $2.99 a month. Peacock is offering three months for $3 a month. Apple TV costs half price for six months. The Disney+, Hulu and Max bundle is $22 a month with ads. And this is the part most shoppers miss: the best streaming deals of the year are not really about saving a few dollars on a subscription. They are about timing. Every one of these offers is built to lock you in before the calendar turns, because the streaming services know that a subscriber who signs up in November is still paying in March. For readers, this guide lists every real streaming deal that is live right now, with the actual prices, the renewal prices, and the catches nobody puts in the headline. For bloggers, it shows why streaming deals are one of the easiest kinds of Black Friday traffic to win, and exactly how to turn that traffic into ad revenue.

The Streaming Deals That Are Live Right Now

Every price in this section was checked against live deal roundups on November 22 and 23, 2026. Prices change fast during deal week, so treat this as a snapshot of what is live today, and always confirm the price on the provider's own checkout page before you subscribe. That rule applies to readers and to bloggers: a deals post with a wrong price loses trust faster than any other kind of post.

Paramount+: $2.99 a Month for Two Months

Paramount+ is running the simplest streaming deal of the season. New and returning subscribers can get either the Essential plan or the Premium plan for $2.99 a month for two months. Premium is normally the ad-free tier, with the local CBS live feed, the full Showtime library, downloads for offline viewing and 4K on selected titles, so getting it for the same price as Essential for two months is the better move if you are choosing between the two. One detail matters at checkout: sign in before you select your plan, not after, or the checkout page may switch you to the Essential plan. Paramount+ is also offering 30 percent off a full year with the code SAVEANNUAL30, for new and returning subscribers. Students, military members, veterans and teachers get 50 percent off. Paramount+ is the deal to recommend to readers who want the most content for the least money, because two months at $2.99 covers the whole holiday season.

Peacock: $3 a Month for Three Months

Peacock Select is available for $3 a month for three months with the code PEA4J3K58GUOWW. That is the entry-level tier, and it does not include live sports or Peacock originals, so readers need to know what they are getting. The fuller Peacock deals in other roundups go as low as $0.99 to $1.99 a month for six to twelve months, depending on the tier and the offer. Peacock also has some of the best discount programs in streaming: $7 a month with ads for students, and $8 a month with ads for military members, veterans, teachers and medical and first responders. Peacock and Apple TV are also sold together as a bundle for $18 a month, or $23 a month with ad-free Peacock. For bloggers, Peacock is a useful post to write because the price is so low that the decision is easy for the reader, and easy decisions convert into clicks.

Apple TV: Half Price for Six Months

Apple TV is $6.99 a month for six months, which is half of its usual monthly price, for new and eligible returning subscribers. The offer runs through November 30, so readers have a few days after Black Friday itself to claim it. Apple TV is also available through Prime Video and Roku, and it is bundled with Peacock for $18 a month. The Apple TV deal is the cleanest example of how streaming services use Black Friday. Apple is not discounting a device or a piece of hardware. It is discounting time. Six months is long enough for a subscriber to finish several original series, build a watchlist and get used to opening the app, and by the time the full price returns, cancelling feels like losing something. Bloggers should explain that honestly, because readers respect a guide that tells them how the deal works instead of just shouting about the discount.

Disney+ and Hulu: $11.99 a Month for Six Months

The Disney+ and Hulu bundle is available for $11.99 a month for the first six months, renewing at $12.99 a month after that. The standard ad-supported bundle is $13 a month, or $22 a month without ads. Students can get the Disney+ and Hulu bundle for $6.49 a month with ads. The three-service bundle of Disney+, Hulu and Max costs $22 a month with ads, or $35 a month without ads. There is also a Disney, Hulu and ESPN Select bundle for $12 a month, or $23 without ads. Disney has confirmed bundle subscribers will get a unified experience inside the Disney+ app from December. For families, the Disney+ and Hulu bundle is usually the best value in this whole list, because it covers kids, general entertainment and live sports in a single subscription, and the six-month introductory price makes the first half of the year genuinely cheap.

Hulu: As Low as $0.99 a Month for a Year

Hulu is running one of the deepest discounts in streaming this Black Friday, with offers from $0.99 to $1.99 a month for twelve months, against a normal price of $7.99 a month for the ad-supported plan. A full year at $0.99 a month costs less than a single month at the regular price, which is why the Hulu deal always goes viral in deal roundups. Hulu also offers 25 percent off the monthly ad-supported plan for military members and veterans, and a $7 a month Spotify bundle for students. The catch with the cheapest Hulu offers is that they are for new and returning subscribers, and the price jumps back to the regular rate after the promotional year. Bloggers should put that renewal price in the same sentence as the deal price. A reader who knows the renewal price and still signs up is a happy reader. A reader who discovers it on their bank statement is a reader who never comes back.

Max: $2.99 to $4.99 a Month for Six Months

Max, the home of HBO, is available from $2.99 to $4.99 a month for six months, depending on the tier. Another roundup lists HBO Max at as low as $10.99 a month for twelve months. The Netflix and Max bundle is $13 a month with ads, saving about $7 a month against buying the two separately, and it can be upgraded to ad-free. Max is the service to recommend to readers who care about prestige drama and films rather than volume, and the six-month window is long enough to watch a full season of a flagship show. For bloggers, Max is also the service with the most interesting future, because Skydance plans to offer a Paramount+ and HBO Max bundle in early 2027, and the services will not be combined right away, which means there will be a whole new round of bundle news to cover in the new year.

YouTube TV, Crunchyroll, Starz and the Rest

YouTube TV offered $59.99 a month for two months against a regular $82.99, but that offer ended on November 16: the best streaming deals do not all wait for Black Friday, and a blogger who publishes early catches the deals late publishers miss. Crunchyroll is $3.99 a month for six months, down from its usual $9.99, and that offer runs through November 30. Starz is $2.99 a month for two months. Amazon Prime Video Channels start from 99 cents. Comcast customers can get Peacock with ads, Netflix with ads and Apple TV together for $18 a month, or add HBO Max and Disney+ and Hulu into larger bundles for $22 to $35 a month. And there is one more change coming that bloggers should note now: ad-supported Peacock Premium will be included with YouTube Premium in early 2027. The streaming market is consolidating into bundles, and every bundle announcement is a new post waiting to be written.

The Bundle Math: What a Year of Streaming Actually Costs

The headline price is never the real price. The real price is twelve months. Disney+, Hulu and Max at $22 a month with ads is $264 a year. The Disney+ and Hulu bundle costs about $150 for the first year. Netflix and Max at $13 a month is $156. Stack the short deals and the total beats any old cable bill: Paramount+ at $2.99 for two months, Peacock at $3 for three, Apple TV at $6.99 for six, Hulu at $0.99 to $1.99 for twelve. Each renews at a different time, so cancel each one when its promotion ends and keep only the ones you watch.

Nobody needs eight services at once. Keep two or three and rotate as new seasons arrive. Carrier bundles help too: Comcast customers get Peacock, Netflix and Apple TV for $18 a month, and Verizon offers Netflix and Max for $13 a month with an eligible plan. These quiet bundles are often cheaper than the Black Friday offers, and a thorough post covers both.

Why Streaming Deals Are the Easiest Black Friday Traffic a Tech Blog Can Win

Laptop deals are fought over by every major publication in the world, and TV deals are dominated by the retailers themselves. Streaming deals are different. A page about laptop deals from 2025 is still partly useful in 2026, because many of the same laptops are on sale. A page about Paramount+ at $2.99 a month is useless the moment the offer expires, which means Google has to find fresh pages every November, and fresh pages from smaller blogs get a real chance to rank. A new post with the real prices, published early and updated daily, can reach the first page.

Streaming prices are also easy to verify. A laptop price depends on the model, the configuration, the retailer and the region. A streaming price is a single number on a single checkout page, so a blogger can check every price in this post in under an hour each morning. A page updated this morning outranks a page published last week.

Streaming readers are also the perfect audience for a monetization blog: a person searching for a Paramount+ deal is comfortable paying online, and that is the same person who will read a post about how to monetize a blog. How to Write a Streaming Deals Post That Ranks on Google

Start with the keyword. The main keyword is "black friday streaming deals 2026", and it belongs in the title, in the first paragraph, in at least one heading, and in the image file name. The supporting keywords are the service names with Black Friday attached: "paramount plus black friday", "peacock black friday deal", "apple tv black friday", "disney plus hulu bundle black friday", "hulu black friday deal", "max black friday deal" and "best streaming deals". Give each one its own heading.

Next, answer the question the searcher actually asked. A person who types "paramount plus black friday" wants the price, the length of the offer, and whether it is the Essential or Premium plan. Give them that answer in the first two sentences of the section, then add the detail. A person who types "best streaming deals" wants a comparison, so give them a short list of the three best offers near the top of the post. Google measures whether readers stay on a page or bounce back to the results, and the fastest way to keep them is to answer their question before they have to scroll.

Then, keep the post alive. Publish early, before Black Friday itself, and update it every morning through Cyber Monday on November 30. Each update should change something real: a price, an expiry date, a sold-out offer, a new bundle. A page that changes every day during deal week will be crawled every day, so every improvement is indexed within hours. Keep paragraphs short and prices bold.

Turning Deal-Seekers Into Ad Revenue

The money in a streaming deals post does not come from the deals. It comes from the traffic, and the traffic this week is worth more than the traffic in any other week of the year. Deal-season readers arrive in buying mode: they read carefully, compare prices and stay longer than a normal reader. Every one of those behaviors increases the number of ad impressions a page generates, and an ad network pays for impressions.

The ad formats that work best on a deals post are the ones that sit where the reader's eyes already are. A 300 by 250 banner inside the article catches readers scrolling through the prices. What Is Changing in Streaming in 2027 — and Why Bloggers Should Care

The deals in this post expire in days, but the changes behind them will shape the next year of streaming coverage, and a blogger who understands them now will be ahead of the competition in January. The biggest change is bundling. Skydance plans to offer a Paramount+ and HBO Max bundle in early 2027, and the two services will not be combined right away. Ad-supported Peacock Premium will be included with YouTube Premium in early 2027, which means millions of YouTube Premium subscribers will suddenly have Peacock without paying extra. Disney+ and Hulu bundle subscribers will get a unified experience inside the Disney+ app starting in December, so the two services will finally feel like one product. Each change is a post waiting to be written.

The second change is the return of the annual plan. Paramount+ is pushing its annual plan with 30 percent off through the SAVEANNUAL30 code, and Max is offering twelve-month pricing. The streaming services have learned that a subscriber who pays for a year upfront is a subscriber who does not churn in February, and they are willing to discount deeply to get that commitment. The third change is the student discounts, which last all year: Peacock at $7 a month and the Disney+ and Hulu bundle at $6.49 a month for students compete with the Black Friday deals themselves. Mentioning them gives students a reason to read in March as well as November.

The Mistakes That Kill a Deals Post

Most deals posts fail for the same reasons. The first is publishing a price you never checked. A streaming price that was correct on November 22 can be wrong on November 24, and a reader who clicks through to a checkout page and finds a different number will never trust your blog again. Check the prices every morning during deal week, and say when you checked them. If a deal ends, mark it expired.

The second mistake is hiding the renewal price. Every deal has two prices: the promotional price and the price after it ends. Paramount+ at $2.99 a month for two months is a great deal, but only if the reader knows what month three costs. Hulu at $0.99 a month for twelve months is spectacular, but only if the reader knows the thirteenth month costs $7.99. Always show both prices together.

The third mistake is writing for Google instead of for people. A post stuffed with the keyword will rank for nothing. A post that answers the reader's question in plain language, with the real prices and the real catches, will rank, because Google's job is to show people the page that helps them most. The fourth mistake is publishing once and forgetting the post. Check prices each morning, add new deals, remove expired ones. That work is unglamorous, and it is the whole business.

Black Friday 2026 is the best streaming sale in years: every major service is on sale at once, and the discounts are deep enough to matter. Paramount+ is $2.99 a month for two months. Peacock is $3 a month for three months. Apple TV is half price for six months. The Disney+ and Hulu bundle is $11.99 a month for six months. Hulu is as low as $0.99 a month for a year. Max is $2.99 to $4.99 a month for six months. Crunchyroll is $3.99 a month for six months, and Starz is $2.99 a month for two months. Black Friday falls on November 27, and Cyber Monday follows on November 30, so there is still time to claim every one of these offers, but the best of them will not wait. For readers: pick the services you will actually watch, check the renewal price, and set a reminder to cancel before the promotion ends.
