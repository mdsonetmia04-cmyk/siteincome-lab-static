When Is Walmart Black Friday 2026? Dates, Times and the Three Sale Events

Walmart split Black Friday 2026 into three events, and knowing the calendar is half the game. The first round of deals began on November 12, with Walmart+ members getting first access to a set of hot deal drops. The main event, Black Friday Deals, runs November 23 through November 29. It began on Walmart.com at 12:01 a.m. Eastern on Monday, November 23, and the in-store portion starts at 6 a.m. local time on Friday, November 27. The final round is Cyber Monday, November 30 through December 2. That is three weeks of deals, not one day, and the best prices are online first. Every event in the ad starts on Walmart.com at 12:01 a.m. Eastern, days before the same deals reach stores.

The rhythm matters for shoppers and for bloggers. Walmart+ members get early access to select hot deal drops for the first 24 hours of each event, and then the deals open to everyone. That early window is where the doorbusters live. The AirPods 4 at $89, the Vizio TVs under $200, and the Ninja Slushi at $199 were all flagged as stock-limited, and the deal teams tracking the sale expect the most popular items to sell through before the weekend. If you are shopping, the move is simple: buy online tonight, choose pickup if you want it faster, and do not wait for the store opening on Friday morning to decide.

5 Walmart Black Friday Shopping Tips That Actually Save Money

First, shop online tonight, not in the store on Friday. Every event starts on Walmart.com at 12:01 a.m. Eastern, days before the same deals reach stores, and the best prices sell out online first. The store opening at 6 a.m. on Friday is for the people who missed the online window, not for the people who planned ahead.

Second, check the price at checkout, not just on the listing. Prices are moving during the event, and the ad is a floor. A TV listed at $198 in the morning can be $188 by the evening, or $208 if the stock is nearly gone. The price that matters is the one on the checkout screen.

Third, use the extended return window. Returns run through January 31, 2027, so a gift bought now can be exchanged after Christmas without a fight. That makes Black Friday the safe time to buy a gift you are not sure about, because the return window covers the entire holiday season.

Fourth, buy the bundle, not the console alone. The Switch 2 bundle at $499 includes the Mario Kart World game and three months of Switch Online, which is $50 off the console and the extras on top. The bundle is almost always the better deal, and it is the one that sells out first.

Fifth, compare Walmart against Amazon and Best Buy on anything over $200. The AirPods Pro 3 are $149 at both Walmart and Amazon, but the TVs are cheaper at Walmart and the laptops are cheaper at Best Buy. A two-minute comparison on a $500 purchase can save $50, which is a ten percent return on two minutes of work.

How Tech Blogs Turn Walmart Black Friday Into Ad Revenue

Here is the part most shoppers never see, and it is the reason this blog exists. Walmart Black Friday is the biggest traffic week of the year for tech blogs, and the traffic is worth money because of how display advertising works. A blog earns money when a reader loads a page and sees ads, and the amount it earns depends on how many readers arrive and where they come from. Readers from the United States earn far more per thousand views than readers from anywhere else, because American advertisers pay more to reach American shoppers. A post about Walmart Black Friday deals is written for American shoppers, which means it attracts exactly the audience advertisers pay the most to reach.

The playbook has four moves. First, publish early, because Google rewards the posts that were live when the searches started. Second, put the real prices in the title, because a title with "$89 AirPods 4" earns the click and a title with "amazing deals" does not. Third, update the post when the prices change, because a post that still says $128 when the TV is $148 loses trust, and trust is the only asset a deals blog has. Fourth, cover the sale as a season, not a day, with separate posts for the early deals, the main event, and Cyber Monday, because each one targets a different search moment and each one earns its own traffic.
