OpenAI Just Made ChatGPT 40% More Expensive Overnight — What the GPT-6.2 Billing Change Means for You (Daily AI Update — November 27, 2026)

If you pay for ChatGPT, your subscription got more expensive this week — and OpenAI did not raise the price. On November 26, 2026, OpenAI announced that a billing error at the launch of GPT-6.2 had been undercharging subscribers, and the fix means the same $20 a month now buys noticeably less. Users of GPT-6.2 Astra, the flagship reasoning model, now hit their usage limits about 40% faster. Users of GPT-6.2 Sol hit theirs about 25% faster. The monthly price did not change. The amount of work it covers did. For millions of daily users, that is a price hike in everything but name — and it lands just as OpenAI prepares a bigger December change, when ChatGPT and Codex move to a single shared token allowance. This is your Daily AI Update for Friday, November 27, 2026.

What Actually Happened: The "Billing Error" That Cost You 40%

The story starts on November 17, 2026, when OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol to ChatGPT subscribers worldwide. Astra is the flagship reasoning model, built for hard problems, long documents, and deep analysis. Sol is the workhorse for multi-step agentic tasks — the model that powers long coding sessions and complex workflows in ChatGPT Work and Codex. Users praised the models, and within days GPT-6.2 was the default reasoning engine for paid plans.

Then, on November 26, OpenAI said there had been a mistake. A billing error at launch meant subscribers were receiving discounts they were not supposed to get on GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage. The error was corrected immediately. OpenAI framed it as a fix, not a price change. Users experienced it very differently.

Here is what the correction means in practice. ChatGPT's paid plans do not sell unlimited use. They sell an allowance — a pool of usage that refills on a rolling basis, with limits measured in five-hour windows and weekly caps on top. When that allowance runs out, the model you want stops responding, and you either wait or switch to a weaker model. Before the correction, a Plus subscriber could send somewhere between 5 and 45 local messages to GPT-6.2 Astra in a five-hour window, according to OpenAI's own published estimates from November 14, 2026. After the correction, that same allowance drains about 40% faster for Astra and about 25% faster for Sol. In plain terms, if you used to get ten hard Astra questions out of your allowance, you now get about six. If you used to get eight long Sol coding sessions, you now get about six.

The reaction was swift and angry. Across X, Reddit, and Hacker News, paying subscribers called the move a stealth price hike — a price increase delivered through the meter instead of the price tag. The correction arrived nine days after launch, after users had built their routines around the new models — and in Black Friday week, when every other company in America was cutting prices. When the same $20 buys 40% less of the best model, the price of the best model has gone up 40%.

There is a second, quieter part to this story. Data from AgentDuel, which pits models against each other on real developer tasks, shows GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol now spend far fewer reasoning and output tokens than the models they are matched against. The drops were statistically significant: the chance that Sol's lower reasoning-token use happened by accident was calculated at 0.00013, and Astra's at 0.0064. OpenAI has not confirmed any change to how much the models reason. But if the models are both costing you more per token and spending fewer tokens per answer, subscribers are being squeezed from both directions: less thinking for more money. That suspicion is why this story is not going away.

The December Change: One Token Allowance for ChatGPT and Codex

The billing correction is not the only change coming. OpenAI's support documentation, updated on November 26, 2026, describes a bigger shift that starts in December 2026. From next month, ChatGPT Plus, Pro, and Business subscribers will move to a single usage allowance counted in tokens, shared across ChatGPT and Codex. Today, ChatGPT and Codex have separate limits. From December, they draw from the same pool.

A token is the unit of information a model processes and generates — roughly three quarters of a word in English. It is now what OpenAI meters. Every message you send, every document you upload, every line of code the model writes, and every web page it reads costs tokens from your allowance. The price of your seat stays the same. What changes is how usage is counted, and how much work that seat covers now depends on which models you pick and how hard you push them.

The rollout will start with a small group of users, and OpenAI says most users will not hit resets more often than they do now. "Most users" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. The people who will notice first are the heavy users: developers running long Codex sessions, writers doing deep research, analysts feeding the model hundred-page documents, and small business owners who built ChatGPT into their operations. For them, one shared allowance means a long coding session in the morning can eat the budget for an afternoon of writing. The two tools now share one wallet.

There is one important exception. Instant chat — the fast, lightweight model for quick questions — is excluded from the shared allowance. Quick questions stay cheap. It is the heavy work, the advanced reasoning models and the coding agents, that draws from the shared pool. OpenAI wants the quick, casual use to feel unlimited, while the serious work is metered precisely. The result is a product that feels generous when you ask it what to cook for dinner and expensive when you ask it to rebuild your company's codebase.

What the Plans Cost Now

Free costs $0 with tight limits and no Codex, and Go costs $8 a month for light users. ChatGPT Plus costs $20 a month, billed monthly, with no annual discount. It includes the GPT-6 reasoning models, expanded messages and uploads, image creation, expanded deep research, expanded memory and context, projects, scheduled tasks, custom GPTs, expanded Codex usage, and ChatGPT Work on desktop, web, and mobile. Its reasoning context window is 256,000 tokens, roughly 320 pages of text. Plus is still $20 a month, the price it launched at in February 2023. What has changed is how usage is counted.

ChatGPT Pro comes in three tiers, all billed monthly only, with no annual option: Pro 100 at $100 a month, Pro 200 at $200 a month, and Pro 500 at $500 a month. Pro adds the GPT-6 Astra Pro reasoning tier, much longer Codex and ChatGPT Work sessions, an always-on agent called a dot, unlimited image creation, and a 400,000-token context window, roughly 680 pages. Pro 200 includes $200 a month in API credits. Only Pro 500 includes Ultrafast mode, which runs GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum speed. Pro 100 includes five times the Plus allowance, Pro 200 ten times, and Pro 500 the most.

Business seats cost $20 per user a month when billed annually, and they share the same December token-allowance change as Plus and Pro. Enterprise pricing is custom. On the API side, there is no plan at all — you pay per token, with no included allowance, which makes the API the most transparent way to buy AI work and the easiest way to see what it actually costs.

Third-party measurements from November 2026 found a full week of Plus Codex use worth about $67 at API list rates, a Pro 100 week about $175 to $330, and a Pro 200 week about $650. If you use ChatGPT lightly, Plus at $20 is still a bargain. If you use it hard, the November 26 correction means your $20 now covers roughly a third less of the best models, and the December merge means your coding and your chatting compete for the same budget. The question is no longer whether ChatGPT is worth $20. It is whether $20 still buys the work you actually do.

Why Users Are Calling It a Stealth Price Hike

OpenAI's defenders have a point: the company says the launch pricing was a mistake, and correcting a mistake is not the same as raising a price. The company also points out that Plus is still $20, the price it has been since February 2023, while the models have become dramatically more capable. GPT-6.2 Astra is a far stronger model than anything Plus subscribers had in 2023. From that angle, $20 still buys more intelligence than it ever has.

But subscribers have a stronger point. Nobody experiences a price tag. They experience a limit. When the model you need stops answering at 2 p.m. instead of 5 p.m., that is a price increase in the only currency that matters: the work you got done. The fact that the price tag did not change is exactly why people call it stealthy. A company that raises its price has to announce it and risk losing customers. A company that quietly makes the meter run 40% faster gets the same effect without the announcement. That is why the phrase "stealth price hike" stuck within hours.

The history makes the suspicion worse. On September 29, 2026, OpenAI announced a cut to Pro allowances, then on October 29 it canceled the cut and doubled the Work and Codex allowance on Pro 100 and Pro 500 instead. Subscribers have now watched OpenAI move the meter in both directions within two months, with little warning. When a company changes what your money buys without telling you in advance, trust erodes — even if each individual change has a reasonable explanation.

The Rest of Today's AI News: DeepL, Google, Neuralink, and More

DeepL launched Aure v2, a new speech-to-speech translation model, at its first-ever event in Tokyo on November 26. Aure v2 translates spoken words directly into speech in another language within a single model, instead of chaining speech recognition, translation, and speech synthesis together. When translating from English to Japanese, it reportedly made 32% fewer errors than Google Gemini 3.5 Live Translate. For anyone who has struggled through a conversation in a language they do not speak, that number matters. The model is available immediately.

Google made its Workspace MCP servers generally available on November 24. MCP, short for Model Context Protocol, lets AI agents read and act on business information. From February 2027, AI agents that touch Workspace services will have to go through Google's MCP servers, and direct API calls will be throttled to Standard-tier quotas no matter what you are paying. Paid high-volume MCP usage starts in April 2027. There is one catch that has developers talking: Google's MCP servers cannot send Gmail. An agent can read your email, summarize it, and draft replies — but it cannot send them. For businesses hoping to automate their inboxes, that is a deliberate hard limit.

DeepMind, Google's AI research lab, has given AI agents autonomous control over a slice of its own compute fleet for running Gemini experiments. The lab's vice president of research, Koray Kavukcuoglu, discussed the move on the DeepMind podcast, and he stressed that recursive self-improvement — AI improving itself without human help — is still distant. But the direction is striking. The world's most advanced AI lab now trusts AI agents to decide how to spend some of its computing power.

Anthropic has not confirmed the model's existence. At the same time, the company revealed that Claude helped rebuild its own API, as Anthropic migrated its software from Python to Rust, cutting CPU usage by 70%.

Neuralink received FDA approval for the LINK pivotal study of its brain implant in ALS, the company announced. Neuralink calls it the first US pivotal trial of an implantable brain-computer interface. Enrollment is open, and participant numbers were not given. For people living with ALS, a brain implant that restores communication is a lifeline.

Forty-two state attorneys general moved to block Character.AI from auctioning off users' chat logs in bankruptcy proceedings. And OpenAI released nine more mathematics papers, claiming they surpass the VP versus VNP proof Anthropic published a day earlier. Peer review has not yet been conducted on either claim, and mathematicians are watching closely.

Two more stories deserve a mention. Tencent open-sourced DFly2, a tool that accelerates AI responses by predicting multiple pieces of text before the main model verifies them, reporting up to 81% higher processing throughput for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at a 64,000-token context length. And Los Angeles Unified, the largest US school district, disabled Google AI Overviews on school devices — a fight over AI summaries in search that is only beginning.

What This Means for Bloggers and Creators

What does the ChatGPT billing change mean for people who use AI to write, publish, and earn? The short answer is that AI-assisted content just got more expensive to produce at scale. If you use ChatGPT to draft posts, research topics, or generate ideas, your $20 now buys about a third less of the best model. That is a real cost, and it lands on the same creators who are already watching AI search take traffic from their blogs — a trend we covered earlier this month when Google admitted AI Mode ads pay less than traditional search ads.

The longer answer is more hopeful. The same week OpenAI tightened its meter, DeepL shipped cheaper multilingual translation, Google opened Workspace to agents, and Tencent open-sourced a tool that makes AI responses up to 81% faster. The cost of AI is not rising everywhere. It is rising for the most popular product, while falling or staying flat for everything around it. Creators who learn to use the right tool for each job — ChatGPT for drafting, Claude for coding, DeepL for translation, Gemini for research — will spend less than creators who pay one company for everything. That is the real lesson of November 27: the AI market is no longer one product. It is an ecosystem, and the people who shop around win.

The Bottom Line

OpenAI did not raise the price of ChatGPT this week. It did something quieter and, for heavy users, more expensive: it fixed a billing error that made GPT-6.2 Astra drain its usage allowance 40% faster and Sol 25% faster, and it confirmed that from December, ChatGPT and Codex will share a single token allowance. The same $20 still buys a subscription. It no longer buys the same amount of work. For most people, Plus remains a bargain. For heavy users, the message is clear — use the right model for each job, compare the alternatives, and watch what OpenAI does next, because the company has reversed course on usage limits before.
