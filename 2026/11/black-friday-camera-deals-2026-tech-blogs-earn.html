The Nikon Z8 is $1,100 off right now. Not $100. Not a bundle trick with a bag and a memory card you did not want. One thousand one hundred dollars off a 45.7-megapixel stacked-sensor flagship that shoots 8K video, down to $3,196.95 from $4,296.95 at Amazon, B&H and Adorama. The Sony a7 IV, the best-selling full-frame hybrid camera of the last four years, is $500 off at $1,998. Black Friday 2026 falls on November 27, but the camera deals did not wait for the calendar — and for photographers who have been watching prices all year, this is the cheapest week to buy a serious camera since last Black Friday.

Camera gear is expensive, and that is exactly why Black Friday camera coverage earns so well. A reader comparing laptop deals might spend $600. A reader comparing mirrorless cameras is deciding between $1,998 and $3,197 bodies, and then buying lenses, cards, batteries and bags on top. Every one of those decisions is a search query, a comparison article and an ad impression waiting to happen.

Nikon Z8: $1,100 Off the Flagship in a Smaller Body

The Nikon Z8 is the deal of this Black Friday, and it is not close. At $3,196.95 it is, for practical purposes, a Nikon Z9 in a smaller, lighter body. The 45.7-megapixel stacked CMOS sensor reads out fast enough to run a fully electronic shutter with no blackout at 20 frames per second in RAW. It records 8K60 in N-RAW and 4K at up to 120 frames per second. In-body stabilization is rated at 5.5 stops, and the body weighs 910 grams.

What makes the Z8 special is not any single specification. It is that Nikon removed the mechanical shutter entirely and built a camera that never has to compromise between stills and video. Wildlife photographers get blackout-free bursts with subject detection that locks onto birds, animals and vehicles. Wedding photographers get a silent camera that shoots 45-megapixel files all day. Video shooters get internal 8K recording that would have cost five figures a few years ago.

The $1,100 saving comes from Nikon's fall savings program, which launched with what Nikon Rumors called the lowest prices of the year so far, with discounts of up to $2,000 across the range. The Z8 discount is available at Amazon, B&H and Adorama, and the same $1,100 cut applies to the Z8 bundled with the 24-120mm f/4 lens. Imaging Resource's live deal tracker lists the Z8 body at $3,196.95 against a $4,296.95 list price, a 26 percent saving, ending Sunday, November 29 at 11:59 p.m. ET.

Nikon Z6 III: $700 Off the Best All-Rounder Nikon Makes

If the Z8 is the headline, the Nikon Z6 III at $1,996.95 is the deal most people should actually buy. It is $700 off its $2,696.95 list price, the same 26 percent discount as the Z8, and it lands at a price where it competes with cameras a full class below it. The Z6 III pairs a 24.5-megapixel partially stacked sensor with 6K oversampled video, fast autofocus and the same excellent ergonomics as the Z8, in a body that costs $1,200 less than its big brother this week.

The partially stacked sensor is the clever part. It reads out much faster than a conventional sensor, which means less rolling shutter in video and faster bursts in stills, without the cost of a fully stacked chip. For hybrid shooters — the photographers who shoot a wedding in the morning and a short film in the afternoon — the Z6 III is the sweet spot of the entire Nikon lineup, and the price leaves room in the budget for a lens.

At $1,996.95 the Z6 III sits almost exactly at the same price as the Sony a7 IV, which sets up the most interesting comparison of this Black Friday. Nikon gives you the faster sensor readout and better video oversampling; Sony gives you the deeper lens catalog. There is no wrong answer, and this week there is no meaningful price difference either. Choose by the lenses you want to own, not by the body.

Sony a7 IV: $500 Off the Camera Everyone Recommends

The Sony a7 IV at $1,998 is the deal that will sell the most cameras this Black Friday. It is $500 off its $2,498 list price, a 20 percent saving, and it is available at Amazon, B&H and Adorama as a body-only purchase. Sony is also running a $50 cashback campaign on eligible Alpha cameras and lenses bought through December 31, 2026, which brings the effective cost down to $1,948 for buyers who claim it.

The a7 IV earns its reputation the boring way: by being good at everything. The 33-megapixel sensor is the right resolution for almost everyone — large enough to crop, small enough that files stay manageable. It records 4K video at up to 60 frames per second, with the autofocus system that made Sony famous for subject tracking. Dual card slots and a deep E-mount lens catalog make it the safest recommendation in photography.

There is one caveat worth stating plainly. The a7 IV has a successor now — the Sony a7 V has arrived — and the a7 IV has appeared at $1,998 several times since then, so this is not a once-in-history price. What it is, is a strong price on a proven camera at the exact moment gift buyers and upgraders are shopping hardest. If you need a camera now, buy it now; the a7 V costs considerably more, so the a7 IV remains the value pick in Sony's full-frame range.

Make sure you are buying the body-only version. The body-only SKU is $1,998; the kit with the 28-70mm lens and the store bundle versions cost more, and some retailers display a $2,198 price for a different configuration. Check that the listing says body only, no lens, before you check out.

Sony a7 III: $600 Off the Budget Full-Frame King

The Sony a7 III at $1,398 is the cheapest way into full-frame photography from a major brand this Black Friday, and it is $600 off its $1,998 list price — a 30 percent discount, the largest percentage cut among the headline bodies. The a7 III is an older camera, succeeded first by the a7 IV and now by the a7 V, but it remains a capable machine: a 24-megapixel sensor, high-quality stills, 4K video at 30 frames per second, and access to the same enormous E-mount lens system as the newer bodies.

This is the camera for a specific buyer: someone moving up from a phone or a crop-sensor camera who wants full-frame image quality and is willing to trade the newest autofocus and video features for $600 in savings. It is also a smart second body for working photographers who already own an a7 IV and want a backup that shares batteries and lenses. Do not buy it if you shoot fast action or demanding video — the autofocus and the 4K30 ceiling will frustrate you, and the a7 IV is worth the extra $600 for that work.

Watch the listings carefully here. Some retailers show the a7 III kit with a lens at $1,598, down from $2,199.99, which is a different product from the $1,398 body. Compare body prices with body prices and kit prices with kit prices.

Canon EOS R5: $700 Off, With One Footnote

Canon shooters are not left out. The Canon EOS R5 is $700 off this week, at $2,599 against a $3,299 list price, through Canon's holiday rebate program, which runs from November 2 to December 6. Multiple deal trackers confirm the $700 figure, and B&H's holiday sale lists the same cut.

The footnote is that not every tracker agrees on the street price. At least one roundup lists the R5 at $2,999, which suggests some retailers are applying the rebate differently or showing different configurations. If you buy the R5, confirm the final price at checkout rather than trusting a headline, and buy from an authorized dealer so the rebate and warranty both apply. The R5 itself remains a superb camera — 45 megapixels, 8K video, best-in-class autofocus — and at $2,599 it is the cheapest it has ever been from authorized retailers.

Nikon Z9: The Flagship Is $1,100 Off Too

The Nikon Z9, the camera the Z8 is based on, is also $1,100 off this Black Friday at Amazon, B&H and Adorama. The Z9 is the bigger, heavier brother: an integrated vertical grip, even longer battery life, and the same 45.7-megapixel stacked sensor with no mechanical shutter. For sports and wildlife professionals who shoot all day with long telephoto lenses, the Z9's balance and battery life justify the larger body. For everyone else, the Z8 delivers the same image quality for less money and less weight.

Lenses, Drones and Action Cameras: Where the Real Savings Hide

Camera bodies get the headlines, but lenses are where Black Friday saves photographers the most money over time. Nikon is discounting NIKKOR Z glass alongside the bodies, with more than $800 off the 14-24mm f/2.8 S wide-angle zoom according to one tracker. Sony's sale covers 143 items including FE lenses, and the $50 cashback campaign applies to lenses as well as bodies. A lens bought at 20 percent off is a lens you will use for a decade.

The smart move is to buy the lens you have been renting. If you rent a 70-200mm three times a year, the Black Friday price often pays for itself within two years of ownership. If you have never rented it, do not buy it just because it is discounted — a cheap lens you never use is still money wasted. The same logic applies to the rest of the sale: DJI drones, Insta360 cameras and GoPro action cameras are all discounted this week, with the GoPro MAX2 at $280 at Amazon, but only buy the action camera if you have a trip or a project that needs it.

Which Camera Should You Actually Buy?

Here is the short version, by photographer. If you shoot wildlife or sports and want the best camera in this sale, buy the Nikon Z8 at $3,196.95. If you shoot a bit of everything and want the best all-rounder for the money, buy the Nikon Z6 III at $1,996.95 or the Sony a7 IV at $1,998 — pick Nikon for video readout and ergonomics, Sony for the lens catalog. If your budget is tight and your subjects move slowly, buy the Sony a7 III at $1,398 and spend the savings on a lens. If you shoot Canon already, the EOS R5 at $2,599 is the upgrade that will last you five years.

Three questions settle most decisions. First, what lenses do you already own? Switching systems costs far more than any body discount saves. Second, do you shoot more stills or more video? The Z6 III and a7 IV are the hybrid picks; the Z8 is the camera for people who need both at the highest level. Third, how much resolution do you actually need? Forty-five megapixels is wonderful for cropping wildlife shots and printing large, and it is a burden for everyone else — bigger files, slower editing, more storage.

Why Camera Week Is the Best Week for Tech Blogs

This is a blog about making money from a website, so here is the part most deal roundups never explain: why a week of camera discounts is worth so much to the blogs covering it. Camera shoppers are the highest-intent readers in tech. They arrive from Google with a specific question — "Nikon Z8 Black Friday deal", "Sony a7 IV $500 off, is it worth it" — and they are ready to spend thousands of dollars. A reader like that is worth more to an advertiser than a hundred casual scrollers, and the ad rates reflect it.

Camera content also earns in ways a generic deals post does not. Readers compare bodies, which means they read long articles instead of bouncing from a price list. They return to the same article as prices change, which means repeat visits. They click through to related coverage — laptop deals for editing and software deals for Lightroom and Photoshop. Each of those clicks is another page view and another ad impression. If you run a tech blog, the camera post is not just a post. It is the top of a funnel that leads to your Black Friday laptop deals guide, and from there to your Black Friday software deals guide.

The monetization works in layers. Display advertising pays on every impression, and camera deal pages earn premium rates because the audience is affluent and ready to buy. Affiliate links pay when a reader buys the camera, and a single Z8 sale at $3,196.95 generates more affiliate commission than a hundred clicks on a cheap accessory. Sponsored placements pay when brands want their lens or accessory featured alongside the bodies. And the email list grows fastest during deal weeks, because readers who want price-drop alerts will hand over an email address without hesitation.

How to Write Camera Deal Coverage That Actually Ranks

If you are a blogger reading this to learn the craft rather than to buy a camera, here is what works. First, verify every price yourself, from at least two sources, on the day you publish. Readers and Google both punish stale prices, and a deal post with a wrong price is worse than no post at all. The prices in this article were checked against Imaging Resource, DIYPhotography, PetaPixel, The Gadgeteer and Camera Times on November 24, and the ones that conflicted — Canon's R5 pricing — are flagged as conflicting rather than presented as certain.

Second, answer the question the searcher actually asked. Someone searching for the Z8 deal wants to know the price, whether it is real, and whether the camera is worth it. They do not want three paragraphs of Black Friday history. Put the price in the first paragraph, the specifications in the second section, and the buying advice where it can be found in seconds.

Third, update the post. A camera deal article is a living document during sale week: prices change, stock sells out, new discounts appear. The blogs that update their posts daily during Black Friday week keep their rankings; the ones that publish and forget slide down the results as their information goes stale.

Five Mistakes That Waste Money in the Camera Sale

The first mistake is buying the most expensive body when a cheaper one does the job. The Z8 is a wonderful camera, and most photographers will never use what makes it special. The Z6 III or the a7 IV will produce identical images for portraits, landscapes, travel and family photography, and the $1,200 saved buys a lens that changes your photography far more than a stacked sensor does. The second mistake is forgetting the total cost: a $1,998 body is not a $1,998 purchase once you add a lens, a memory card, a spare battery and a bag, so budget the system, not the body. The third mistake is buying grey-market gear to save a little more — if the price is far below every authorized dealer, the warranty is probably not valid, and during Black Friday the authorized dealers match each other anyway. The fourth mistake is buying a kit lens you will never use because it looks like a bargain; buy the body, then buy the lens you chose deliberately. The fifth mistake is waiting for a better price that never comes. The Z8 at $1,100 off and the a7 IV at $1,998 are the prices the deal trackers have been waiting for all year, and stock is the real risk this week: a sold-out deal is not a deal at all.

What Happens After Black Friday

Cyber Monday, November 30, is the last big day of the sale, and most camera deals either continue through it or return for a final push. Nikon's savings end on November 29, so the Z8 and Z6 III prices are not guaranteed to survive into Monday. Sony's sale also ends November 29, though the $50 cashback runs through December 31. Canon's rebate runs to December 6, which gives Canon shoppers a longer window.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 is the best camera sale in years, and the two deals that define it are simple: the Nikon Z8 at $3,196.95, saving $1,100 on a flagship that does everything, and the Sony a7 IV at $1,998, saving $500 on the most recommended full-frame camera on the market. The Z6 III at $1,996.95 is the smartest buy for most people, the a7 III at $1,398 is the cheapest full-frame entry point, and the Canon R5 at $2,599 is the Canon shooter's upgrade. Deals run through November 29 for Nikon and Sony, Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday is November 30, so the window is short and the stock is finite. Buy the camera that fits the photography you actually do, budget for the lens and the cards, and buy from an authorized dealer.
