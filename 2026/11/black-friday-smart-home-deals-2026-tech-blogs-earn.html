Black Friday Smart Home Deals 2026: How Tech Blogs Turn the Year's Biggest Smart Home Sale Into Ad Revenue

Black Friday 2026 falls on November 27, and the smart home deals are already live. Amazon kicked off its Black Friday Week on November 19, running through Cyber Monday on November 30, and Best Buy launched its main Black Friday event the same day with ten days of deals and doorbusters. The early prices are genuinely strong: the Ring Battery Doorbell 2K is selling for $39.99 against a $99.99 comparison price, the new Google Home Speaker is down to $79.99 from $99.99, and the Ring Indoor Cam 2K is just $24.99. For shoppers, this is the cheapest week of the year to build a smart home. For tech bloggers, it is one of the highest-traffic weeks of the year — and one of the best weeks to earn ad revenue from deal-hunting readers.

Smart home gear is a perfect Black Friday category for publishers. The products are cheap enough that readers buy on impulse, expensive enough that the savings feel real, and confusing enough that people search for guidance before they buy. A reader deciding on a $25 smart plug needs to know which doorbell works with the chime they already own, whether the camera needs a subscription, and whether the $50 bundle beats buying the pieces separately. That is the gap a good deal post fills.

This guide does two things. First, it walks through the best Black Friday smart home deals of 2026 that are actually live right now, with the real prices and the retailers selling them. Second, it explains exactly how tech blogs turn this one week of deal traffic into serious ad revenue.

Why Smart Home Deals Are the Biggest Opportunity of Black Friday 2026

Every Black Friday, a few product categories dominate the search results. Televisions and laptops get the headlines, but smart home gear quietly generates some of the highest purchase intent of the entire event. The first reason is price. Most smart home devices cost between $20 and $150, which puts them in the sweet spot where a 40 to 60 percent discount turns a "maybe later" into a "buy now."

The second reason is the ecosystem effect. Smart home products are rarely bought one at a time. A reader who buys a Ring doorbell at $39.99 will soon want a Ring Chime, an extra battery, and maybe an indoor camera to match. A reader who buys an Echo Dot for $24 will start looking at smart plugs, smart bulbs, and a smart display.

The third reason is confusion. Will this doorbell work with my existing chime? Does this camera record without a monthly subscription? Is the new Google Home Speaker better than the Echo I already have? Every one of those questions is a search query, and every search query is a chance for a well-written post to rank. The blogs that win Black Friday answer the questions people type into Google the night before the sale, and in 2026 the deals run for two weeks, so blogs that publish early and update daily capture every wave.

The 10 Best Black Friday Smart Home Deals Live Right Now

The prices below were reported by deal trackers and retailers between November 17 and November 23, 2026. Prices change quickly during Black Friday week, and some offers sell out, so treat these as the prices to beat and always check the current price before you buy.

1. Ring Battery Doorbell 2K — $39.99 (was $99.99)

This is the single best value smart home deal of Black Friday 2026. The Ring Battery Doorbell 2K was listed at $39.99 at Ring, Target, and Home Depot, down from a $99.99 comparison price — a 60 percent discount on the doorbell most American homes will buy this year. It records in 2K resolution, offers a 140-degree head-to-toe view, and works on battery power or wired to an existing doorbell circuit, so it fits almost any front door without an electrician. The one warning to give readers is the subscription question. Ring's cameras record motion events to the cloud only with a Ring Protect plan, so a reader who wants to review footage later needs to budget for that.

2. Ring Indoor Cam 2K — $24.99

The Ring Indoor Cam 2K was listed at $24.99 at Ring and Best Buy, and it is the cheapest way to add a camera to a room you care about: a nursery, a living room, a home office, or a pet's favorite corner. At this price, many shoppers buy two or three. The camera records in 2K and pairs with the Ring app, so a household that already owns one Ring device gets a single app for everything. The bundle angle matters here too. Ring listed a bundle of the Battery Doorbell and the Indoor Cam together at $50, against a combined regular price of $150.

3. Google Home Speaker — $79.99 (was $99.99)

Google's newest smart speaker was cut to $79.99 at Best Buy and Walmart during the early Black Friday sales, down from $99.99. This is more than a basic Bluetooth speaker. It supports Gemini for Home, Google's AI assistant, delivers 360-degree sound, and works as a Matter hub with a Thread border router, which means it can control the new generation of smart home devices that speak the Matter standard. The Google Home Speaker is the most interesting deal for readers who already live in Google's world — Android phones, Nest cameras, Google TV.

4. Ring Wired Doorbell 2K — $44.99 (was $79.99)

For homes that already have doorbell wiring, the Ring Wired Doorbell 2K at $44.99 is the cleaner option. Because it is wired it never needs its battery charged. The trade-off is installation: it needs existing wiring and a compatible transformer, so it is not the right pick for a renter or a home without a wired doorbell.

5. Echo Show 16 and the New Echo Lineup

Amazon put its newest Echo devices in its featured Black Friday discount group this year, including the Echo Show 16, the Echo Time bedside smart alarm clock, and the Ring Pan-Tilt Indoor Cam 2K. The Echo Show 16 is the biggest Alexa screen Amazon has made, with a 16-inch full high-definition display, Fire TV built in, and a 13-megapixel camera with night vision. The older Echo devices follow a reliable pattern. The Echo Dot (5th generation) has a regular price of $49.99 and historically falls to between $18 and $25 on Black Friday. The Echo Show 8, regularly $149.99, historically falls to between $75 and $90. The Echo Pop, regularly $39.99, falls to between $15 and $22.

6. Blink Mini 2K+ and Blink Battery Doorbell 2K+

Amazon's own Blink cameras are the budget champions of Black Friday 2026. The Blink Mini 2K+ indoor security camera was listed at $16.99, down from $39.99, and the Blink Battery Doorbell 2K+ was listed between $38.99 and $44.99, down from as much as $99.99. At $16.99, the Mini 2K+ is cheaper than a takeaway meal, and it is the camera most likely to end up in a stocking. Blink cameras work with Alexa, so they fit naturally into an Amazon-centered home.

7. Ring Outdoor Cam 2K — $50 (was $100)

The Ring Outdoor Cam 2K was listed at $50, down from $100, and it covers the part of the home the doorbell cannot see: the driveway, the backyard, the side gate. It records in 2K and is designed for outdoor use, which makes it the natural second purchase for anyone who bought the doorbell. Deal trackers also flagged the Ring Floodlight Cam 2K, with 2,000 lumens of light and hardwired power, in the $50 to $100 range during the Ring sale.

8. Google Nest Learning Thermostat (4th Gen)

The Google Nest Learning Thermostat (4th generation) was listed in the $200 to $300 range in Best Buy's Black Friday sale, and it is the smart home purchase with the longest payback. A learning thermostat adjusts the heating and cooling schedule on its own, and it ships with a temperature sensor and Matter support. The saving on the device is only part of the story. The real saving is on the energy bill, month after month, which is why this is the product to recommend to readers who ask "is it actually worth it?"

9. Smart Plugs, Smart Lighting, and the Under-$25 Starters

The cheapest smart home deals of Black Friday 2026 are also the most useful for first-time buyers. Matter-compatible smart plugs were selling for less than $25, with a three-pack of Tapo P125M plugs listed at $24.99. The Amazon Echo Dot (5th generation) bundled with a TP-Link smart plug was listed at $24. The Amazon Echo Spot, a compact Alexa device with a display, was listed at $49.99 against a $79.99 regular price. These are the gateway products: a plug that turns a lamp on by voice, a bulb that changes color, a speaker that answers questions.

10. SimpliSafe and the Security System Deals

SimpliSafe is offering 70 percent off new systems with professional monitoring for Black Friday 2026, plus a discounted outdoor camera. A full security system is a different kind of purchase from a doorbell: it is a subscription, a commitment, and a decision that usually involves the whole household. The 70 percent discount applies to new systems with professional monitoring, so the reader needs to understand what the monthly monitoring costs after the discount before they buy.

Smart Home Price Floors: How to Tell a Real Deal From a Fake One

Not every Black Friday price is a real discount. The defense is simple: memorize the price floors. A price floor is the lowest price a product has realistically sold for on past Black Fridays, and it tells you instantly whether today's deal is worth taking. Here are the floors that matter for smart home gear in 2026, based on historical Black Friday pricing across Amazon, Best Buy, Walmart, and Target.

Smart speakers: the Echo Dot (5th generation) has a regular price of $49.99 and a real Black Friday floor of $18 to $25. The Echo Pop, regularly $39.99, falls to $15 to $22. The Google Nest Mini, regularly $49, falls to $20 to $30. The Apple HomePod mini, regularly $99, rarely falls below $79. Smart displays: the Echo Show 5, regularly $89.99, falls to $40 to $55. The Echo Show 8, regularly $149.99, falls to $75 to $90. The Google Nest Hub (2nd generation), regularly $99.99, falls to $50 to $65. Video doorbells: the Ring Battery Doorbell, regularly $99.99, falls to $50 to $65 in a normal year — and the $39.99 price in 2026 beats that floor, which is why it leads this guide. Security cameras: the Wyze Cam v4, regularly $35.99, falls to $20 to $28. Smart thermostats: the Amazon Smart Thermostat, regularly $79.99, falls to $40 to $55.

The rule for readers is straightforward.

Where to Shop: Amazon, Best Buy, Walmart, and Target Compared

Four retailers carry almost every smart home deal worth writing about, and they behave differently enough that the comparison is worth making. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs from November 19 through Cyber Monday, November 30, and it is the best place to start comparing prices. Its own devices — Ring, Blink, Echo — get the deepest cuts.

Best Buy is the best destination for big-ticket smart home gear and for Google products. Its main Black Friday event began on November 19 with ten days of deals and doorbusters, and it cut the Google Home Speaker to $79.99 early in November. Walmart's next wave of deals was scheduled for November 23, and it competes hardest on the products everyone wants: the Google Home Speaker at $79.99 was listed at Walmart as well as Best Buy, and the Ring Battery Doorbell 2K at $39.99 appeared at multiple retailers at once.

The practical advice for readers is to check at least two retailers before buying anything over $50. The practical advice for bloggers is to say exactly that, and to update the post every morning. A deal post that is updated at 8 a.m., noon, and 6 p.m. becomes the reader's homepage for the week.

The Hidden Cost Nobody Puts in the Headline: Subscriptions

Every smart home deal post that only lists the hardware price is lying by omission. Most video doorbells and cameras need a subscription for cloud recording, and that subscription is a monthly cost that lasts for years. Ring's cameras record motion events to the cloud only with a Ring Protect plan. Google's Nest cameras have their own subscription. Blink offers local storage on some devices, which is one reason its cameras are so popular with budget buyers. A $39.99 doorbell that needs a monthly plan is not a $39.99 purchase. It is a $39.99 purchase plus a subscription, and the honest post says so.

A blog that explains the subscription cost next to the hardware price earns the reader's trust, and trust is the asset that matters most in deal blogging. The ecosystem is the other hidden cost. A Ring doorbell works best with other Ring devices. A Nest thermostat works best with other Nest devices. An Echo works best with Alexa-compatible plugs and bulbs.

How Tech Blogs Turn Smart Home Deal Week Into Ad Revenue

Now the part that matters for publishers: how does a blog actually earn money from a week of smart home deals? The answer has four parts — search traffic, page layout, ad formats, and updates — and each one is simple on its own. Together they are the difference between a post that earns a few dollars and a post that earns hundreds.

Advertisement

Search traffic comes first, because without readers there is nothing to monetize. Smart home deal searches follow a predictable pattern every November. In early November, readers search for guides: "best smart home deals," "Ring doorbell Black Friday price," "Google Home Speaker deal." In the week of the sale, they search for prices: "Ring doorbell $39.99," "Echo Dot Black Friday 2026," "best smart home deals under $50."

The keyword strategy is equally practical. Target the product plus the price: "Ring Battery Doorbell 2K $39.99" is a search with clear intent, and it is far easier to rank for than "Black Friday deals."

Page layout is the second part, and it is where most small blogs lose money they have already earned. A deal post should put the best deal at the top, above the fold, with the price, the old price, and the retailer named in the first screen. Readers who came from search will leave in seconds if they have to scroll through five paragraphs of introduction to find the price. The structure should serve the impatient reader first and the curious reader second: a short summary of the best deals at the top, then the detailed sections for the readers who want them.

The third part is ad formats. Deal traffic is high-volume and low-patience, so the formats that work are the ones that do not interrupt the reader. A 728x90 leaderboard at the top of the post and a 300x250 rectangle inside the article earn steadily through deal week, because they sit where the reader's eyes already are. A popunder can earn well during high-traffic days, but it should be used carefully: a reader who is annoyed will not come back tomorrow, and deal week lasts for two weeks.

The fourth part is updates, and it is the part that separates the blogs that earn from the blogs that merely publish. A deal post is not finished when it is published. It is finished when the sale ends. That means checking prices every morning, marking sold-out deals as sold out, adding new deals as they appear, and changing the date in the post so readers and search engines can see it is current.

There is one more revenue lever that many small blogs miss: the internal link. A reader who lands on a smart home deal post is a reader who is interested in deal season in general. Link them to your other deal posts, and a single visitor becomes three or four page views, each carrying ads. What to Buy First If You Are Starting From Zero

If you have never owned a smart home device, the choices above can feel overwhelming, so here is the simple version. Start with a smart speaker and two smart plugs, and spend under $50 in total. Add a video doorbell next, because it is the device that changes daily life the most: you will know who is at the door from anywhere in the world.

Buy the speaker and the plugs from the same ecosystem, because that decision shapes everything that follows. If you use an Android phone and Google services, buy the Google Home Speaker. If you shop on Amazon and use Alexa, buy an Echo. If you use an iPhone and Apple services, buy a HomePod mini — and accept that it will rarely drop below $79.

Finally, remember the calendar. Black Friday is November 27, and Cyber Monday follows on November 30, and Amazon's Black Friday Week runs through November 30, so there is still time to claim every one of these offers. If you see the Ring Battery Doorbell 2K at $39.99, buy it. If you see the Google Home Speaker at $79.99, buy it. If you see a bundle that includes two things you actually want, buy the bundle.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 is the best week of the year to build a smart home, and the prices prove it. The Ring Battery Doorbell 2K at $39.99 is the deal of the season, the Google Home Speaker at $79.99 is the best way into Google's smart home, and the Blink cameras at $16.99 are the cheapest way to start. The smart move is to buy the doorbell first, add a speaker and a couple of plugs, and remember the subscription cost before you check out. For bloggers, the lesson is just as clear: publish early, lead with the price, answer the questions retailers will not answer, update every morning, and link your deal posts to each other so one visitor becomes many page views. The sale ends on November 30. The audience you earn this week does not have to.
