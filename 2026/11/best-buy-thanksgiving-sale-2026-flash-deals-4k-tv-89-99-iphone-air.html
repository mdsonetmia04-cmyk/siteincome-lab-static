The 2026 Sale Calendar: Every Date That Matters

One of the biggest mistakes shoppers make is treating Black Friday as a single day. In 2026 it is a two-week season, and the dates matter because the best price on any given product may appear early and disappear before Friday. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs from November 19 through November 30. Best Buy's Black Friday sale runs from November 19 through November 28, with doorbusters refreshing through the weekend and the Cyber Monday event starting after Saturday. Target's Black Friday sale runs from November 22 through November 29, with up to 50 percent off thousands of gifts. Walmart's online event started November 23. CNN Underscored's team has rounded up 140 deals they consider genuinely worth shopping, updated daily through the weekend.

The pattern is clear: the retailers who opened earliest have the deepest early prices, and the doorbusters rotate. Friday brings a fresh set, Saturday clears the remaining stock, and Cyber Monday on November 30 is the last chance for the electronics deals that did not sell out.

How to Tell a Real Deal From a Fake One

Not every Black Friday price is a real discount. Some retailers raise a price in October and cut it back in November, so the "sale" price is the same as the price you would have paid in September. Here is how to check any deal in under a minute, before you spend a dollar.

First, check the price history. Free tools like CamelCamelCamel for Amazon and the price-history graphs on deal sites show what a product actually sold for over the last six months. If the "was" price never existed, the discount is fake. The AirPods Pro 3 at $149 is a real deal because they launched at $249 and have never been below $199 before this week. The Insignia TV at $89.99 is real because the same model was $179.99 in October.

Advertisement

Second, compare the same product across at least two stores. A genuine Black Friday price is usually matched by a competitor — the AirPods Pro 3 are $149 at both Amazon and Best Buy. When only one store claims a discount and nobody else is close, be suspicious. Third, watch for the bundle trick: some "deals" include a gift card or a free accessory you did not ask for, and the headline price only looks good because the bundle hides the real cost.

Fourth, check the model number: retailers sometimes create special holiday models that look identical to the regular version but use cheaper parts. The deals in this post are all standard retail models. Finally, remember the return policy — Best Buy's holiday return window is longer than usual. A deal you cannot return is not a deal.

Why Thanksgiving Day Is a Gold Mine for Tech Blogs

Here is the part most people miss: Thanksgiving Day itself is one of the most profitable days of the year for a tech blog, and it has almost nothing to do with the deals. It has everything to do with attention. Millions of Americans are sitting on a couch after dinner, phone in hand, with hours to kill before Friday. They are not working, they are not commuting, and they are actively looking for something to read and something to buy. Google searches for "Best Buy Thanksgiving sale" and "Black Friday deals" spike on Thursday evening, and the blogs that published early in the day capture that traffic.

A reader who clicks a deal post on Thanksgiving evening is a buyer, not a browser. They have a budget, a gift list, and a deadline — the flash sale ends at midnight. That intent is exactly what advertisers pay for: a thousand pageviews from Thanksgiving deal hunters are worth more than ten thousand from casual readers on a random Tuesday.

There is a second reason Thanksgiving works for publishers: competition is lower than on Black Friday itself. Every deal blog in America publishes on Friday. Far fewer publish a complete, verified deal guide on Thursday morning, when the flash sale actually goes live. The blogs that do publish early win the Thursday searches, rank first by Friday, and keep that ranking through the weekend because Google rewards the page that was there first. Our Black Friday earbuds deals guide showed the same effect: a post published early in the sale window earns traffic for days, while a post published on the day itself competes with hundreds of identical articles.

The Playbook: How Tech Blogs Turn One Day of Deals Into Ad Revenue

So how does a small tech blog actually make money from a day like today? The answer is not affiliate links alone, although those help. The real money comes from display advertising, and the playbook is simple enough for any blogger to copy.

Step one is publishing early with verified prices. A deal post that goes live in the morning on Thanksgiving Day, with every price checked against a live source, will outrank a post published at noon with the same information. Google's freshness signals reward the first accurate publisher, and readers reward the blog that saves them from checking five stores themselves. That is why every price in this post carries a source and a date — trust is the product, and the deals are the proof.

Step two is covering the whole store, not just the headline deal. A post about a single $89.99 TV ranks for one search; a post that covers TVs, Apple, Google, Samsung, appliances and smart home ranks for dozens, and it keeps the reader on the page longer. Longer time on page means more ad impressions and more revenue.

Step three is updating the post as prices change. A deal post is a living document during Black Friday week. Prices drop, stock runs out, new doorbusters appear. A blogger who updates the same post three times a day keeps it fresh in Google's eyes and gives readers a reason to come back. Each update is a new chance to rank, and each returning reader is another ad impression.

Step four is the ad layout. The highest-paying formats — the 300 by 250 rectangle, the 160 by 600 skyscraper, the 728 by 90 leaderboard and the native banner — belong in the highest-visibility spots: above the fold, at the top of the post, in the middle of the article and just below the first deal list. Step five is the email list: a deal post that ends with an invitation to get tomorrow's deals by email converts a one-day reader into a subscriber who comes back on Friday, Saturday and Cyber Monday.

None of this requires a big budget — just a blog, a few hours, and the discipline to check every price before publishing.

Is the $89.99 Insignia TV a good television?

It is a good second television. The 43-inch F50 Series is a 4K LED TV with the smart features most people need, and at $89.99 it costs less than a budget soundbar. For a bedroom, a dorm room or a guest room, it is the best value in the sale.

Several are identical — the AirPods Pro 3 are $149 at both stores, and the AirPods Max 2 are $419 at Best Buy against $429 at Amazon. Best Buy's MacBook and iPad mini prices are generally better, and Best Buy offers in-store pickup, which Amazon cannot match for a last-minute gift.

Some will. New doorbusters appear on Friday, and a few prices may fall by another ten or twenty dollars. But the all-time lows in this post — the AirPods Pro 3, the Apple Watch Series 12, the M6 MacBook Pro — are unlikely to go lower, and they are the most likely to sell out. Buy them today rather than gambling on Friday.
