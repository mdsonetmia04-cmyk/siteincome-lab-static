Black Friday 2026 is five days away, and the laptop is the star of the sale. Adobe forecasts that Americans will spend $12.9 billion online on Black Friday alone, part of a $275.1 billion holiday season, and laptops sit at the center of the deepest discounts in electronics. For tech bloggers, that combination makes laptop deal coverage the highest-earning content a small publisher can write this week. This guide breaks down the verified deals, explains the 2026 price twist, and gives you a playbook to turn Black Friday laptop searches into ad revenue.

The opportunity is bigger than one shopping day. Adobe's holiday forecast, published on September 28, 2026 and based on more than one trillion visits to United States retail sites, expects $47.5 billion in online spending across Cyber Week, the five days from Thanksgiving on November 26 through Cyber Monday on November 30. Cyber Monday stays the biggest single day at $15.1 billion, but Black Friday is growing faster, up 9.2 percent year over year against 6.2 percent for Cyber Monday. Shoppers are searching for specific models, not just generic sale pages, and that is exactly the traffic a well-built deal post can capture.

The $275 Billion Backdrop: Why This Black Friday Is Different

Adobe expects United States online sales from November 1 to December 31, 2026 to reach $275.1 billion, up 6.7 percent from last year. Thanksgiving Day is forecast at $6.9 billion, Black Friday at $12.9 billion, and Cyber Monday at $15.1 billion. Last year Americans spent $11.8 billion online on Black Friday and $14.25 billion on Cyber Monday. Both days should set new records in 2026, and Black Friday is growing faster.

Three shifts in how people shop make this year especially good for publishers. First, mobile now drives the majority of holiday spending: Adobe expects 57.4 percent of all holiday-season online spending to come from mobile devices, and 63 percent on Thanksgiving Day itself. Second, shoppers are leaning on artificial intelligence to find deals. Adobe expects AI traffic to United States retail sites to rise 130 percent year over year this holiday season, with the biggest jump on Thanksgiving at 159 percent, followed by Black Friday at 95 percent and Cyber Monday at 88 percent. Third, buy now pay later keeps growing: Adobe expects it to drive $21.3 billion across November and December, including $807 million on Black Friday alone — and laptops are exactly the purchases shoppers split into installments.

Discounts are real but not uniform. Adobe expects discounts of up to 30 percent off list price during Cyber Week, with the deepest cuts for electronics and computers landing on Cyber Monday, while the best deals for televisions, toys, apparel, appliances, and furniture land on Black Friday. A laptop post can earn on both days if it covers early deals now and gets updated with the Cyber Monday prices when they land. Amazon's Black Friday sale is already live, with Cyber Monday deals running through Monday, November 30. Best Buy's official sale began on November 19, running ten days of doorbusters through November 28, and HP's promotion runs from November 1 to November 28, ending at 11:59 PM Pacific Time.

The 2026 Twist: Memory Prices Changed the Math

There is one thing every laptop deal post this year has to explain: laptops got more expensive before they got cheaper. Memory makers moved factories toward the high-bandwidth memory AI data centers buy, squeezing supply for laptop memory and storage. The laptop makers passed the cost on, and the price hikes were large enough to change what a "good deal" means in 2026.

Apple moved first and most visibly. On June 25, 2026, Apple raised the price of the 512GB MacBook Air from $1,099 to $1,299, the 1TB MacBook Pro from $1,699 to $1,999, and the entry-level MacBook Neo from $599 to $699, citing memory and storage costs. Dell warned in December 2025 that its prices would rise by at least 15 to 20 percent, and Gartner forecast in March 2026 that average PC prices would rise 17 percent across the year. The result: a laptop advertised as 40 percent off may still cost more than the same model did a year ago — and your readers know it.

For a publisher, this is the angle that makes your post trustworthy. A deal post that only lists discounts reads like a retailer press release. A post that explains the price history — what the laptop cost before the June hike, what the discount actually saves, and whether the deal beats the pre-hike price — reads like advice from someone who did the homework. That trust turns a one-time search visitor into a returning reader, and it keeps a post ranking when Google's November core update rewards real expertise. We covered that update in our guide to the Google November 2026 Core Update. For every laptop you feature, state three numbers: the current sale price, the list price it is discounted from, and what the model cost before the 2026 price hikes if you can verify it. When a deal genuinely beats the old price, say so clearly. When a discount only erases the hike, say that too.

The Best Black Friday Laptop Deals 2026, Verified

Every price below was checked against retailer pages during the week of November 16 to 21, 2026. Prices change daily during Black Friday week, so re-check each one before you publish — a page showing a price the retailer no longer honors loses trust fast.

Apple MacBook: The Headline Deal

The MacBook Air with the M5 chip is the single most searched laptop deal of Black Friday 2026, and for once the discount is genuinely good. Amazon cut the 13-inch MacBook Air with M5, 16GB of memory, and 512GB of storage to $1,049 on November 21, down from its $1,299 list price — it was $1,099 earlier in the week. The 15-inch MacBook Air with M5 is $1,249, down from $1,499. Before Apple's June price hike, the 512GB MacBook Air cost $1,099. At $1,049, the deal does not just discount the new price — it beats the old one by $50, and that is why this deal leads every roundup. The new MacBook Neo, Apple's entry-level laptop with the A18 Pro chip, is also discounted: $649 for the 256GB model, down from $699, and $749 for the 512GB model, down from $799. At $649 it is the lowest entry point into the Mac lineup this Black Friday.

HP: The Deepest Discounts in the Sale

HP is running the broadest laptop sale of Black Friday 2026, with up to 56 percent off selected laptops and free shipping. The HP OmniBook 5 Flip, a 14-inch touchscreen two-in-one with an Intel Core 3 processor and 8GB of RAM, is $539.99, down from $999.99 — a 46 percent cut. The HP OmniBook 5 with a 14-inch 2K OLED touchscreen is $449.99 at Best Buy, down from $799.99, a $350 saving. The HP OmniBook 3, a 16-inch laptop with a 2K touchscreen, is $449.99 at Best Buy, down from $850, which deal trackers call its lowest price ever. The HP OmniBook X Flip, a 16-inch Copilot+ PC with a Ryzen AI 9 processor and 32GB of RAM, is $1,000, down from $1,850. And for readers on the tightest budget, the HP 14-inch laptop with an Intel N150 processor is $179.99, down from $349.99 — a working Windows laptop for under $200. HP's sale runs through November 28, so these prices should hold into Black Friday itself.

Dell: Business Laptops at Consumer Prices

Dell's Black Friday laptop deals lean toward business and premium machines, and the discounts are large in dollar terms even when the percentages look modest. The Dell XPS 14, with a 14.5-inch 3.2K OLED touchscreen, is $1,699.99, down 23 percent. The Dell 16S with a Core Ultra 9 processor, 32GB of RAM, and 512GB of storage is $1,499.99. The standout is the Dell Pro 15 business laptop, listed at $599.99 against a $1,549 list price — a 61 percent cut that puts a business-class machine at a budget price. Frame Dell's deals for the reader who works from their laptop all day: XPS for premium build quality, Pro for value, and the 16-inch models as desktop replacements.

ASUS and Lenovo: Gaming Power and Slim Ultrabooks

ASUS owns the gaming laptop deals this year. The ASUS ROG Strix G16 with a Ryzen 9 processor and an NVIDIA RTX 5060 graphics card is $2,399.99, down from $4,499.99 — a 47 percent cut on a serious gaming machine — while another configuration of the same model is $1,179.99, down from $1,586.67. The ASUS Vivobook 14 with a 2K display and Snapdragon X1 chip is $399.99, down from $700, its lowest tracked price. Lenovo's headline deal, the Yoga Slim 7x at $649 down from $1,299, already ended on November 19, which is a useful warning for your post: the best doorbusters sell out or expire before Black Friday itself. Lenovo's IdeaPad Slim 3i, a 15.6-inch business laptop, is still available at Walmart for $439.99, down from $739.89. Group the deals by reader, not by brand: shoppers care whether it is the best MacBook, the cheapest good laptop, the best gaming laptop, or the best laptop for work.

Where the Traffic Comes From

Laptop deal traffic follows a pattern every November: searches climb in early November, spike on the weekend before Black Friday, and peak twice — on Black Friday itself and again on Cyber Monday, when Adobe expects the deepest electronics discounts. But the searches that convert best are not the generic ones. "Black Friday laptop deals" is the biggest query, and it is also the hardest to rank for, because every major publication targets it. The searches a small blog can actually win are the specific ones: "MacBook Air M5 Black Friday price," "HP OmniBook 5 Flip deal," "best gaming laptop under $1,500 Black Friday," "cheapest laptop Black Friday 2026." Each has lower volume but far higher intent.

That is why your post needs to name models and prices instead of only listing brands. A page that says "HP laptops up to 56 percent off" competes with every other page. A page that says "HP OmniBook 3 16-inch 2K touchscreen, $449.99 at Best Buy, lowest price ever tracked" answers a question nobody else is answering in exactly that form. Google's November 2026 core update reinforced this: pages with specific, verifiable facts rank better than pages that restate the retailer. Your advantage as a small publisher is specificity.

Social traffic matters more during deal season than the rest of the year. A genuinely good price — the $179.99 HP laptop, the $1,049 MacBook Air — gets screenshotted and shared in messaging groups and deal forums within minutes of being published. Make your deals easy to share: short sentences, prices in bold, and a clear headline price in the opening paragraph. And remember that more than half your readers are on a phone, so test your post on a mobile screen before you publish.

How Tech Blogs Actually Earn From Laptop Deal Posts

A laptop deal post earns in three ways, and the best publishers use all three at once. The first is display advertising. Deal traffic is high-intent traffic, and advertisers pay more to reach people who are about to spend money than to reach people who are browsing casually. The formats that earn most stay visible while the reader scrolls through prices: a 300x250 unit inside the article, a 728x90 unit near the top, and a 160x600 unit beside long deal lists. Our guide to how much Adsterra pays per 1,000 visitors explains why geography matters: the same thousand visitors can be worth dramatically more from the United States than from a low-CPM country, and Black Friday deal searches are overwhelmingly American.

The second way is affiliate revenue. Retailer affiliate programs pay a percentage of each sale that comes through your link, and a laptop is a high-value sale, so even a modest percentage adds up fast when the product costs $1,000 or more. Think of affiliate income as a bonus on top of ad income, not a replacement: most readers will not click your link, and many who do will buy after the tracking window expires. Build the post to earn from ads first, so it pays even when nobody buys.

The third way is the one most new publishers miss: the deal post is a doorway. A reader who lands on your laptop deals page during Black Friday week owns a laptop, shops online, and trusts your taste. If your site has related content — a guide to the best VPN deals, a roundup of AI tools for creators, a hosting deal post for the blog they might start with their new laptop — that reader clicks through and views more pages, which means more ad impressions from the same visit. Internal links are how one search visitor becomes three or four pageviews. The 6-Move Playbook for Deal-Post Publishers

Publishing a laptop deal post is not hard. Publishing one that ranks and earns takes a system — these six moves, built for a small blog competing against publications with ten times your traffic.

Move 1: Publish Early, Update Often

The biggest mistake new publishers make is waiting for Black Friday to publish. By November 27, the big sites have been ranking for three weeks. Publish while the early deals are live and update the post every day through Cyber Monday. Each update signals freshness to Google and gives you a reason to share the post again.

Move 2: Lead With the Best Price, Not the Brand

Your opening paragraph should contain the single best deal in the post. A reader who sees "$1,049 MacBook Air" in the first three lines stays. A reader who has to scroll through three paragraphs of introduction leaves. Put the price or the discount in your headline too: a headline with a number beats one without it.

Move 3: Verify Every Price Yourself

Never copy a price from another blog without checking the retailer page. Deal roundups copy each other constantly, and a wrong price copied five times becomes a wrong price everywhere. Click through to the retailer, confirm the price, and note the time you checked it. If a deal has expired, remove it the same day.

Move 4: Explain the Price History

This is the move that separates a useful post from a copied one, and it matters more in 2026 than in any recent year because of the memory-driven price hikes. For each featured laptop, say what it cost before the hike, what it costs now, and whether the deal actually saves money against the old price. The MacBook Air at $1,049 beats its pre-hike $1,099 price — that fact is worth more than any discount percentage.

Move 5: Place Ads Where Eyes Already Are

Deal readers scroll in a predictable pattern: headline, first deal, price table, second deal, and then they either click out or keep scrolling. Put your best ad units along that path — a 300x250 unit after the first two deals, a banner above the price list, and a tall unit beside the longest section — instead of stacking them all at the bottom where nobody looks. Keep the page fast: deal traffic is the least patient traffic on the internet.

Move 6: Plan the Follow-Up Before You Publish

A deal post is the start of a sequence, not a single article. Before you publish, decide what comes next: a Cyber Monday update, a "deals still live" post for the week after, and a gift guide for December. Each links back to the laptop post. The sites that win Black Friday cover the whole season, not just the day.

Mistakes That Kill a Deal Post

The fastest way to waste a good Black Friday is to make one of the classic deal-post mistakes. The first is publishing prices you never verified: a reader who clicks through to a $1,049 MacBook Air and finds it at $1,299 will never trust your site again. The second is keyword stuffing — repeating "Black Friday laptop deals 2026" in every sentence does not help you rank anymore. Use the phrase naturally, in the title, a few headings, and the opening, then write like a person. The third is copying a retailer's description word for word: Google has seen that text before and will rank the retailer above you every time. Write your own summary of who the laptop is for. The fourth is forgetting the disclosure: if you earn from affiliate links, say so clearly. The fifth is publishing late and never updating — a deal post with November 20 prices is a liability on November 27. If you cannot update it daily through Cyber Monday, publish a smaller post you can maintain instead.

Your 7-Day Plan Before Black Friday

With five days left, here is the schedule that turns one post into a week of earnings. Today, publish the laptop deal post and share it once on every channel you use. Tomorrow, re-check every price, update the ones that moved, and add any new deal. On Wednesday, publish a short companion post — the best laptop accessories under $50, or the best laptop for students — and link it to the main deal post. On Thanksgiving Day, check your post on a phone and fix anything that looks broken. On Black Friday, update the prices in the morning and again in the evening. On Saturday, publish a "deals still live" update for the shoppers who missed Friday. And on Cyber Monday, update the post one final time with the new prices and change the headline to reflect them. That is seven days of work from one post — how a small blog earns like a big one during the biggest shopping week of the year.

The Bottom Line

Black Friday 2026 is a $12.9 billion day inside a $275.1 billion season, and laptops are the product where the discounts, the search demand, and the buyer intent all line up at once. The deals are real — a MacBook Air at $1,049 that beats its pre-hike price, HP laptops at up to 56 percent off, gaming machines cut nearly in half — but they only earn for the publisher who verifies every price, explains the price history honestly, and updates the post daily through Cyber Monday. Publish early, lead with the best price, and treat the deal post as the start of a season of content rather than a single article. The shoppers are already searching. Make sure your post is the one they find.
