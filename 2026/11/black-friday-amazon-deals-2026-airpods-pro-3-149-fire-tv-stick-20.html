Here is the deal every tech blogger waits all year for: AirPods Pro 3 for $149, a Fire TV Stick 4K for $20, and an Echo Dot for $55 — all live right now on Amazon, all at prices being called all-time lows. The sale is not a one-day event. Amazon's Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday event runs for twelve straight days, from November 19 through November 30, 2026, and it is the single biggest traffic week of the year for the blogs that cover it. If you run a tech blog, a deals site, or any website that earns from display ads, this is the week your traffic chart can look like a rocket launch. If you are a shopper, this is the week to buy the gadgets you have been watching since summer. This post does both jobs: every major Amazon Black Friday deal for 2026, with the real prices verified today, and the playbook tech blogs use to turn this one sale into a month of ad revenue.

Amazon announced the event on November 9, 2026, and it kicked off at 12:01 a.m. Pacific time on Thursday, November 19. It runs through Cyber Monday, November 30, which means shoppers get twelve days of deals, not one frantic Friday. For the first time ever, Amazon is dropping new deals twice on both Black Friday and Cyber Monday — at midnight and at 8 a.m. Pacific time — and the headline savings run up to 60 percent off. That structure changes how smart shoppers behave, and it changes how smart bloggers publish. A blog that updates through the week, catches the double drops, and ranks for the exact product names people type into Google — "AirPods Pro 3 Black Friday price," "Fire TV Stick 4K deal," "Echo Dot $55" — earns the clicks, and the ad impressions that come with them, all week long.

Why Amazon's 12-Day Sale Is the Biggest Money Week of the Year for Tech Blogs

Every niche has its Super Bowl, and for tech and deals blogs, it is this week. Search interest in Black Friday deals does not build slowly and fade gently; it spikes like a wall. People who never read a tech blog all year suddenly search for the exact price of the exact gadget they want, and they click the result that shows the price in the title. That is the whole game. A post titled "Amazon Black Friday Deals 2026" with real prices in the first paragraph will outrank a prettier post with no numbers, because the searcher is not browsing — they are hunting. They want to know whether the AirPods Pro 3 are really $149, whether the Fire TV Stick 4K is really $20, and whether the deal is still live. Answer those questions in the first screen, and you keep the reader. Make them scroll through three paragraphs of throat-clearing first, and they bounce back to Google and click your competitor.

The second reason this week pays so well for publishers is that the traffic is not just big, it is buying traffic. Readers arriving from a "Black Friday deals" search are in the most commercial mindset a visitor can have: wallet open, decision nearly made, comparing two prices. Advertisers bid more for that audience during the holiday season than at any other time of year. So a tech blog does not need a miracle to have its best revenue week of the year — it needs traffic, and this sale delivers the traffic, as long as the blog publishes the right posts at the right time.

Let us get to the numbers, because the numbers are the post. Every price below was verified from live deal coverage of Amazon's Black Friday Week on November 25 and 26, 2026, and every one of them is either an all-time low or the lowest price of the year so far. Our Black Friday earbuds guide covers the wider earbuds market, but on Amazon specifically, the AirPods Pro 3 at $149 is the price every other earbuds deal is measured against this year. If you have been waiting for a reason to upgrade from older AirPods, or from a cheaper pair of buds entirely, this is the reason, and it is $100 worth of reason.

The rest of Apple's lineup is nearly as aggressive. The brand-new AirPods Max 2 are down to $419 from $549, a $130 discount. The Apple Watch Series 12, the 42mm model, is $299, down from $399 — another $100 off and a new all-time low. The Apple Watch SE 3 starts from $179, which makes it the cheapest way into a current Apple Watch this holiday season, and the model most gift buyers should look at first, because it does almost everything the flagship does for roughly half the money. The M5 MacBook Air, Apple's newest laptop, is $300 off, a rare discount on a machine that only just arrived. And the Apple Pencil with USB-C is down to $49. Apple almost never discounts this deeply on its own store.

At these prices, the question is not whether to buy a Fire TV Stick, but how many rooms in the house still have a television without one. A $20 streaming stick is also the perfect gift for the person who already has everything.

The mid-price items, the $100-to-$200 range, are where the volume is, because that is the range where a shopper can say yes without a family meeting.

The Sale Calendar: Dates, Double Drops, and When Prices Change

The most common mistake shoppers make during Amazon's Black Friday Week is treating it as one day. It is a twelve-day event with a rhythm, and knowing the rhythm is worth real money. The sale began on November 19 and runs through November 30. Thanksgiving Day is Thursday, November 26 — today — and it is a real shopping day in its own right. Black Friday itself is Friday, November 27. The weekend follows, and Cyber Monday, November 30, is the final day and the last chance for the sale prices.

The detail that matters most this year is the double drop. For the first time, Amazon is releasing new deals twice on both Black Friday and Cyber Monday — at midnight and at 8 a.m. Pacific time. In Eastern time, that is 3 a.m. and 11 a.m. The morning drop is the one most shoppers will actually catch, and it is the one most blogs will be late to, because the deals go live while the blogger is still asleep or still writing. The blogs that win this week are the ones that check the sale at 11 a.m. Eastern on Friday and Monday, update their posts within the hour, and tell their readers exactly what is new.

There is a second timing lesson, and it is about patience. Amazon has said that most Black Friday deals will stay at the same low price through the whole twelve-day event, which means there is no need to panic-buy on the first day. But the "Today's Big Deals" section is different: those offers are limited-time, they refresh daily, and they can sell out. The right strategy is simple. Buy the all-time-low flagship items — the AirPods Pro 3, the Fire TV Stick 4K, the Echo Dot — as soon as you see the price you want, because those are the items that sell out. For everything else, check the daily themed drops and compare with other retailers. Our Best Buy Thanksgiving sale guide shows how the other big retailers are playing the same week, and the smart shopper checks both before buying anything over $200.

What to Buy, What to Skip, and How to Spot a Real Deal

Not every discount in a Black Friday sale is a real discount, and the shoppers who save the most money are the ones who know the difference. A real deal has three signs. First, the "was" price matches the price the product actually sold for most of the year — the AirPods Pro 3 really were $249, the Echo Dot really was $100, the Fire TV Stick 4K really was $60. Second, the discount is the deepest of the year, not a price the product hits every other month. Third, the product is one people actually wanted before the sale started. The AirPods Pro 3, the Fire TV Stick 4K, and the Echo Dot pass all three tests. That is the whole checklist, and it takes about thirty seconds to apply.

What should you skip? Skip the deal on a product you have never heard of, from a brand you cannot pronounce, with a discount so large it looks like a mistake. Skip the television whose model number you cannot find reviewed anywhere, because retailers sometimes build special models for Black Friday with cheaper panels and fewer features than the model you researched. And skip the upgrade you do not need: the M5 MacBook Air at $300 off is a wonderful deal, but only if you need a laptop. A discount on something you would not buy at full price is not a saving — it is spending with better marketing.

The Blogger Playbook: How Tech Blogs Turn One Sale Into a Month of Revenue

This is the part of the post most readers will skip, and it is the part that pays for the blog you are reading. A tech blog does not earn money from Black Friday by accident. It earns by doing four specific things, and every one of them is visible in the post you are reading right now.

First, it publishes early and updates often. The main Amazon deals post goes live before the sale starts, or in the first days of it, so Google has time to find it, rank it, and send it traffic while the sale is still running. Then it gets updated every time a price changes or a new deal drops. An updated post tells Google the page is alive, and it tells the reader the prices are trustworthy. A post that still shows a sold-out price on Cyber Monday is a post that has stopped earning.

Second, it puts the prices in the title and in the first paragraph. Searchers click the result that answers their question before they click it. "AirPods Pro 3 at $149" in the title is not clickbait — it is the answer, delivered up front, and it is the reason the post ranks for the searches that matter. The same rule applies to the opening paragraph, which is also the text Google shows as the description under the link. If the description contains the price, the click is nearly guaranteed.

Third, it covers the sale as a season, not a day. One post about Amazon's sale is a lottery ticket. Ten posts — the main guide, the Apple deals, the Fire TV deals, the TV deals, the earbuds deals, the Thanksgiving flash deals, the Cyber Monday preview — are a portfolio. Each one catches a different search, each one links to the others, and the reader who arrives for one deal discovers five more. That internal linking turns one click into three page views.

Fourth, it writes for the reader who is about to spend money, not for the reader who is browsing for fun. That means short paragraphs, clear prices, honest verdicts, and a clear statement of what to buy and what to skip. A reader who trusts your verdict on the AirPods will trust your verdict on the television, and a reader who trusts your verdicts comes back in December for the Christmas gift guide. Trust is the only asset a deals blog has that cannot be copied, and it is built one honest price at a time.

The Mistakes That Cost Blogs Their Best Week of the Year

For every blog that has its best week of the year during Black Friday, another one wastes it, and the mistakes are always the same. The first is publishing too late. A post that goes live on Friday afternoon has missed three days of searches, and that traffic is gone forever. The second is publishing without prices, or with prices copied from last year's sale. A reader who clicks through and finds the wrong price never comes back. The third is publishing one post and stopping, which leaves the whole long tail of searches — the product-specific searches, the "is it worth it" searches, the Cyber Monday searches — to competitors who kept publishing.

The fourth mistake is forgetting the follow-up. The readers who found your blog during Black Friday week are the warmest audience you will ever have. If you publish nothing for them in December, they will forget you by January. A Christmas gift guide, a "what to buy with your gift card" post, and a New Year sale roundup turn one week of traffic into a season of readers.
