Best Google AdSense Alternatives in 2026 (10 Networks Compared)

Google AdSense is the default first ad network for most bloggers — and for many, the first disappointment. Approval can take weeks, the payout floor is $100, and the average RPM bloggers reported in 2025 was just $2.34 per 1,000 pageviews. On a small blog, AdSense can take months to pay anything at all.

The market moved a lot in 2025–2026. Raptive lowered its bar to 25,000 pageviews, Mediavine opened a low-traffic on-ramp, Ezoic raised its bar so high it left the small-publisher game — while Adsterra, Monetag, and Infolinks still take sites from day one.

This post compares 10 networks on the four things that decide your income: entry requirement, payout minimum, reported RPM, and the honest catch. Every requirement and payout figure was checked against the networks' own pages or publisher guides citing them in November 2026 — and where a network doesn't publish a number, the table says so instead of inventing one.

How this comparison was made

Two rules: (1) Entry requirements and payout minimums were checked on each network's own pages, or guides citing them, in October–November 2026 — re-check on the day you apply, because these changed a lot in 2025–2026. (2) RPMs are reported averages, not promises. Single figures ($2.34, $34.86) come from Productive Blogging's 2025 survey of 187 bloggers (self-reported, mostly US/UK); ranges ($1–4, $3–10) come from publisher guides. Your RPM depends on niche, country, season, and layout.

The 2026 ladder moved — in both directions

  • Ezoic went up, 25×. Until February 19, 2026 the bar was about 10,000 monthly visits. It is now 250,000 monthly active users for new sites. Earlier sites are grandfathered — but lose that status if they remove Ezoic's integration for more than 7 days. A smaller Incubator program accepts 20 publishers a month.
  • Raptive went down, 4×. In October 2025 it cut its minimum from 100,000 to 25,000 monthly pageviews (with a Tier-1 traffic share rule for smaller sites).
  • Mediavine split in two. Full Mediavine no longer uses a session count — it asks for $5,000 in annual ad revenue. Its Journey program takes growing sites from 1,000 Tier-1 sessions in 30 days.
  • Monumetric held steady at 10,000 pageviews, still the only network here that charges a setup fee ($99 under 80,000 pageviews).

The late-2026 ladder: AdSense → Adsterra/Monetag (day one) → Journey (1,000 Tier-1 sessions) → Monumetric (10,000 pageviews) → Raptive (25,000 pageviews) → Mediavine ($5,000/year) — with Ezoic now above all of them, mostly out of reach.

1. Google AdSense — the baseline

  • Entry requirement: No traffic minimum. Every site is reviewed for original content, policy compliance, and a minimum age (18+ to hold the account). Approval can take days to weeks, and new or thin sites are routinely rejected.
  • Best for: Your first network. It accepts almost any niche, pays reliably, and its ads are the least likely to hurt your user experience or your Google standing.
  • Payout minimum: $100, paid monthly once your balance clears it.
  • Reported RPM: $2.34 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average). Publisher revenue share is 68% for content ads.
  • Honest limitation: The lowest RPM here, and the $100 floor means a small blog can wait months for its first payment. AdSense is also famous for sudden account bans with minimal explanation and no real appeal. Safest network, slowest-paying at the same time.

3. Monetag — the no-minimum workhorse

  • Entry requirement: None. No traffic minimum, no minimum pageviews, and it accepts social traffic, Telegram Mini Apps, and Android APKs as well as websites. The domain must be self-hosted; adult and torrent sites are not accepted.
  • Best for: Small and international sites that premium networks won't touch. Formats: popunder, push, in-page push, vignette banner, and SmartLink. It is the publisher brand of Propeller Ads Ltd (see #4).
  • Payout minimum: $5 on PayPal, Skrill, and WebMoney; $20–30 on Payoneer depending on country; $100 on crypto; $500 on wire. Default schedule is twice a month (4th and 19th); weekly payouts are reserved for Silver-level publishers and above.
  • Reported RPM: $1–4 (publisher guides). Monetag itself publishes no rate card.
  • Honest limitation: Same trade-off as Adsterra: the best-paying formats (popunder, push) are the ones readers like least, RPMs are low on Tier-3 traffic, and the payout schedule is confusing — homepage says weekly, help center says twice a month. Test it, don't assume.

4. PropellerAds — read this before you sign up on the wrong side

  • Entry requirement: None — but as a publisher, you don't sign up here anymore. PropellerAds moved its publisher business to Monetag in 2022; it now serves advertisers only. If you land on propellerads.com looking to earn, you'll be redirected.
  • Payout minimum / reported RPM: $5 / $1–5 (same inventory as Monetag, above).
  • Honest limitation: The brand split itself: any guide still listing "PropellerAds for publishers" with fresh 2026 numbers is quoting stale data — publisher terms, payouts, and formats are Monetag's now.

5. Media.net — the contextual specialist

  • Entry requirement: No published traffic minimum, but approval is a genuine quality review: high-quality English-language content and significant traffic from the US, UK, and Canada. Some guides cite an informal 10,000-pageview bar, but Media.net doesn't publish one — apply on content quality, not a number.
  • Best for: Content-heavy sites in finance, tech, and B2B with mostly Tier-1 traffic. It runs contextual ads from the Yahoo/Bing network, which is why it can out-earn AdSense on commercial US/UK traffic.
  • Payout minimum: $100, paid Net 30 via PayPal or wire.
  • Reported RPM: $3–10 on Tier-1 traffic (publisher guides); some finance/tech sites report higher.
  • Honest limitation: Outside Tier-1 traffic and commercial niches it earns little — a blog with mostly Tier-3 readers will do better on Adsterra or Monetag. The $100 floor is as slow as AdSense, and approval can reject without useful detail.

6. Journey by Mediavine — the new on-ramp to premium RPMs

  • Entry requirement: 1,000 sessions from Tier-1 countries (US, Canada, UK, Australia) within 30 days, original audience-first content, and Mediavine's free Grow plugin running on your site for at least 30 days. Ad partners also want about four months of domain history.
  • Best for: Small blogs with a real US/UK/CA/AU audience that want premium-network RPMs years earlier than the old rules allowed. It is self-supported (no dedicated account manager) with a built-in path to full Mediavine once your site reaches $5,000 in annual ad revenue.
  • Payout minimum: $25 (reported), paid Net 65. Publisher revenue share is 70%.
  • Reported RPM: $11.15 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average) — roughly 5× AdSense's reported average.
  • Honest limitation: Only Tier-1 sessions count — a blog with 3,000 monthly sessions that are 70% Tier-3 does not qualify. It requires WordPress (the Grow plugin), so Blogspot sites can't use it directly, and Net 65 means waiting over two months for your first payment.

7. Raptive — the highest reported RPM in this list

  • Entry requirement: 25,000 monthly pageviews (lowered from 100,000 on October 16, 2025). Between 25,000 and 99,999 pageviews, at least 50% of traffic must come from the US, UK, Canada, New Zealand, or Australia (40% above 100,000). The domain must be at least 6 months old, content 100% original, and Google Analytics properly configured.
  • Best for: Lifestyle, food, home, and finance blogs with a strong Tier-1 audience. Raptive (formerly AdThrive) is a full-service managed network: it designs your ad layout and runs the auctions for you.
  • Payout minimum: Not published on the pages we checked — confirm at application. Revenue share is a flat 75%, paid Net 45.
  • Reported RPM: $34.86 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average) — the highest in this comparison, about 15× AdSense's reported average.
  • Honest limitation: The geography gate is the real barrier: most blogs outside the US/UK/CA/AU/NZ can't qualify even at 25,000 pageviews. It's also exclusive — your only display and video ad source — and the floor still excludes most new blogs.

8. Monumetric — managed ads from 10,000 pageviews (with a fee)

  • Entry requirement: 10,000–80,000 pageviews a month for its Propel program (the range must hold for three consecutive months, per its program overview). The site must be WordPress or Blogger, have a sidebar at least 300px wide, room for at least six ad slots, and at least 50% of traffic from the US, UK, Canada, or Australia.
  • Best for: Small blogs that want a managed network to install and optimize ads for them, without waiting for 25,000 pageviews.
  • Payout minimum: $10, paid Net 60 (within the first 10 business days of the month). Above 80,000 pageviews you move to Ascend with no setup fee.
  • Reported RPM: $6.77 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average).
  • Honest limitation: The only network here that charges you to join — a one-time $99 setup fee under 80,000 pageviews. Six ad slots is a heavy commitment for a small blog, Net 60 is the slowest payout here, and at 10,000 pageviews $99 takes months to earn back.

9. Ezoic — the network that left small publishers behind

  • Entry requirement: 250,000+ monthly active users for new sites, effective February 19, 2026 (verified via Google Analytics). Sites already monetizing before that date are grandfathered — unless they remove Ezoic's integration for more than 7 days, which voids the exemption. A limited Incubator program accepts 20 smaller publishers a month.
  • Best for: Large sites that want AI-tested ad layouts and access to premium demand. Ezoic sits between you and multiple ad networks, testing placements automatically.
  • Payout minimum: $20, paid monthly.
  • Reported RPM: $11.08 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average).
  • Honest limitation: For most readers of this blog, the honest answer is: don't apply. At 250,000 users it is no longer an entry-level network — and its reported RPM ($11.08) is no better than Journey's ($11.15), which you can join at 1,000 Tier-1 sessions.

10. Infolinks — the stackable extra

  • Entry requirement: None. No traffic minimum, easy approval, and it works on any site.
  • Best for: Stacking on top of your main network. Infolinks sells in-text ads (keywords in your content become ad links), in-fold, in-tag, and in-frame units — formats that use no traditional ad space, so they layer over AdSense, Adsterra, or a managed network without competing for the same slots.
  • Payout minimum: $50, paid Net 45 via PayPal, bank wire, ACH, or eCheck. Publisher revenue share is 70%.
  • Reported RPM: $1–3 (publisher guides).
  • Honest limitation: The lowest RPMs here — supplemental income, not a main network. In-text ads can annoy readers, and the $50 floor is high for what it earns. Test it for 30 days, watch bounce rate, drop it if readers hate it.

Two warnings that save more money than any network switch:

  1. Don't switch for a $1 RPM difference at low traffic. At 5,000 pageviews a month, a $2→$4 RPM jump is worth $10 a month; growing to 20,000 pageviews is worth $40+ at the same RPM. Content growth beats network-hopping until you're past 25,000 pageviews.
  2. Check the exclusivity clause. Managed networks (Raptive, Mediavine) require exclusivity — your only display ad source. Self-serve networks can usually run on separate placements, but always read the terms: signing an exclusive deal, then running a second network's ads, is the fastest way to get terminated.

Honest pros and cons of leaving AdSense

Pros

  • Faster first payout: Adsterra and Monetag pay from $5; AdSense makes you wait for $100. For a small blog, that is the difference between seeing money in month one and month six.
  • Higher RPMs exist: Journey ($11.15), Raptive ($34.86), and Mediavine ($31.55) all reported multiples of AdSense's $2.34 average — if you qualify and your audience is Tier-1.
  • Traffic that AdSense ignores still pays: Adsterra and Monetag monetize Tier-2/Tier-3 traffic and non-English audiences that premium networks won't take.
  • Approval is faster elsewhere: Adsterra approves in ~10 minutes; AdSense can take weeks and rejects thin sites outright.

Cons

  • AdSense is the safest network: Google's ads are the least likely to hurt your user experience, your Core Web Vitals, or your standing with Google itself. Aggressive formats (popunder, push) earn more per visitor and cost you readers.
  • Premium RPMs need premium traffic: The networks that pay $30+ RPMs demand 25,000 pageviews and mostly Tier-1 readers. For most new blogs, those numbers are a year or more away.
  • Some alternatives charge you: Monumetric's $99 setup fee is real money before you've earned a cent.
  • Switching has hidden costs: new ad code, layout re-testing, exclusivity clauses, payout schedules that restart your waiting clock. A switch is a project, not a toggle.

FAQ

What is the best AdSense alternative for a new blog? Start with AdSense itself plus one no-minimum network — Adsterra or Monetag — and compare what your traffic earns. Past 1,000 Tier-1 sessions a month, apply to Journey by Mediavine for the biggest RPM jump open to small sites.

Which ad network pays the highest RPM? Raptive averaged $34.86 per 1,000 pageviews and Mediavine $31.55 in Productive Blogging's 2025 survey of 187 bloggers, against $2.34 for AdSense. But those averages come from established blogs with mostly US/UK readers — and most new blogs can't join either network yet.

Can I use AdSense and an alternative together? Infolinks is built to stack — its in-text ads use no display slots. Self-serve networks like Adsterra and Monetag can usually run on separate placements alongside AdSense. Managed networks (Raptive, Mediavine) require exclusivity — read the terms first.

Why did Ezoic raise its minimum to 250,000 users? Ezoic raised its minimum for new sites from ~10,000 visits to 250,000 monthly active users on February 19, 2026. Earlier sites are grandfathered unless they remove the integration for more than 7 days; smaller sites can apply to its Incubator (20 publishers a month).

Do I need traffic to join Adsterra? No. Adsterra has no traffic minimum and approves publishers in about 10 minutes, in 248 GEOs. Earnings depend on niche and reader geography — see How to Get Approved on Adsterra as a Publisher (Step-by-Step, 2026).

Which network pays the fastest? Adsterra and Monetag both pay from $5 on biweekly schedules. AdSense and Media.net make you wait for $100. Journey pays Net 65 and Monumetric Net 60 — the slowest first payments even after you qualify.
