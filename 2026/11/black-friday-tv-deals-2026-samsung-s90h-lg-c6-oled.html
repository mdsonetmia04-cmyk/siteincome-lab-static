OLED vs Mini LED vs QLED: Which Panel Should You Buy?

Three technologies dominate this year's deals, and the marketing names make them sound more different than they are. OLED means every pixel makes its own light, so black is truly black and the contrast is perfect. That is why the Samsung S90H and the LG C6 look so good in a dark room — and why they cost more. Mini LED means a regular LCD screen lit by thousands of tiny backlights, which makes the picture much brighter than old LCD televisions and much cheaper than OLED. The TCL QM6K, the Samsung M80H and the Hisense U7 are all Mini LED sets, and they are the right choice for bright rooms. QLED is Samsung and TCL's name for an LCD television with a quantum dot layer that makes colors richer; most QLED sets this year are also Mini LED sets, so the terms overlap.

The buying rule is simple. Dark room and picture quality first: buy OLED — the S90H at $1,397.99 or the C6 at $1,349.99. Bright room or budget under $1,000: buy Mini LED — the TCL QM6K at $599.99 is the value king, the Hisense U7 at $997.99 the big-screen king. Budget under $300: buy whatever 65-inch 4K set is cheapest from a brand you recognize. At that price, size matters more than specs.

Gamers should pay attention to one number above all others: refresh rate. A 120Hz or 144Hz panel makes fast games look smoother, and it matters on the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X. The LG QNED84B at $479.99 and the Samsung M80H at $399.99 both offer 144Hz at prices that would have been unthinkable two years ago.

What Size TV Should You Buy?

The most common regret in television buying is buying too small. People measure their wall, imagine a 55-inch set, and then wish they had bought the 65-inch within a month. The old rule — sit about one and a half times the screen's diagonal away — still works well. For a 55-inch television, sit about 7 feet away. For a 65-inch set, about 8 feet. For a 75-inch set, about 9 to 10 feet. Most American living rooms sit 8 to 10 feet from the sofa to the wall, which means most living rooms should have a 65-inch or 75-inch television — and this year good 65-inch sets start at $229.99 and good 75-inch sets at $449.99.

Bedrooms are different. A 50-inch or 55-inch set is usually right for a bedroom, because you sit closer and the room is darker. The Amazon Ember 50-inch at $239.99 or the LG QNED84B 55-inch at $479.99 both fit that job. For a dorm room or a small apartment, the 43-inch LG QNED73B at $249.99 — down from $359.99 — is the compact option that still feels like a real television rather than a large monitor.

Before you order, measure twice. A 75-inch television is about 66 inches wide and 38 inches tall, and it needs a stand at least that wide or a wall mount rated for its weight. Many retailers offer free delivery on large televisions during Black Friday week, but installation usually costs extra.

How to Spot a Fake Black Friday TV Discount

Not every discount is as honest as it looks, and the difference between a real deal and a fake one is worth understanding before you spend hundreds of dollars. A real discount compares today's price with a price the store actually charged recently. The LG QNED84B is a good example: LG charged $729.99 for it on November 2, and now charges $479.99 — a real $250 saving you can check. The TCL QM7L is another: $999.99 at Best Buy on October 29, $849.99 now, a real $150 drop.

A fake discount compares today's price with a launch price that nobody ever paid. The LG C6 is the classic case: its $2,699.99 launch price makes the $1,349.99 sale price look like a 50 percent discount, but the set was already selling for $1,399.99 at LG's own store before the sale. That does not make it a bad deal — $1,349.99 is still a superb price — but the honest saving is closer to $50 off the recent street price than $1,350 off the launch price. The same applies to the Hisense U7: $997.99 is a genuine bargain, but the set had already drifted well below its $1,999.99 launch price before November.

Here is the three-step check for any deal. First, search the model number and look at what it cost in October. Second, check the manufacturer's own store, not just Amazon or Best Buy, because maker stores often show the real recent price. Third, be suspicious of any discount bigger than 50 percent on a current-year model. The deals in this post pass that test: the S90H at $1,397.99 is an all-time low, the TCL QM6K at $599.99 is a real drop, and the Samsung M80H at $499.99 is $200 below its genuine regular price.

How Tech Blogs Turn TV Week Into Ad Revenue

Now the part that matters if you run a website. Black Friday television week is one of the highest-earning weeks of the year for tech blogs, and the reason is the advertising, not affiliate commissions. A reader comparing a $1,397.99 OLED with a $599.99 Mini LED is about to spend real money, so advertisers in electronics, streaming and gaming all bid hard for that reader's attention. Deal content in late November earns several times more per thousand views than the same traffic in an ordinary week.

Second, television buyers read more pages. A shopper reads a deals roundup, then a comparison of OLED versus Mini LED, then a size guide, then a review of the model they like — and each page carries ads. That is why the smartest tech blogs publish a cluster of related articles for Black Friday week and link them together. Our earlier posts on Black Friday monitor deals, Black Friday streaming deals and Black Friday gaming deals work the same way: one reader, several pages, several ad impressions.

Third, the content stays valuable after the sale ends. People search for television advice all year — "best 65 inch TV", "OLED vs QLED" and "what size TV for my room" are evergreen searches. Update the prices after Black Friday week instead of deleting the post, and the same article keeps earning through Christmas, the Super Bowl, and into the spring. A post that earns for one week is a lottery ticket; a post that earns for six months is an asset.

Fourth, television week is the best time to grow an email list. Offer a simple deal alert — "we will email you if the S90H drops below $1,300" — and a meaningful share of readers will sign up. That list is worth more than the week's ad revenue, because you can email those readers again for Cyber Monday, Christmas, and the Super Bowl sales in January and February.

Finally, the publishing playbook. Publish early — the best deals went live on November 19, and the blogs that published that day captured the first wave of search traffic. Update daily — prices change, models sell out, and Google rewards pages updated during sale week. Use real prices with the date you checked them, and write for the reader standing in a store aisle with a phone in their hand: short paragraphs, clear prices, a direct recommendation in every section.

Quick Answers Before You Buy

Is Black Friday actually the cheapest time to buy a TV? For most models, yes — but not by much. The S90H at $1,397.99 is an all-time low and the TCL QM6K at $599.99 is 20 percent off, but the LG C6 was already near its Black Friday price in late October. The real advantage of Black Friday is selection: every model is on sale at once.

Should I wait for Cyber Monday? Cyber Monday, November 30, sometimes brings a second wave of discounts, mostly on accessories and smaller televisions. The big television deals rarely get cheaper on Monday — and the popular models are often sold out by then. If the television you want is in stock at a price you like, buy it now.

Which TV should I buy if I only want one recommendation? Buy the Samsung 65-inch S90H OLED at $1,397.99 if your budget allows it — it is the best television of 2026 at its lowest price ever. Buy the TCL 65-inch QM6K at $599.99 if you want the best value. Buy the TCL 65-inch S4CF at $229.99 if you want the biggest screen for the least money. Those three cover every budget, and you cannot go wrong with any of them.
