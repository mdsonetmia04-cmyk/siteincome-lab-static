Black Friday Xbox Deals 2026: No Console Discount — Series X Stays $799.99, But Controllers From $49 and 2,000 Games Up to 80% Off — How Gaming Blogs Turn the Year's Biggest Xbox Sale Into Ad Revenue

Here is the honest truth about Black Friday Xbox deals in 2026: Microsoft is not discounting the Xbox Series X or the Xbox Series S this year. After the price increase on August 1, 2026, the disc-drive Series X costs $799.99, the all-digital Series X costs $749.99, and the cheapest new Xbox is the 512GB Series S at $499.99. But the sale around the console is genuinely excellent. Microsoft's Black Friday sale runs from November 19 through December 2 and cuts up to 80 percent off more than 2,000 Xbox and PC games, the standard Xbox Wireless Controller is down to $49.06 from $64.99, the Elite Series 2 Core controller is $111.74 instead of $149.99, Game Pass Ultimate memberships are discounted at several retailers, and a certified refurbished Series X is available from Microsoft itself for $649.99. Every price in this post was verified against live listings on November 25, 2026.

The Headline Nobody Wanted: No Xbox Console Discount in 2026

Microsoft raised Xbox console prices on August 1, 2026, the third increase since early 2025, and memory and storage costs are the reason the company and retailers keep giving. Tom's Guide reported on November 19 that no Xbox console is meaningfully discounted for Black Friday because of high memory prices, and Microsoft's own Black Friday announcement, published on November 17, lists games and accessories only. The Xbox sale that opened on November 19 at 9 p.m. Pacific covers more than 2,000 games at up to 80 percent off, discounts on controllers, and free engraving on custom controllers through Xbox Design Lab — but not a single dollar off a new console.

Advertisement

That makes 2026 different from almost every Black Friday before it. Anyone quoting you a $499 or $549 Series X this week is showing you an old listing or a third-party seller you should check very carefully before trusting. If you want a brand-new Xbox Series X this Black Friday, you will pay $799.99 for the disc version or $749.99 for the digital version. The lesson for gaming bloggers is just as valuable: when the product everyone searches for has no discount, the honest headline — "no console discount" — is the one that earns the click, because it answers the exact question millions of people are typing into Google right now.

Xbox Series X and Series S Prices Right Now

Before you buy anything, you need the real prices in front of you, because the numbers have moved so much this year that last year's prices will mislead you. Here is the full current lineup at Microsoft's own store, verified against listings published in mid-November 2026. The Xbox Series X with a disc drive, the flagship console with a 1TB SSD and 4K gaming at up to 120 frames per second in supported titles, lists at $799.99. The Xbox Series X Digital Edition, the same 1TB console in white without a disc drive, lists at $749.99. The Xbox Series S with 1TB of storage lists at $599.99, and the Xbox Series S with 512GB — the cheapest way into the current generation of Xbox — lists at $499.99. None of these carries an official Black Friday discount.

Two details deserve your attention. Second, the Series S is no longer the $299 impulse buy it once was: at $499.99 for 512GB it costs as much as a Series X did a few years ago, and the 1TB model at $599.99 is only $150 less than the digital Series X. If you mainly play Game Pass titles and do not care about discs, the digital Series X at $749.99 is arguably the better buy than a 1TB Series S at $599.99, because you get a much more powerful console and 4K output for $150 more. Retailer bundles are the one place where the effective price moves — a bundle that adds a game, an extra controller, or Game Pass time is worth comparing against the $799.99 list price plus the value of what is included, and in the UK, Currys is selling an Xbox Series X bundled with EA Sports FC 27 for £699, down from £719.

The One Console Deal That Exists: Certified Refurbished for $649.99

If you want an Xbox Series X below list price this Black Friday, there is exactly one official route, and it is not a sale price — it is a refurbished console. Microsoft's own store lists certified refurbished Series X consoles at $649.99, which is $150 less than the $799.99 price of a new disc-drive console, and the sale page describes refurbished units as the only console price cut in the whole event, at up to $150 off. A certified refurbished console from Microsoft is not a used console from a stranger: it has been inspected, cleaned, tested, and repackaged by Microsoft, and it carries a warranty. For a household that wants the full Series X experience, $649.99 is the cheapest official route to one during this sale.

Check two things before you buy refurbished. First, stock is limited and it moves fast, because every deal hunter in America has read the same advice. Second, buy from Microsoft's own store or another authorised seller, not a marketplace listing that uses the word "refurbished" loosely — a genuine Microsoft certified refurbished unit carries the same testing and warranty, while a third-party "refurbished" console at a similar price may not. If the refurbished stock sells out, your next best option is a retailer bundle, and after that, simply waiting. Cyber Monday on November 30 is unlikely to produce a console discount that Black Friday did not, because the same price increase and the same component costs will still be there on Monday morning.

2,000 Games Up to 80% Off: Where the Real Sale Lives

The heart of the Xbox Black Friday sale is the games catalogue, and it is genuinely big. From November 19 through December 2, Microsoft is discounting more than 2,000 Xbox and PC games by up to 80 percent. This is the part of the sale where the savings are real, immediate, and available to everyone, whether you already own a console or you are buying your first one. The discounts cover recent releases as well as older catalogue titles, and they apply to games you buy and own outright, not to subscription time.

The headline game deal is Gears of War: E-Day Premium Edition, down to $69.99 from $99.99 — a 30 percent cut on one of the year's biggest Xbox releases — and the premium upgrade alone is $21, down from $30. Beyond that single title, the sale follows the usual pattern of deep cuts on older games and moderate cuts on newer ones, which is exactly how a smart shopper should approach it: buy the games you have been waiting to play, not the games that were just released at full price. And here is the exception that proves the rule — the biggest game of the year is not on sale at all. GTA 6 costs $79.99 for the standard edition and $99.99 for the Ultimate Edition, with no discount in the Xbox sale. If you are buying an Xbox this week partly to play GTA 6, budget the full $79.99 and treat any other discount as the saving.

Controllers: The Best Xbox Deals of Black Friday 2026

If the console is not on sale, the controller is where the real hardware savings live, and this is the part of the sale most shoppers should act on first. The standard Xbox Wireless Controller in Carbon Black or Robot White is $49.06 at the Xbox store, down from $64.99. The Shock Blue and Pulse Red versions are $52.30. If you want a premium controller, the Elite Series 2 Core in white is $111.74, down from $149.99, a saving of more than $38. The Forza Horizon 6 Limited Edition controller is $74.99, down from $89.99.

The retailers have their own controller deals on top of Microsoft's prices, and the best of them is a bundle rather than a straight discount. Newegg is selling the Xbox Wireless Controller in Carbon Black together with a $50 Xbox gift card for $77.99, down from $114.99 if you bought both separately — a $37 saving. The deal runs through November 30 and is limited to five per customer. Third-party options are also discounted: the Razer Wolverine V3 Tournament Edition is $69.72, down from $99.99, the Nacon Revolution X Unlimited is $149.99, down from $199.99, and the GameSir G7 HE wired controller, which uses Hall Effect sticks that resist drift, is $44.99, down from $49.99. Buy one standard controller at $49.06 for everyday play — and a second one now if you play multiplayer on one console.

Game Pass Ultimate: Small Discounts, Real Value

Microsoft has not announced a Black Friday discount on Game Pass itself, and that is the second disappointment of this sale after the console. The join page shows a 14-day trial for $1, then $22.99 a month for Game Pass Ultimate, which is the regular price. But the retailers have stepped in where Microsoft did not. Newegg is selling a three-month Game Pass Ultimate membership, delivered by email, for $56.99, down from $68.99 — a $12 saving that works out to about $19 a month instead of $22.99. StackSocial has a one-month membership listed at $17.99 instead of $22.99, and a $100 Xbox gift card is selling for $90, which is effectively a 10 percent discount on anything you buy from the Xbox store, including Game Pass time and games.

Whether Game Pass is worth it depends on how you play. Game Pass Ultimate includes hundreds of games, day-one first-party releases, cloud gaming, and EA Play, and this year's sale catalogue marks the base games of Gears of War: E-Day, Forza Horizon 6, and Halo: Campaign Evolved as included in Ultimate, with Grounded 2 and Final Fantasy VII Remake Intergrade included in Premium. If you play three or more new games a year, the subscription pays for itself against buying those games outright, especially when the games cost $69.99 each. The smart move is to stack the discounts: buy the three-month membership at $56.99 to try Ultimate through the holidays, use the $90 gift card for the games you want to own, and check your plan before you buy any game, because many of the games on sale this week are already included in the subscription you may already be paying for.

Where to Shop: Every Store, Every Date

The dates matter this year, because the sale is not one event but several overlapping ones. Microsoft's own Xbox Black Friday sale runs from November 19 at 9 p.m. Pacific through December 2, with more deals added on November 23. The Microsoft Store's separate sale runs from November 19 to November 28 and is the one that includes the certified refurbished consoles. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs from November 19 through November 30, and it is where the standard controller has been spotted under $50. Best Buy's event started on November 19 and covers controllers and games, Newegg's controller-and-gift-card bundle runs through November 30, and the UK's Currys has the Series X and EA Sports FC 27 bundle at £699.

The practical plan is to check Microsoft's store first for the games and the refurbished console, then Amazon and Best Buy for controllers, then Newegg for the bundles. Prices change hourly during Black Friday week, and the listings quoted here were verified on November 25, so confirm the price at checkout before you buy. Stock is limited on the refurbished Series X, the $49.06 controller, and the Newegg bundle — buy those first.

Xbox vs PS5 vs Switch 2: The Console That Is Actually on Sale

Xbox is not the only console without a discount this year, and the comparison across all three tells you something important about the whole market. Sony did not discount the PlayStation 5 either: as we covered in our Black Friday PS5 deals post, the PS5 is $649.99, the Digital Edition is $599.99, and the Pro is $899.99, all full price after April's price rise — but Sony's accessory sale is strong, with the DualSense at $54.99, the PS VR2 $100 off, and a year of PlayStation Plus Premium at $107.19 for new members. Nintendo's Switch 2 is the only console with a real bundle deal: as we covered in our Black Friday Nintendo Switch 2 deals post, the Switch 2 is selling at $469 with the Mario Kart World bundle $50 off, and Nintendo's sale runs from November 22 through December 5. That makes the Switch 2 bundle the only genuine Black Friday console deal in America this year.

So which console should you buy? If you want the best value hardware deal this week, it is the Switch 2 bundle, because it is the only console actually discounted. If you want the most powerful console and the best subscription service, it is the Xbox Series X, and the refurbished unit at $649.99 is the way to get it below list price. If you already own a PlayStation, the PS5's accessory deals and its games sale are the strongest of the three. And if you want GTA 6, the honest answer is that you need a PlayStation 5 or an Xbox Series X or S, because the game is not on Switch, and you will pay full price for it on either console — $79.99 standard, $99.99 Ultimate.

How Gaming Blogs Are Turning This Sale Into Ad Revenue

This is a blog about making money from a website, so here is the part that matters most to the people who run gaming blogs: a Black Friday with no console discount is not a bad week for publishers. It is a better week than a normal one, because confusion is traffic. Millions of people are searching "Xbox Series X Black Friday deal" right now, and most of the results they find are either outdated, misleading, or silent on the question they actually asked. A blog that answers the question honestly — no, the console is not discounted, here is what is — earns the click, the trust, and the return visit, and it earns ad revenue on every page those readers open while they are deciding what to buy instead.

The playbook that works this week has five moves, and any gaming blog can run all five before Friday. First, publish the honest headline post — a title that says "No Console Discount" outperforms one that promises a deal that does not exist, because readers who feel misled do not come back. Second, publish the comparison post: "Xbox vs PS5 vs Switch 2 Black Friday" spikes every year, and this year the honest answer is interesting, because only one of the three consoles is actually discounted. Third, publish the specific deal lists, not the generic one: "Best Xbox games under $20" and "Best Xbox controllers under $50" are separate searches with separate audiences, and each one is a page that can rank on its own and carry its own ads. Fourth, update the posts daily, because prices change hourly during Black Friday week and a post with today's date and today's verified prices outranks a post from last week.

Should You Buy an Xbox This Black Friday?

The decision depends on which Xbox buyer you are. If you already own a Series X or Series S, this is the best Black Friday in years for you: buy the games, because 2,000 of them are up to 80 percent off, buy a second controller at $49.06, and consider the three-month Game Pass Ultimate membership at $56.99 if you want to play the new releases through the holidays. If you do not own an Xbox yet and you want one, buy the certified refurbished Series X at $649.99 if you can get it, because it is the only official console saving this year. If the refurbished stock is gone, buy the games and the controller now and buy the console in January, when the holiday demand has passed. If your budget is $500 and it will not move, buy the 512GB Series S — but understand that 512GB fills up fast, and you will probably want a storage expansion card sooner than you think. And if you are buying mainly for GTA 6, buy the console and the game together and budget the full $79.99 for the game, because it is not discounted anywhere — our GTA 6 launch coverage explains why the game is selling at full price and why that is unlikely to change soon.

What to Watch Before Cyber Monday

Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday is November 30, and there are three things to watch between now and then. First, watch the refurbished stock at Microsoft's store, because when it sells out it is gone, and it is the only console deal in the sale. Second, watch Amazon and Best Buy on Friday morning, because the controller prices move during the day and the $49.06 standard controller has been known to sell out by the afternoon in past years. Third, watch for the Cyber Monday additions: Microsoft adds more game deals on November 23 and the sale runs through December 2, so the catalogue on Monday may include titles that were not discounted on Friday. The Newegg controller-and-gift-card bundle ends on November 30, the Microsoft Store sale ends on November 28, and the Design Lab free engraving ends on December 2.

The Bottom Line

Microsoft did not discount the Xbox this Black Friday — the Series X is $799.99, the digital Series X is $749.99, the Series S starts at $499.99, and none of them carries a console discount after the August price increase — but the sale around the console is real and worth your money. The standard controller is $49.06, the Elite Series 2 Core is $111.74, more than 2,000 games are up to 80 percent off until December 2, a three-month Game Pass Ultimate membership is $56.99, and a certified refurbished Series X is $649.99 from Microsoft itself. Buy the refurbished console if you can get it, and buy the controller and the games while they are cheap. The console may not be on sale, but the Xbox experience is, and that is where the money is this week.
