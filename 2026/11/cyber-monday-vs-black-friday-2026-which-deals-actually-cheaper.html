Cyber Monday vs Black Friday 2026: AirPods Pro 3 $149, Dyson V9 $269.99, Switch 2 Bundle $499.99 — Which Deals Were Actually Cheaper

Black Friday is over, Cyber Monday is here, and millions of shoppers are asking the same question with their wallets still open: did I buy too early, or did I wait too long? Here is the honest answer for 2026 — for the biggest products of the year, the two days were mostly a tie. AirPods Pro 3 cost $149 on Black Friday and $149 on Cyber Monday. The Nintendo Switch 2 bundle was $499.99 on both days. The Dyson V9 was $269.99 on both days. Adobe Analytics expects Americans to spend $15.1 billion online on Cyber Monday, November 30, and $12.9 billion online on Black Friday, November 27 — but the biggest story of the weekend is not which day won. It is that the two days stopped being different. The deals started weeks ago, the prices barely moved between Friday and Monday, and the shoppers who saved the most were the ones who knew exactly which products change price and which ones never do. This is the full head-to-head comparison: twelve products, real prices from Amazon, Walmart, Target, Best Buy and Costco, and a clear verdict on every one — plus what the tie means for your wallet, and for the bloggers and creators who earn from this weekend.

The Short Answer: For Most Products, It Was a Tie — and That Is the News

If you bought the AirPods Pro 3 for $149 on Black Friday, you did not lose anything by skipping Cyber Monday. If you waited for Cyber Monday hoping the price would drop to $129, you waited for nothing — the price stayed at $149. The same is true of the Switch 2 bundle at $499.99, the Dyson V9 at $269.99, the MacBook Air M5 at $1,049 and the Sony WH-1000XM6 headphones at $348. Across the biggest tech products of the season, the Cyber Monday price was the Black Friday price, repeated. Gizmodo's deals team, which tracked Amazon's sale all week, put it plainly: most Amazon prices you see now should stay the same until Cyber Monday, because Amazon already made its last round of price changes on Monday before Black Friday. In past years, most Cyber Monday prices matched the Black Friday ones, and 2026 followed the same pattern.

The Numbers Behind the Weekend: $47.5 Billion in Five Days

Before the product-by-product comparison, it helps to see how big this weekend actually is, because the scale explains why the prices barely moved. Adobe Analytics, which tracks transactions on US retail websites, forecasts $275.1 billion in US online spending for the whole holiday season from November 1 to December 31, 2026 — up 6.7 percent from 2025. Cyber Week, the five days from Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday, is forecast to drive $47.5 billion of that, up 7.4 percent year over year, or about 17.3 percent of the entire season's online spending in just five days. Cyber Monday itself is expected to pass $15 billion in a single day for the first time ever — $15.1 billion, up 6.2 percent — while Black Friday is forecast at $12.9 billion, up 9.2 percent. Last year the actual numbers were $14.25 billion on Cyber Monday and $11.8 billion on Black Friday, so both days are setting records again. Thanksgiving Day, the quietest of the five, is still expected to bring in $6.9 billion online.

The most important number in Adobe's forecast is not the total, though. It is the growth gap: Black Friday is growing faster than Cyber Monday, 9.2 percent against 6.2 percent, because retailers pushed their deepest discounts earlier and earlier. Discounts averaged about 14 percent in early November, rose to 21 percent just before Thanksgiving, and peaked at around 30 percent during Cyber Week. In other words, Black Friday stopped being a single day and became a month, and Cyber Monday became the last day of that month rather than a separate event. When the discounts are spread across four weeks, the price on Friday and the price on Monday are set by the same spreadsheet, in the same week, by the same team — so of course they match.

Two other shifts shaped the weekend. First, the phone won. Adobe expects a record 63 percent of Thanksgiving Day online spending to come through mobile devices, and 57.4 percent across the full season. More than half of Cyber Monday's sales last year were placed on a phone. Second, artificial intelligence is now a shopping channel in its own right: Adobe expects traffic to US retail sites from AI tools to rise 130 percent this holiday season, with the biggest jump on Thanksgiving, up 159 percent, followed by Black Friday, up 95 percent, and Cyber Monday, up 88 percent. On Cyber Monday 2025, clicks from AI tools to retail sites rose 670 percent. Shoppers are asking chatbots what to buy, and the chatbots are sending them to the same product pages everyone else is reading. And the payment method changed too: buy now, pay later is forecast to drive $21.3 billion across November and December, including $807 million on Black Friday and more than $1 billion on Cyber Monday alone. A record 202.9 million Americans shopped between Thanksgiving and Cyber Monday last year, according to the National Retail Federation. This year, with spending up across the board, that record is expected to fall as well.

Twelve products, twelve ties. Not one of the biggest deals of the year got cheaper on Cyber Monday, and not one of them got more expensive either. That is the whole story of 2026 in a single table — and it is worth pausing on, because it breaks the rule every shopper learned over the last twenty years. The old rule was simple: Black Friday for TVs and consoles in stores, Cyber Monday for laptops and headphones online. The new rule is that the biggest retailers set one price for the whole Cyber Week and hold it. Amazon's sale runs straight through November 30. Walmart's sale runs in waves, with the third wave covering November 30 to December 2. Target's deals run through the weekend. When every store's sale is a week long, there is no "Friday price" and no "Monday price" — there is only the Cyber Week price.

Where Cyber Monday Actually Won

Cyber Monday did win somewhere — just not on the headline products. Its wins were quieter, and they were all online. The first win is software and subscriptions. Cyber Monday began in 2005 as an online-only event, and twenty-one years later it is still the day for the things you download rather than the things you carry home. Software, apps, streaming bundles, cloud storage, VPNs and online courses all run their deepest discounts on Monday, when the buyers are back at their desks and the sellers are chasing the office crowd. Our earlier Cyber Monday software guide covers that side of the day in detail, and the pattern held again in 2026: the Monday-only deals were overwhelmingly digital.

Another Cyber Monday win is Walmart's third wave. Walmart runs its holiday sale in three waves, and the third wave runs from November 30 to December 2 — Cyber Monday and the two days after it. That wave is the last big restock of the season, and it is online-only for many items. Shoppers who treat Cyber Monday as the end of the sale are wrong: for Walmart, it is the start of the last wave. The same is true in reverse for the small shops we covered in our Small Business Saturday guide — Saturday, November 28, belonged to the independents, and the big chains took the weekend back on Monday. The calendar is a relay race now, not a single sprint.

Where Black Friday Actually Won

Black Friday won the weekend in exactly the places where a physical store still beats a website. The first is the television aisle. TVs are the product where Black Friday's lead is biggest and oldest: the deepest TV discounts of the year have always been Friday doorbusters, and 2026 was no exception. Our Walmart Black Friday guide tracked the 65-inch Hisense 4K Roku TV at $228, down from $398, and the 55-inch TCL 4K Roku TV at $168. Best Buy countered with a 65-inch TCL television at $229.99, down $240, and an Insignia 40-inch Fire TV at $89.99. The Samsung S90H OLED was $1,397.99 and the LG C6 was $1,349.99. Costco's showstopper was a 98-inch TCL TV for $999.97 — a television the size of a wall, for less than a thousand dollars. None of those prices improved on Cyber Monday, and several of the cheapest TVs sold out in stores on Friday and never returned online. For TVs, Black Friday was the day, and the shoppers who waited until Monday found the shelves — virtual and real — already picked over.

Black Friday also won the flash deals. Best Buy ran a 24-hour Thanksgiving Day flash sale with a 4K TV at $89.99, $350 off the iPhone Air, and Sony headphones at $169.99 — deals that existed for one day only and vanished before Cyber Monday began. That is the second lesson of the weekend: the deals that disappear are the ones that were never meant to last. A flash deal is a marketing event, not a price. It exists to get you into the store or onto the app on a specific day, and once the day is over, the price is gone. If you see a one-day flash price on something you want, the correct move is to buy it then, because Cyber Monday will not bring it back.

How to Tell a Real Deal From a Fake One

After a weekend of identical prices, the skill that saves you the most money is not timing. It is telling a real deal from a fake one. The first test is the list price. A discount is only real if the "was" price is a price the product actually sold at. The AirPods Pro 3 list at $249, and they sold at $249 for months before the sale — so $149 is a real $100 saving. The Switch 2 bundle lists at $549.99 and sold at $549.99 — so $499.99 is a real $50 saving. But some stores raise the "was" price in November so the discount looks bigger than it is, and some products carry a list price nobody ever paid. If a product claims 70 percent off but you have never seen it at the list price, the discount is a story, not a saving.

The second test is the price history. Before you buy, check what the product cost last month, not just what it costs today. The Lenovo Chromebook at $189 looks like a 61 percent discount from $489, and it is — but only because the list price is the manufacturer's suggested price, and the street price before the sale was already lower. The saving is still real, but it is smaller than the headline suggests. The products with the most honest discounts this weekend were the ones with the most boring price histories: the same price for months, then a sharp, verifiable cut. AirPods Pro 3, Switch 2, Dyson V9 — all three had stable prices before the sale and a clean cut during it. That is what a real deal looks like.

What the Tie Means for Bloggers and Creators: The Money Lesson

This blog exists to answer one question: how does a website earn money? Cyber Monday 2026 gave the clearest answer of the year, and it is not the one most new bloggers expect. The money in this weekend was not in the price. It was in the comparison. Nobody searches for "AirPods Pro 3 $149" after they already know the price. They search for "AirPods Pro 3 vs AirPods 4", "Cyber Monday vs Black Friday", "is the Switch 2 bundle worth it" and "which Dyson should I buy". The search that earns is the question, not the answer — and the page that earns is the one that answers the question honestly, with real prices, real trade-offs and a clear verdict.

That is why comparison posts like this one outperform deal lists. A deal list says "AirPods Pro 3, $149" and the reader leaves in five seconds. A comparison says "AirPods Pro 3, $149 at three stores, $179.99 at Best Buy, same price on Friday and Monday, and here is why you should buy it today instead of waiting" — and the reader stays, reads, clicks and comes back. Time on page, return visits and trust are the currency of a monetized blog, and they are earned by doing the homework the reader does not have time to do: checking three stores, reading the price history, and saying plainly when the answer is "it does not matter which day you buy". The bloggers who earned the most this weekend were the ones who told their readers the truth about the tie, not the ones who shouted that Monday would be cheaper.

The other lesson is about what to do with the traffic once you have it. Cyber Week traffic is the most valuable traffic of the year, because the readers are buyers, not browsers. A reader who lands on a comparison post with a credit card in their hand is worth many times more than a reader who lands on a general article in July. That is why the ad placements on this site — the display units at the top and bottom of every post, the native banners inside the article, the footer links — matter most in this exact week. A blog that earns $0.03 from a thousand Bangladeshi impressions in an ordinary week can earn many times more from the same thousand impressions when they come from the United States during Cyber Week, because the advertisers are bidding for buyers, not just readers. Traffic from the top CPM countries — the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, France, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Sweden and Japan — is the whole game, and Cyber Week is when that game pays best. If you run a blog, this is the week to publish, to update old posts with fresh prices, and to make sure every page loads fast on a phone, because more than half of your readers are shopping on one.

After Cyber Monday: The Shopping Calendar Is Not Over

Cyber Monday is the biggest online shopping day of the year, but it is not the last one — and the shoppers who think the sales end on Monday will miss the rest of the season. The very next day, Tuesday, December 1, is Giving Tuesday, the day when the spending turns to giving: nonprofits, creators and small businesses run their biggest donation campaigns of the year, and the shoppers who spent the weekend buying for themselves spend Tuesday giving to others. For bloggers and creators, Giving Tuesday is the most under-covered day of the whole season — almost nobody writes about it, and the competition for the searches is nearly zero. Then comes the long tail of December: Green Monday in mid-December, the last big online shipping deadlines, and the final Saturday before Christmas, December 19, when the stores run their last doorbusters for the shoppers who waited until the very end. The discounts get smaller after Cyber Monday, but they do not stop, and the last-minute shoppers are the most desperate buyers of the year.

The Bottom Line

Cyber Monday 2026 did not beat Black Friday 2026, and Black Friday did not beat Cyber Monday. The AirPods Pro 3 were $149 on both days. The Switch 2 bundle was $499.99 on both days. The Dyson V9 was $269.99 on both days. Twelve products, twelve ties — because the two days stopped being different. Adobe expects $15.1 billion in online spending on Cyber Monday and $12.9 billion on Black Friday, but the real winner of the weekend was the shopper who checked three stores, checked the price history, and bought the thing they actually wanted at a real price. The store matters more than the day. The price history matters more than the headline discount. And the best deal of the weekend is the one you needed, bought once, at the right price — whether you bought it on Friday, on Monday, or on any day in between. Cyber Monday is November 30. The deals are already live. Go and buy the right thing.
