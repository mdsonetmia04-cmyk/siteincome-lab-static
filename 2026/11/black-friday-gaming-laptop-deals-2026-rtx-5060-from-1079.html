If your budget is under $800, this is the deal.

RTX 5050 vs RTX 5060 vs RTX 5070: Which GPU Is Actually Worth Your Money?

The graphics card decides how well a gaming laptop plays games, and this year the choice is simpler than the marketing makes it look. Nvidia's 50-series laptop cards — the RTX 5050, 5060, 5070, 5070 Ti and 5080 — all share the same Blackwell architecture, so they all support DLSS 4, the AI upscaling that generates extra frames and makes games run smoother than the raw hardware should allow. The difference is how much raw power they have, and how much you pay for it.

The RTX 5050 is the entry card. It plays esports titles like Valorant, League of Legends and Fortnite at high frame rates, and it handles AAA games at 1080p with medium settings. GamesRadar's Black Friday target price for a 5050 laptop is $1,100 to $1,200, and the deals above mostly land in that band. Buy a 5050 if your budget is under $1,200 and you mostly play competitive or older games. The RTX 5060 is the sweet spot, and it is where most buyers should land. It handles 1080p gaming comfortably, runs 1440p well in most titles, and with DLSS 4 it punches above its weight in demanding games. The market average for a 5060 laptop is $1,514.94, and this Black Friday you can buy one for $1,079.99. A 5060 laptop at $1,100 to $1,400 is the best value in gaming hardware right now, full stop.

The RTX 5070 is the step up for serious players. It makes 1440p gaming at high settings genuinely comfortable. The market average is $2,239.01, but GamesRadar's Black Friday target is $1,700 to $1,900 — and the Walmart OMEN at $1,299 blows that target away. If you can stretch to $1,300, the 5070 is the best upgrade you can buy this year. One warning: laptop graphics cards are not the same as desktop cards with the same name. A laptop RTX 5070 is slower than a desktop RTX 5070, because it runs at lower power to fit inside a thin chassis. Compare laptop prices to laptop prices, never to desktop prices, and check the wattage — a 5060 running at 115 watts will beat a 5070 running at 65 watts.

How to Spot a Fake Gaming Laptop Discount

Not every Black Friday gaming laptop deal is real. Some retailers inflate the "was" price so the discount looks bigger than it is, and some laptops are "on sale" at the same price they sold for all year. Here is how to tell the difference in under a minute. First, compare the price against the market average, not the crossed-out price. GamesRadar publishes Black Friday target prices every year, and for 2026 they are $1,100 to $1,200 for an RTX 5050 laptop, $1,400 to $1,600 for an RTX 5060, and $1,700 to $1,900 for an RTX 5070. A deal at or below the target is real. A deal far above the target with a huge percentage discount is usually fake — this year's example is an MSI Cyborg 15 advertised at $2,669 with 40 percent off a $4,449 list price, when its RTX 5070 puts the fair price at $1,700 to $1,900.

Second, check the price history: Amazon listings show a 30-day low badge when the current price is genuinely the lowest in a month, and the Acer Nitro V at $1,079.99 carries one. Third, watch for listing traps: some Amazon laptop listings in this year's sale have one review or none at all, on machines costing as much as $3,929, and others are sold by third-party resellers at marked-up prices. Always check the seller is Amazon, the manufacturer, or a named retailer you recognise. And remember the graphics card is only part of the machine: a laptop with an RTX 5070 but only 8GB of memory and a dim 250-nit screen is a worse buy than a 5060 machine with 16GB and a bright 300-nit display.

What to Check Before You Buy

A gaming laptop is a four-figure purchase, and five checks separate a good buy from a regret. First, memory: 16GB is the minimum in 2026, and 32GB is worth paying for if you keep laptops for four or five years — several of the best deals above already ship with 32GB. Second, storage: 512GB is barely enough, because a single modern game can take 100GB or more, so prefer 1TB and check for a second SSD slot. Third, the screen: look for at least 300 nits of brightness and a 144Hz or higher refresh rate, because a dim 60Hz screen wastes a good graphics card. Fourth, the battery: expect two to four hours of gaming away from the charger. Fifth, the warranty and return policy: Costco's return policy is the best in the business, and Amazon's holiday returns give you room to test properly.
