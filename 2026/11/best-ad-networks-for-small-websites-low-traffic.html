7 Best Ad Networks for Small Websites With Low Traffic (2026)

Your blog gets 300 visitors a day and earns exactly $0. Not because your content is bad — because every "best ad network" list you read assumes you already have 50,000 monthly visitors. The premium networks won't even look at you. Google AdSense will make you wait months for a $100 payout. And somewhere in the comments, someone is telling you to "just grow your traffic first."

Here is the truth nobody in those lists admits: you do not need big traffic to start earning. Several real ad networks accept brand-new sites with no traffic minimum at all — and one of them pays out from just $5. This post ranks the 7 best ad networks for small websites in 2026, on the four things that actually decide your income: entry requirement, payout minimum, reported RPM, and the honest catch. Every figure was checked in November 2026 against the networks' own pages or publisher guides citing them — and where a network doesn't publish a number, the table says so instead of inventing one.

How this list was made

Three rules, so you can trust the table:

  1. Every network here accepts a small site. Either it has no traffic minimum at all, or its bar is 1,000 sessions or 10,000 pageviews a month — reachable within a few months of consistent publishing. The networks that demand 25,000+ pageviews are listed separately at the end, as your next step, not your first.
  2. Entry requirements and payout minimums were checked on each network's own pages, or guides citing them, in October–November 2026. Re-check on the day you apply — these rules moved a lot in 2025–2026, and they will move again.
  3. RPMs are reported averages, not promises. Single figures ($2.34, $11.15) come from Productive Blogging's 2025 survey of 187 bloggers (self-reported, mostly US/UK); ranges ($1–4, $3–10) come from publisher guides. Your RPM depends on niche, country, season, and layout — a finance blog and a meme blog on the same network earn completely different money.

It is the publisher brand of Propeller Ads Ltd (PropellerAds moved its publisher business to Monetag in 2022; it now serves advertisers only).

3. Google AdSense — the safe baseline every small site should also try

  • Entry requirement: No traffic minimum. Every site is reviewed for original content, policy compliance, and a minimum age (18+ to hold the account). Approval can take days to weeks, and new or thin sites are routinely rejected. Guides tracking approvals in 2026 report that blogs with 15–25 substantial posts, an About page, a Contact page, a Privacy Policy, and an ads.txt file get approved fastest.
  • Best for: Your first network. It accepts almost any niche, pays reliably, and its ads are the least likely to hurt your user experience or your Google standing.
  • Payout minimum: $100, paid monthly once your balance clears it (earnings finalize around the 3rd, payment issued between the 21st and 26th).
  • Reported RPM: $2.34 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average). Publisher revenue share is 68% for content ads.
  • Honest limitation: The lowest RPM on this list, and the $100 floor means a small blog can wait months for its first payment — at a $2.34 RPM, a blog with 5,000 monthly pageviews earns about $12 a month, roughly eight months before payout. AdSense is also famous for sudden account bans with minimal explanation and no real appeal. Safest network, slowest-paying at the same time. 4. Media.net — the contextual specialist

  • Entry requirement: No published traffic minimum, but approval is a genuine quality review: high-quality English-language content and significant traffic from the US, UK, and Canada. Some guides cite an informal 10,000-pageview bar, but Media.net doesn't publish one — apply on content quality, not a number.
  • Best for: Content-heavy sites in finance, tech, and B2B with mostly Tier-1 traffic. It runs contextual ads from the Yahoo/Bing network, which is why it can out-earn AdSense on commercial US/UK traffic.
  • Payout minimum: $100, paid Net 30 via PayPal or wire.
  • Reported RPM: $3–10 on Tier-1 traffic (publisher guides); some finance/tech sites report higher.
  • Honest limitation: Outside Tier-1 traffic and commercial niches it earns little — a blog with mostly Tier-3 readers will do better on Adsterra or Monetag. The $100 floor is as slow as AdSense, and approval can reject without useful detail. Note: Media.net has repositioned itself in recent years as a supply-side platform doing managed header bidding, not just the Yahoo/Bing contextual network older guides describe — check its current offering before you apply.

5. Infolinks — the stackable extra

  • Entry requirement: None. No traffic minimum, easy approval, and it works on any site.
  • Best for: Stacking on top of your main network. Infolinks sells in-text ads (keywords in your content become ad links), in-fold, in-tag, and in-frame units — formats that use no traditional ad space, so they layer over AdSense, Adsterra, or a managed network without competing for the same slots.
  • Payout minimum: $50, paid Net 45 via PayPal, bank wire, ACH, or eCheck. Publisher revenue share is 70%.
  • Reported RPM: $1–3 (publisher guides).
  • Honest limitation: The lowest RPMs here — supplemental income, not a main network. In-text ads can annoy readers, and the $50 floor is high for what it earns. 6. Journey by Mediavine — the premium on-ramp for small blogs

  • Entry requirement: 1,000 sessions from Tier-1 countries (US, Canada, UK, Australia) within 30 days, original audience-first content, and Mediavine's free Grow plugin running on your site for at least 30 days. Ad partners also want about four months of domain history. (Verified against Mediavine's requirements page, modified October 6, 2026.)
  • Best for: Small blogs with a real US/UK/CA/AU audience that want premium-network RPMs years earlier than the old rules allowed. It is self-supported (no dedicated account manager) with a built-in path to full Mediavine once your site reaches $5,000 in annual ad revenue.
  • Payout minimum: $25 (reported — Mediavine's own pages don't publish a Journey minimum), paid Net 65. Publisher revenue share is 70%.
  • Reported RPM: $11.15 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average) — roughly 5× AdSense's reported average.
  • Honest limitation: Only Tier-1 sessions count — a blog with 3,000 monthly sessions that are 70% Tier-3 does not qualify. It requires WordPress (the Grow plugin), so Blogspot sites can't use it directly, and Net 65 means waiting over two months for your first payment. 7. Monumetric Propel — managed ads from 10,000 pageviews (with a fee)

  • Entry requirement: 10,000–80,000 pageviews a month for its Propel program (the range must hold for three consecutive months, per its program overview). The site must be WordPress or Blogger, have a sidebar at least 300px wide, room for at least six ad slots, and at least 50% of traffic from the US, UK, Canada, or Australia.
  • Best for: Small blogs that want a managed network to install and optimize ads for them, without waiting for 25,000 pageviews.
  • Payout minimum: $10, paid Net 60 (within the first 10 business days of the month). Above 80,000 pageviews you move to Ascend with no setup fee. Revenue share is not published (some guides report ~70% at Propel).
  • Reported RPM: $6.77 per 1,000 pageviews (Productive Blogging 2025 survey average).
  • Honest limitation: The only network here that charges you to join — a one-time $99 setup fee under 80,000 pageviews. Six ad slots is a heavy commitment for a small blog, Net 60 is the slowest payout here, and at 10,000 pageviews $99 takes months to earn back. Two warnings that save more money than any network switch:

  1. Don't switch for a $1 RPM difference at low traffic. At 5,000 pageviews a month, a $2 to $4 RPM jump is worth $10 a month; growing to 20,000 pageviews is worth $40+ at the same RPM. Content growth beats network-hopping until you're past 25,000 pageviews.   2. Check the exclusivity clause. Managed networks (Raptive, Mediavine) require exclusivity — your only display ad source. Self-serve networks can usually run on separate placements, but always read the terms: signing an exclusive deal, then running a second network's ads, is the fastest way to get terminated.

Honest pros and cons of starting small

Pros

  • Faster first payout: Adsterra and Monetag pay from $5; AdSense makes you wait for $100. For a small blog, that is the difference between seeing money in month one and month six.
  • Higher RPMs exist early: Journey ($11.15) and Monumetric ($6.77) both reported multiples of AdSense's $2.34 average — if you qualify and your audience is Tier-1.
  • Traffic that premium networks ignore still pays: Adsterra and Monetag monetize Tier-2/Tier-3 traffic and non-English audiences that premium networks won't take.
  • Approval is faster elsewhere: Adsterra approves in ~10 minutes; AdSense can take weeks and rejects thin sites outright.

Cons

  • AdSense is the safest network: Google's ads are the least likely to hurt your user experience, your Core Web Vitals, or your standing with Google itself. Aggressive formats (popunder, push) earn more per visitor and cost you readers.
  • Premium RPMs need premium traffic: The networks that pay $11+ RPMs demand 1,000 Tier-1 sessions or 10,000 pageviews — and most new blogs are a year away from that.
  • Some alternatives charge you: Monumetric's $99 setup fee is real money before you've earned a cent.
  • Switching has hidden costs: new ad code, layout re-testing, exclusivity clauses, payout schedules that restart your waiting clock. A switch is a project, not a toggle.
