Why Robot Vacuums Are the Strongest Deal Category of Black Friday 2026

An early sweep of roughly 3,500 Amazon products during Black Friday Week found robot vacuums to be the strongest deal category of the entire sale, with most models discounted 40 to 50 percent and several self-emptying docks included at those prices. Amazon's own sale preview names iRobot and Bissell at up to 40 percent off in floor care, but the real-world prices are better than the preview suggests: discounts reach 53 percent on some of the best-known models. Bob Vila's coverage of the sale, published November 20, 2026, reports robot vacuums and mop combos from Ecovacs, Roborock, Shark, iRobot, Dreame, Eufy, and Narwal marked down by up to 53 percent, with self-emptying models starting at $199.99.

That matters because the self-emptying dock is the feature that decides whether a robot vacuum actually gets used. A robot you must empty after every run becomes a chore with extra steps. A robot that empties itself into a dock you touch once every seven to nine weeks becomes something you forget about — which is the entire point. This year, for the first time, that feature is available at budget prices. The Roborock Q7 M5+, which pairs LiDAR navigation with 10,000 Pa of suction and a self-emptying dock, is $199.99, down from $359.99. That is a premium feature set at a price that was unheard of two years ago.

The timing is unusually good for buyers. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs a full twelve days, November 19 to November 30, so the deals are live right now rather than locked behind a single Friday. But the best prices are not guaranteed to survive the whole window: the deepest prices appear early in the week, the most popular models sell out before Black Friday itself, and prices bounce back up between sale stages. If you see the model you want at the price you want, waiting for a better one is usually the wrong move.

Best Budget Robot Vacuums Under $200

The under-$200 tier is where Black Friday 2026 is most generous, and it is also where most first-time buyers should look. Three models stand out, and they cover three different kinds of buyer.

Roborock Q7 L5 — $139.99 (was $249.99)

The Q7 L5 is the cheapest robot vacuum on this list, at $139.99 on Amazon — 44 percent off its $249.99 list price — and Roborock's own US site lists it at $119.99 as of November 20. It is a vacuum-and-mop combo you empty yourself, with 8,000 Pa HyperForce suction, a dual anti-tangle system, and three levels of water flow for mopping. For a small apartment or a single-floor home with mostly hard floors, this is the deal of the sale. The trade-off is the manual emptying — you are the dock — and the absence of LiDAR navigation, which means it cleans in a less efficient pattern than its pricier siblings. But at this price, that is a fair trade.

iRobot Roomba 105 Vac — $149.99 (was $299.99)

The Roomba 105 Vac, reported at $149.99 at Target, is the cheapest way into the Roomba range: a simple vacuum-only robot for flats and smaller homes, at half its $299.99 list price. Buying a Roomba is, for many people, buying the brand they already trust — iRobot invented this category, and its app, mapping, and customer support are the most polished in the business. The 105 Vac is deliberately simple: it vacuums, it navigates your home, it goes back to its dock. It does not mop, and it does not empty itself. What it does, it does reliably. If you have pets and hard floors and you want a robot that just works without a learning curve, this is the one.

Roborock Q7 M5+ — $199.99 (was $359.99)

The Q7 M5+ is the model we would recommend to most people, and it is the best value self-emptying pick of the entire sale. At $199.99 — $160 off its $359.99 list price — it gives you LiDAR navigation, 10,000 Pa of suction, and a self-emptying dock that holds seven to nine weeks of dust before you need to touch it. That combination used to cost $400 or more. The self-empty dock matters most of all, because it is the difference between a gadget you use daily and a gadget that ends up in a closet. One warning: some deal trackers list this model at $249.99, so the $199.99 price may be limited-time or store-specific. If you see it at $199.99, that is the price to grab.

Best Self-Emptying Mid-Range Deals

Between $250 and $450 sits the sweet spot for most family homes: self-emptying docks, real mopping, and enough suction for carpet and pet hair. Four models compete here, and the differences between them are about your floors, not about quality.

iRobot Roomba 105 Combo with AutoEmpty Dock — $269.99 (was $469.99)

The Roomba 105 Combo with AutoEmpty Dock, reported at $269.99 against a $469.99 list price, vacuums and mops in one run and empties itself into its dock, so you never touch a dusty bin. This is the best pick in the sale for pet owners who want the Roomba ecosystem. The combo design means one pass handles both dry dirt and wet messes, and the AutoEmpty dock means the robot is genuinely autonomous for weeks at a time. Another check of the same model at a different store showed $237.48 against a $449.99 list price, so the street price is settling somewhere in the $240 to $270 range. Either way, you are saving roughly $200 on a robot that does the two jobs most households actually need.

Roborock Q10 S5+ — $279.98 (was $549.99)

The Roborock Q10 S5+, at $279.98 against a $549.99 list price, is 49 percent off and adds an auto-lifting mopping system: the mop lifts itself when the robot crosses carpet, so your rugs stay dry while your hard floors get washed. It pairs 10,000 Pa suction with the same self-empty dock approach as the Q7 M5+. If your home mixes hard floors and rugs — which most homes do — the auto-lifting mop is the feature that stops you from having to babysit the robot.

Roborock Qrevo S Pro — $379.99 (was $699.99)

The Qrevo S Pro, at $379.99 against a $699.99 list price, is the entry point into Roborock's premium Qrevo line. The difference between a Q-series robot and a Qrevo robot is the dock: Qrevo docks wash the mop pads, dry them, and refill the water tank, so the robot maintains itself between runs. At $379.99, you are getting a self-maintaining mop system for the price a basic self-emptying robot cost last year. For homes with large areas of hard flooring, this is the point where mopping stops being a gimmick and starts being genuinely useful.

Roomba Max 715 vs Roomba Plus 415X — both $399.99

Two Roombas share the $399.99 price point. The Roomba Max 715 Vac Robot with AutoEmpty Dock is $399.99, down from $699.99 — a vacuum-only robot with a self-emptying dock, aimed at homes that are mostly carpet. The Roomba Plus 415X Combo, also $399.99 against a $799.99 list price, is 50 percent off and adds mopping with an AutoWash dock. Same price, different trade: the Max 715 gives you stronger vacuum performance, while the Plus 415X gives you the mop. Mostly carpet and pets? Take the Max 715. Mostly hard floors? Take the Plus 415X.

Shark and Eufy: The Quiet Bargains

Roborock and iRobot dominate the headlines, but two other brands are offering some of the deepest percentage discounts in the sale — and they are the brands many shoppers overlook.

Shark AI Ultra with Self-Empty Base — $259.99 (was $549.99)

The Shark AI Ultra Robot Vacuum with Self-Empty Base is $259.99, down from $549.99 — a 53 percent cut, tied for the deepest percentage discount in the sale. Shark's strength is pet hair: its self-cleaning brush roll is designed to stop hair wrapping around the brush, which is the single most annoying maintenance job on any robot vacuum. If you have a shedding dog, this feature matters more than any suction number.

Shark Navigator Robot RV2110 — $199.99 (was $299.99)

The Shark Navigator Robot RV2110, at $199.99 against a $299.99 list price, is the simpler Shark: a straightforward robot for pet hair in smaller homes, without the self-empty base. It is the right pick if you want Shark's hair-handling brush design but do not want to pay for the dock.

Eufy C28 — $379.99 (was $799.99)

The Eufy C28 Robot Vacuum and Mop is $379.99, down from $799.99 — also 53 percent off. Eufy is Anker's smart-home brand, and its robots have a reputation for quiet operation and straightforward apps. The C28 is a vacuum-and-mop combo at a price that, two years ago, would have bought you a basic vacuum-only model.

How to Pick the Right Robot Vacuum (Without Overpaying)

Five questions decide the right pick, and they are all about your home, not the spec sheet. How much carpet do you have? Mostly carpet means prioritizing suction and a self-emptying dock — the Roomba Max 715 or the Roborock Q7 M5+. Mostly hard floors means the mop system matters more, and a Qrevo model is worth the step up. Do you have pets? Then you need a brush design that resists tangles — Shark's self-cleaning brush roll or Roborock's dual anti-tangle system — and a self-emptying dock, because a pet home fills a small dustbin in a single run. How big is your home? A small apartment does not need LiDAR navigation; the Q7 L5 at $139.99 will clean it perfectly well. A large multi-room home does need LiDAR, and it needs a dock that empties and refills itself. Will you actually maintain it? Be honest: if you will not wash mop pads by hand every week, buy a robot whose dock washes them for you. Black Friday is the one week when the self-maintaining models cost what the basic models usually cost. One final caution: check the model number carefully before you buy. Q7 L5, Q7 M5+, Q10 S5+, Qrevo S Pro, Qrevo 2 Pro — the differences are real, and a deal on the wrong model is not a deal.

Mistakes to Avoid

Prices bounce — the same model can show $199.99 in the morning and $249.99 in the evening, so if you see the price you want, buy it. Check the seller: buy from Amazon itself or the brand's official store, not a third-party marketplace seller with a price that looks too good to be true. Ignore the discount percentage and look at the actual price. And do not buy a robot vacuum you do not need just because it is cheap — a $139.99 robot is only a bargain if it cleans floors you actually have.
