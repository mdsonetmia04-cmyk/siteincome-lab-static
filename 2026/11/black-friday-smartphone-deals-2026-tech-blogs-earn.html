Black Friday 2026 is five days away, and the smartphone is the product every American shopper is searching for first. The year's biggest phone discounts are already live at Amazon, Best Buy, and Samsung, with flagship models cut by hundreds of dollars and carriers offering up to $1,200 off with a trade-in. For tech bloggers, that mix of huge demand and huge discounts makes phone deal coverage the highest-earning content a small publisher can write this week.

This guide does two things at once. First, it rounds up the real Black Friday 2026 smartphone deals — every price below was checked against live retailer pages on November 22, 2026. Second, it shows how a tech blog turns those deals into revenue: which ad formats earn, where to place them, and how to keep the post earning through Cyber Monday on November 30.

Why Smartphones Are the Biggest Money Post of Black Friday Week

Every Black Friday, one product category drives more searches than all the rest combined, and in 2026 that category is the smartphone. There are three reasons, and understanding them is the first step to earning from them.

First, the phone is the one purchase almost every household makes on a predictable cycle. People replace their phones every two to three years, and millions of those replacement cycles land in November because shoppers have learned that Black Friday is when the best prices appear. A laptop is a considered purchase a household makes once every five years. A phone is a purchase a huge slice of the country is already planning, and the only question is which model and which store.

Second, the discounts are genuinely large. A Galaxy S26 Ultra at roughly $450 off its list price is a saving a shopper can feel. An iPhone Air at $300 off changes a buying decision. When the discount is that big, shoppers search harder, compare more pages, and click more links — and every one of those clicks is inventory a blog can monetize.

The numbers behind the season back this up. Adobe forecasts that Americans will spend $12.9 billion online on Black Friday alone, as part of a $275.1 billion holiday season. Phones sit at the center of that spending because they are the product where the discount, the search demand, and the buyer intent all peak at the same moment.

The 2026 Calendar: When Every Sale Actually Starts

Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, November 27, and Cyber Monday follows on Monday, November 30. But the sales started more than a week early, and the exact dates matter because the best prices do not always wait for Friday.

Amazon's Black Friday sale began on Thursday, November 19, at 3:01 a.m. ET, and runs through Cyber Monday, November 30. Amazon is running "double drops" of fresh deals at midnight and 8 a.m. PST on both November 27 and November 30, which means the deepest discounts may appear twice — once on Black Friday itself and once on Cyber Monday morning. A blog that only updates on Friday will miss the second wave.

Best Buy's sale also began on November 19, running for ten days through November 28. Best Buy stores are closed on Thanksgiving, with an online-only 24-hour flash sale that day, and on Black Friday most stores open at 6 a.m. and close at 10 p.m. One detail worth flagging for readers: on November 27, My Best Buy members can buy a $100 Best Buy gift card for $60 in stores, which is effectively free money for anyone already shopping there.

Target's Black Friday sale starts on Sunday, November 22, with stores opening at 6 a.m. local time on Black Friday. Walmart's sale begins on Monday, November 23. Apple, as always, does not cut its own prices: from November 27 to 30, Apple's own store offers a gift card worth up to $300 with eligible products instead of a discount. The real iPhone discounts live at Best Buy, Amazon, and the carriers, not at apple.com.

Samsung Leads the Sale: The Best Galaxy Deals Live Now

Samsung owns the deepest unlocked-phone discounts of Black Friday 2026, and the Galaxy S26 FE is the headline deal of the entire sale. The 128GB Galaxy S26 FE is $499.99 at Best Buy, Amazon, and Samsung — $200 off its $699.99 list price, a 29 percent cut, with no trade-in and no membership required. The 256GB model is $599.99, down from $799.99, saving the same $200. Deal trackers call it the best price ever seen for this 2026 release, and the phone itself is a strong package: a 6.7-inch screen, a large 4,900mAh battery, and a triple rear camera system.

The flagship Galaxy S26 Ultra is discounted almost as aggressively. The 256GB model has been selling for roughly $949.99 at both Best Buy and Amazon, down from its $1,399.99 list price — a cut of about $450. The 512GB model is $1,049.99 at Best Buy, $350 off the same $1,399.99 comparable value, while Samsung's own store is offering $350 off the Ultra outright. For readers who want the best phone Samsung makes, this is the lowest price of the year, available unlocked with no trade-in.

The foldables are discounted too, which matters because foldables are usually the last phones to go on sale. The Galaxy Z Fold 8 in 256GB is $1,549.99 at Best Buy and Amazon, $350 off its $1,899.99 list price. The Galaxy Z Flip 8, the clamshell foldable, is $200 off at Amazon. The Galaxy S26+ in 512GB is $300 off. And for shoppers on a tighter budget, the Galaxy S25 Edge in 256GB is $400 off at $700, while the budget Galaxy A17 5G starts at $245 at Best Buy.

iPhone Deals: Carriers Pay, Retailers Cut, Apple Gives Gift Cards

Apple never discounts iPhones in its own store, so the iPhone story of Black Friday 2026 is really two stories: what the carriers are offering, and what the retailers are cutting.

The carrier story is the bigger number. AT&T and T-Mobile are both offering up to $1,200 off the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max with an eligible trade-in, and the same $1,200 off the dual-screen iPhone Duo. At AT&T, the iPhone 18 Pro can be had at no cost with an eligible trade-in. T-Mobile's offer works with a trade-in in any condition on an eligible unlimited plan. Verizon is bundling rather than discounting: its iPhone 18 Pro offer adds a free Apple Watch SE 3 and AirPods 5 on top of the phone for customers on its Simplicity Plan, though the AirPods require adding the watch with its own service line.

Readers need the honest warning that belongs next to every one of those numbers: carrier discounts arrive as monthly bill credits spread over 36 months, not as a lower price at checkout. T-Mobile's own terms spell that out. A reader who cancels early loses the remaining credits. A good deal post says that plainly, because a reader who feels misled never comes back, and a reader who trusts the post clicks the next deal too.

The retailer story is simpler and, for many shoppers, better. Best Buy has the 256GB iPhone Air unlocked at $800, $300 off its $1,099 list price, with the 512GB at $1,000 (down from $1,299) and the 1TB at $1,400 (down from $1,699). Best Buy is also selling "excellent" condition open-box iPhone Air units for as much as $799 under the list price — the 256GB open-box in Space Black is $696 — which is worth a line for bargain hunters who do not mind an opened box.

Google Pixel and the Rest of the Android Field

Samsung and Apple take the headlines, but Google's Pixel 11 Pro is also discounted at Amazon this Black Friday, and it deserves a place in the roundup for a simple reason: Pixel buyers are comparison shoppers. They read reviews, compare cameras, and visit more pages before buying than any other phone audience. A Pixel section brings in readers who click through to three or four pages on the same blog, which is exactly the behavior that raises ad revenue per visit.

The rest of the Android field follows the same pattern as Samsung: unlocked prices cut outright, no trade-in needed, at Amazon and Best Buy. The Nothing Phone 4a Pro in 128GB is $397.99 at Best Buy, 20 percent off. Budget phones from $245 upward give the post a price range that starts low enough to catch the "cheap phone Black Friday" searches, which are some of the highest-volume phone queries of the season.

Carrier Deals vs Unlocked Deals: The Comparison Readers Actually Need

The single most useful section in a phone deal post is the one that explains the difference between a carrier deal and an unlocked deal, because most shoppers do not understand it — and the ones who do are the ones who share the post.

An unlocked deal is a lower price at checkout. The Galaxy S26 FE at $499.99 is $499.99, paid once, and the phone belongs to the buyer outright. It works on any carrier, it can be resold at any time, and there is no monthly bill attached to the discount. The saving is smaller on paper than the carrier offers, but it is real money, today, with no strings.

A carrier deal is a larger number spread over time. Up to $1,200 off sounds like a free phone, but it arrives as 36 monthly bill credits, it requires a trade-in, and it usually requires a specific unlimited plan. The total saving can genuinely be larger than any unlocked discount — a free iPhone 18 Pro is a free iPhone 18 Pro — but it locks the buyer to one carrier for three years, and leaving early means losing the remaining credits.

The honest advice is simple: if the reader is happy with their carrier and plans to stay for three years, the carrier deal is usually the better deal. If the reader wants freedom, wants to switch carriers, or wants to sell the phone next year, the unlocked deal is the better deal. A blog that gives that advice plainly earns the reader's trust, and trust is what turns a one-time Black Friday visitor into a subscriber who returns for the next sale.

How a Tech Blog Actually Earns From Phone Deal Traffic

Traffic is not income. A phone deal post that gets 20,000 visitors and earns nothing has failed, and the reason it failed is almost always the same: the blog treated the deal post like a news article instead of a revenue page. Here is how the earning actually works.

Display ads are the first revenue stream, and they work best on deal posts because the readers are buyers. Advertisers pay more to reach people who are about to spend money, so the same 1,000 visitors earn more on a phone deal post than on a general tech news post. The format matters: a 300x250 banner placed right after the second paragraph catches readers while they are still deciding, and a second 300x250 after about forty percent of the article catches the readers who scrolled past the first deal and are still comparing. A 728x90 leaderboard at the top and a 160x600 skyscraper below the article add revenue without interrupting the reading experience, and a popunder fires once per visit and earns from readers who would otherwise leave without clicking anything. The rule is to use several formats, but never so many that the page feels like an ad with an article attached — one aggressive format too many, and the bounce rate climbs faster than the revenue.

Affiliate links are the second stream, and on phone deal posts they can out-earn display ads. The post itself is a buying guide, so a link that says "check the price at Best Buy" is not an interruption — it is the next step the reader was already going to take. The key is to link the store name in the deal itself, not in a separate "buy here" button at the bottom that nobody scrolls to. Every deal in this post follows that rule: the price, the store, and the link belong together in the same sentence.

The Verification Rule: Never Publish a Price You Have Not Checked

Deal posts live or die on accuracy, and Black Friday is the worst week of the year for getting a price wrong, because prices move every day. The Galaxy S26 FE started this sale at $150 off and was improved to $200 off within days. A post that still shows the old figure is not just wrong — it is visibly wrong to any reader who clicks through, and that reader will not trust the next post.

The rule is simple and non-negotiable: every price in the post must be checked against a live retailer page or a deal tracker that cites one, on the day of publication, and re-checked every morning the post is live. If a price cannot be verified, it does not go in the post. If a deal has expired, it comes out the same day. If a price has dropped further, the post is updated the same day, because a lower price is news and news brings traffic back.

The Update Schedule: One Post, Ten Days of Earnings

A deal post is not a one-day article. The best-performing deal posts in this series are updated four times across the sale, and each update is a new chance to rank, a new reason for Google to recrawl the page, and a new wave of traffic.

Update one happens on the morning of Black Friday, November 27. Check every price in the post, change the ones that moved, and add any deal that appeared overnight — Amazon's double drop at midnight is the most likely source of new prices. Change the intro line to say the prices were verified that morning, because a timestamp that says "updated November 27" tells both Google and the reader that the post is current.

Update three happens on Cyber Monday morning, November 30, when Amazon runs its second double drop. This is the biggest update of the sale, because Cyber Monday prices are often different from Black Friday prices: some deals get better while others disappear. Change the headline to reflect Cyber Monday, keep the Black Friday prices that are still live, and add the new ones.

Update four is the quiet one: on December 1, change the post's framing from a live deal roundup to a record of the sale, and link forward to the next sale post. The post keeps ranking for "Black Friday phone deals" searches for weeks after the sale, and a reader who lands on it in December should find a useful summary and a link to the next live deals, not a page full of expired prices. Five Mistakes That Kill a Phone Deal Post

The first mistake is publishing prices from last year. Every November, blogs republish their old Black Friday post with the year changed and the prices untouched, and every November those posts rank for a day and then collapse when readers report the prices are wrong. A deal post is only as good as its newest price.

The second mistake is hiding the price. Some bloggers write three paragraphs of introduction before the first dollar figure appears, worried that leading with a price looks cheap. It is the opposite: the reader searched for a price, and the blog that shows it first wins the click, the trust, and the ranking. Lead with the price, every time.

The third mistake is covering too many phones. A roundup of thirty phones with one line each is a list, not a guide, and Google treats it like one. Five to eight verified deals, each with a price, a saving, a store, and one sentence of context, will outrank a list of thirty every time.

The fourth mistake is ignoring the carriers. A post that only covers unlocked phones misses half the search traffic and all of the biggest headline numbers. The carrier deals deserve their own section, with the bill-credit warning stated plainly, because the readers searching for carrier deals are the readers closest to buying.

What to Buy: A Quick Decision Guide

For readers who want the short version, here is the decision in four lines. If you want the best value phone of the sale, buy the Galaxy S26 FE at $499.99 — the biggest discount on the most complete phone at the lowest price. If you want the best phone Samsung makes, buy the Galaxy S26 Ultra at roughly $949.99 for the 256GB model. If you want an iPhone without a carrier contract, buy the iPhone Air at $800 from Best Buy. And if you are happy to stay with your carrier for three years, take the trade-in deal on the iPhone 18 Pro and let the bill credits do the work.
