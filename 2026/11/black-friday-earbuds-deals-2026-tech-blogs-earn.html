The single best Black Friday deal in audio this year is not a speaker, a soundbar, or a pair of over-ear headphones. It is a pair of earbuds that fits in your pocket. Wireless earbuds have quietly become the perfect Black Friday product. Almost everyone already owns a pair and knows which model they want; when the price drops, they just need to find the deal first. Why Earbuds Are the Hottest Audio Deal of Black Friday 2026

Wireless earbuds are the most-used piece of personal technology most people own after their phone. They are the thing people wear on the commute, during the workout, on the work call, and on the flight home for Thanksgiving. A pair bought two or three years ago has a tired battery, a case that no longer holds a charge the way it used to, and noise cancelling that was good for its time but is now a generation behind. Black Friday is the moment millions of people finally replace them. Retailers know earbuds are a gift that almost never misses: they fit everyone, need no sizing, and work with the phone the recipient already owns. A $149 pair of AirPods Pro 3 in a stocking feels like a $249 gift, and that giftability is why deal pages for earbuds convert so well.

The 10 Best Black Friday Earbuds Deals of 2026

1. Apple AirPods Pro 3 — $149 (Regular $249, Save $100)

This is the deal of Black Friday 2026, in audio and arguably in tech as a whole. The AirPods Pro 3, Apple's newest flagship earbuds, are $149 at Amazon and Walmart, down from $249. That is a 40 percent discount, the lowest price the earbuds have ever reached, and $50 less than they sold for on Prime Day. Best Buy has them at $170, which is still a strong price if Amazon sells out again. The deal has already gone out of stock once at Amazon and come back, so if you see it live, do not wait for a better price. There will not be one. For $149 you get Apple's best noise cancellation ever in earbuds, fuller sound with better bass, a built-in heart rate sensor, and live translation, with a USB-C case and redesigned ear tips. For iPhone owners there is no better earbud at any price.

2. Sony WF-1000XM6 — $248 (Regular $329.99, Save About $82)

Sony's flagship earbuds are the choice for listeners who care about sound quality above everything else, and they are $248 at Amazon and Best Buy right now, down from $329.99. Some listings show $249.99, which is the same deal rounded differently. Either way, it is roughly $80 off Sony's best earbuds, and the first serious discount on a model that launched as the company's new flagship. The XM6 continues Sony's tradition of the richest, most detailed sound in the category, with improved drivers, better noise cancelling, and a more comfortable fit. Android users should look here first: Sony's app gives them the full feature set, including the detailed equalizer.

3. Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (2nd Gen) — $229 (Regular $299, Save $70)

If noise cancelling is the feature you care about most, stop here. The Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds in their second generation are $229 at Amazon, Best Buy, and Bose itself, down from $299. That is $70 off, and it puts the best noise cancelling you can buy in a pair of earbuds within reach of the mid-range. CNN's audio team, which has tested these earbuds head to head against every rival, calls them the best active noise cancellation of any earbuds they have tested, and says they are perfect for holiday travel. The second generation adds better call quality, more reliable on-head detection, and USB-C audio playback for wired listening from a laptop or in-flight system. At $229 they cost $80 more than the AirPods Pro 3 deal, but for a frequent flyer or commuter, the extra silence is worth it.

4. Samsung Galaxy Buds 4 Pro — $170 (Regular $250, Save $80)

Samsung's newest flagship earbuds are $170 at Amazon right now, down from $250 — an $80 discount and an all-time low for a model that launched this year. Some deal trackers list them between $169.99 and $179 depending on the retailer, but the Amazon price of $170 is the one to beat. For Galaxy owners these are the natural pick, with instant pairing, Samsung's high-quality codecs, and deep ecosystem integration. At $170 they undercut the AirPods Pro 3's regular price by $79 for a comparable flagship experience on Android.

5. Beats Powerbeats Pro 2 — $179 (Regular $249, Save $70)

For runners, gym-goers, and anyone who has ever lost an earbud mid-workout, the Powerbeats Pro 2 are the answer, and they are $179 at Amazon right now, down from $249. Some listings show $179.95, which is the same deal. These earbuds are built differently from every other pair on this list: they hook over the ear, so they stay put through sprints, burpees, and heavy lifts no matter how much you sweat. The second generation adds a heart rate sensor, stronger noise cancelling, and a slimmer design, with big punchy bass and a battery that lasts a full week of training sessions per case charge. If your earbuds need to survive the gym, these are the ones.

6. Beats Studio Buds+ — $90 (Regular $169.99, Save About $80)

The best value in the entire sale might be the cheapest deal on this list. The Beats Studio Buds+ are $90 at Amazon, Best Buy, and Target, down from $169.99 — a lowest-ever price and nearly half off. CNN's audio team calls them a fantastic all-around pair and a strong AirPods Pro alternative. You get noise cancelling, transparency mode, and good sound that works with both iPhone and Android. At $90, you can buy two pairs for less than the regular price of one pair of AirPods Pro 3.

7. Sony WF-1000XM5 — $180 (Regular $299.99, Save About $120)

Last year's Sony flagship is this year's bargain. The WF-1000XM5 are down to $180 at Amazon, an all-time low and roughly $120 off their original price. These were the best-sounding earbuds on the market when they launched, and the only reason they are $180 instead of $248 is that Sony replaced them with the XM6. If your budget tops out around $180, the XM5 is the pick — and when the remaining stock is gone, it is gone for good.

8. Bose Ultra Open Earbuds (2nd Gen) — $249 (Regular $299, Save $50)

Not everyone wants to seal the world out. Runners who need to hear traffic, parents who need to hear their children, and office workers who need to hear a colleague all want earbuds that let the world in while the music plays. The Bose Ultra Open Earbuds in their second generation do exactly that, clipping gently onto the ear instead of sealing into it, and they are $249 at Amazon and Best Buy right now, down from $299 — $50 off just months after they hit stores. CNN's audio team notes they sound fantastic and hold up great during workouts, and the open design means no pressure or fatigue from a sealed fit.

9. Shokz OpenDots 2 — $140 (All-Time Low)

The Shokz OpenDots 2 are the favorite running earbuds of the audio reviewers at Business Insider, and they are $140 right now — another new all-time low. Shokz built its reputation on open-ear earbuds that leave you aware of your surroundings, which is why runners and cyclists swear by them. The OpenDots 2 clip on securely and sound far better than their size suggests. They are a niche pick, but for the person they are made for, they are the only pick.

10. Soundcore Liberty 5 — About $75

Anker's Soundcore brand has become the quiet giant of budget audio, and the Liberty 5 are available for approximately $75 this Black Friday. That is a flagship-level feature set for roughly the price of a dinner out, and a smart second pair for anyone who already owns a flagship. Soundcore's prices move through the week, so check the live price — anything under $80 is a genuine deal.

How to Pick the Right Pair Before You Buy

Start with your phone. If you own an iPhone, the AirPods Pro 3 at $149 are the obvious answer — they integrate with iOS in ways no other earbud can, from automatic switching between your iPhone, iPad, and Mac to Find My tracking when you lose one. If you own a Samsung Galaxy phone, the Galaxy Buds 4 Pro at $170 are the equivalent answer, with the same kind of deep integration. If you own any other Android phone, the Sony WF-1000XM6 or the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds will give you the best experience, and both work beautifully with iPhones too. Next, think about how you will actually use them. If you fly, commute on a train, or work in a noisy office, noise cancelling is the feature that will change your day, and the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds are the best at it. If you run, lift, or train, fit and sweat resistance matter more than anything else, and the Powerbeats Pro 2 with their ear hooks or the Shokz OpenDots 2 with their open design are built for exactly that. If you listen to music for hours and care about how it sounds, the Sony XM6 is the audiophile's choice. If you need to hear the world around you, the Bose Ultra Open Earbuds are the only design that solves that problem properly.

Timing: Buy Now or Wait for Friday?

This is the question every shopper asks, and this year the answer is clear: buy now if the deal you want is live. The old advice — wait for Black Friday itself, because prices drop further on the day — no longer matches how the sale works. Amazon's sale is a twelve-day event that started on November 19, and the best prices have already appeared. The AirPods Pro 3 at $149 is an all-time low that may not survive to Friday, because it already sold out once. The Galaxy Buds 4 Pro at $170, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds at $229, and the Sony XM5 at $180 are all at prices that retailers have no reason to cut further. When a deal is already at its lowest price ever, waiting does not make it cheaper. Waiting makes it gone. If the model you want is out of stock on Friday, check again on Cyber Monday, November 30, when retailers restock for the final day. The smart move is to buy the deal that is live now — you can return it if a better price appears. One warning matters more than any other this week: buy from real retailers. The deals in this article are from Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, Target, and the manufacturers' own stores. If you see the AirPods Pro 3 for $79 on a website you have never heard of, it is a scam. Counterfeit earbuds are one of the most common Black Friday frauds. Stick to the retailers named in this article, check that the marketplace seller is the retailer itself, and never buy electronics from a site that only accepts wire transfers or gift cards.

Why Earbuds Searches Are a Gold Mine for Tech Blogs

If you run a website, the earbuds rush is not just a shopping event. It is one of the most valuable traffic waves of the year, and the mechanics of it are worth understanding. Deal searches are different from ordinary searches. The person typing "AirPods Pro 3 Black Friday deal" already knows what they want to buy. They are not browsing. They are not researching. They are one click away from spending $149, and they will click the result that looks most current, most specific, and most trustworthy. That is why the title of this post leads with the exact price — AirPods Pro 3 at $149, $100 off — instead of a vague promise. A searcher who sees the price in the title clicks, because the title already answered their question and the article promises the details. Deal content also moves fast: a post published on Monday can rank by Tuesday and earn all week, so the publishers who move fast win the biggest wave of searches. Speed matters, but accuracy matters more, because a deal post with a wrong price loses its reader in seconds. The reader checks the price, finds it wrong, and never comes back. What Deal Traffic Means for Ad Revenue

For publishers, the connection between deal traffic and income is direct. Display advertising pays per thousand impressions, and the rate depends on who is reading and what they are reading about. A reader shopping for $149 earbuds is a reader that electronics advertisers want to reach, which means the ads shown on a deal page earn more per impression than the ads on an ordinary article. Advertisers pay far more to reach shoppers in the United States and Europe than readers elsewhere, which is why this blog targets American and European searchers. During Black Friday week, the combination of high-value readers and high-value advertisers produces the best rates of the year, and a single well-ranked deal post can generate more ad revenue in seven days than a dozen ordinary posts generate in a month. The most valuable placements on a deal page are the ones the reader actually sees: the banner at the top, the rectangle inside the article near the first deals, and the native recommendations below the content. A reader who scrolls through ten deals passes every one of them, which is why long, detailed deal posts earn more than short ones.
