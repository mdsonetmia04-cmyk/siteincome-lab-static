Buccaneers vs Bears: How Sports Blogs Turn NFL Traffic into Money

November 9, 2026

Buccaneers vs Bears is the most searched NFL game in America today — more than 1 million searches in the last 24 hours. Every big NFL game creates the same wave: millions of fans searching for scores, highlights, predictions, and analysis in a single day.

For most people, that wave is just entertainment. For sports bloggers, it is income. A single game-day post can bring more visitors in one afternoon than a normal blog gets in a month. The question is how to turn that traffic into money.

Why NFL Game Days Are Gold for Bloggers

Sports traffic has three things advertisers love:

  1. Huge volume in a short time — a big game can send thousands of visitors to one post in hours.
  2. Predictable schedule — you know the fixtures weeks in advance, so you can publish before the traffic arrives.
  3. Passionate readers — fans stay longer, read more pages, and click more than casual visitors.

That combination is exactly what ad networks pay for. Every 1,000 views is an earning opportunity.

What to Publish Around a Big Game

You do not need to be a sports journalist. Small sports blogs earn by publishing useful, simple content around each game:

  • Game previews — key players, recent form, and what to watch. Publish 1–2 days before kickoff so Google finds it in time.
  • Score updates and recaps — a short recap posted right after the game catches the second wave of searches.
  • Team and player guides — "Buccaneers vs Bears history" or player profiles earn traffic all season, not just on game day.
  • Opinion and analysis — your own take stands out from the big sites, and Google rewards original views.

The key is timing. A preview published the morning of the game is too late. Publish early, and let Google index it before the wave hits.

How Sports Blogs Actually Earn

Most sports blogs earn from display ads. The simple setup that works for beginners:

  1. Start a free blog — Blogger (Blogspot) costs nothing and is owned by Google, so it is a natural place to start.
  2. Get approved on an ad network — Adsterra approves small and new blogs, which is why many sports bloggers start there. Our guide explains the process: How to Get Approved on Adsterra.
  3. Use the right ad formats — a native banner inside the article and a social bar that stays visible while fans scroll. Both fit sports content because they do not interrupt the reading experience.
  4. Compare your options — not every network suits a new blog. See Adsterra vs AdSense before you choose.

A simple example of the math: if your CPM is $2, then 100,000 game-day views earn around $200 from display ads alone — from a single post. Bigger games and better ad placement push that higher.

Mistakes New Sports Bloggers Make

  • Copying scores and stats from big sites. Google already has ESPN and NFL.com. Your edge is your own angle, not their data.
  • Publishing only on game day. By then, the big sites have already taken the traffic. Publish early.
  • No monetization set up. Traffic without ads earns nothing. Set up your ad network before the season starts, not during it.
  • Ignoring mobile readers. Most sports fans search on their phones during the game. Your blog must load fast and look clean on mobile.
