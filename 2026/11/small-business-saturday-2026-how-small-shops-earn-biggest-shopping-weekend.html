Small Business Saturday 2026: How Small Shops Earn From the Year's Biggest Shopping Weekend

Tomorrow morning, while the big-box stores are still counting their Black Friday receipts, the most profitable street in America will not be a mall. It will be Main Street. On Saturday, November 28, 2026, millions of Americans will walk into the bakery that knows their order, the bookstore that remembers their taste, and the toy shop that wraps gifts by hand — and they will spend, by American Express's own survey estimate, roughly $18 billion doing it. That is the quiet miracle of Small Business Saturday: a holiday invented by a credit card company in 2010 has become the one day of the year when the smallest shops in the country outsell the giants on their own turf. For the owners of those shops, it is the biggest single earning day of the year. For bloggers, creators, and anyone who earns money from the internet, it is the most under-covered shopping holiday on the calendar — which is exactly why it pays so well to cover it.

Small Business Saturday sits in the middle of the biggest shopping weekend on Earth. Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26, 2026. Black Friday follows on Friday, November 27. Small Business Saturday is Saturday, November 28. Cyber Monday closes the weekend on Monday, November 30. The National Retail Federation expects 182.7 million Americans to shop at some point across that five-day stretch, and it forecasts total holiday sales of $1.013 trillion, up 4.2 percent on last year. Every one of those numbers is a headline. But the number that matters for a small shop owner is simpler: 88 percent of American consumers say they are likely to shop at a small business this holiday season, according to the 2026 American Express Shop Small Impact Study — the highest level American Express has recorded since it started asking the question in 2012. More than half of them, 56 percent, say they plan to shop or eat at a small business on Small Business Saturday itself.

That is demand, stated plainly, in a year when shoppers are watching every dollar. The same NRF survey found that 82 percent of holiday shoppers are taking steps to manage costs: 25 percent are buying less expensive gifts, 24 percent are cutting back on spending elsewhere, and 20 percent are buying for fewer people.

When Is Small Business Saturday 2026?

Small Business Saturday 2026 is Saturday, November 28, 2026 — the Saturday after Thanksgiving and the day after Black Friday. It has fallen on that Saturday every year since American Express founded it in 2010, and the U.S. Small Business Administration has co-sponsored it since 2011. The date never moves in the way other holidays do, because it is anchored to Thanksgiving, which is always the fourth Thursday of November in the United States. That makes it easy to plan around and easy to forget, which is why the shops that plan for it in October are the ones that clean up in November.

The full 2026 holiday calendar around it looks like this. Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. Black Friday is Friday, November 27 — the day the big retailers fight over doorbusters and the day our own Amazon Black Friday deals guide tracked AirPods Pro 3 at $149. Small Business Saturday is November 28. Sunday, November 29, is the quiet day, when the weekend's online orders start arriving and the shops restock. Cyber Monday is November 30, the biggest online shopping day of the year, with Adobe forecasting $15.1 billion in U.S. online spending. The five days together are the make-or-break stretch for American retail, and Small Business Saturday is the only one of the five that belongs to the little guy.

What the Numbers Actually Say — and What They Don't

The headline figure gets quoted every year, so let us be honest about what it is. American Express estimated that shoppers reported spending $18 billion at small businesses on Small Business Saturday in 2025, down from about $22 billion reported for 2024. The cumulative figure since tracking began is $241 billion. Those numbers come from surveys — the 2025 estimate came from a Teneo survey of 2,945 American adults on November 30, 2025 — and American Express says plainly in its own footnotes that they are projections from what shoppers said they spent, not a count of receipts or card data. They measure participation, not a forecast for any single shop's till. A blogger who quotes them as hard sales figures is misleading readers, and a shop owner who budgets against them is budgeting against a survey.

But the direction underneath the numbers is real, and it is more useful than the headline. The 2026 Shop Small Impact Study, fielded by Teneo on September 9–11, 2026, surveyed 1,008 American adults and 503 owners or managers of businesses with 2 to 99 employees. Its findings tell a story a shop owner can actually act on. Eighty-eight percent of consumers are likely to shop small this holiday season, the highest since 2012. Fifty-six percent plan to shop or dine at a small business on the Saturday itself. Eighty percent of owners say the day can help build relationships that last beyond the holidays. And 70 percent of owners say this year's holiday customer spending will affect their ability to grow in 2027. Read that last number twice: for seven in ten small businesses, this one weekend helps decide whether next year is a growth year or a survival year.

Then there is the number that explains why the day matters to a town, not just a till. For every dollar spent at a small business in the United States, 68 cents stays in the local community, according to the SBA. That is the multiplier effect the SBA's administrator talked about when she urged Americans to shop small on November 28: the money does not fly off to a distant headquarters; it pays the rent on the shop next door, the wages of the teenager behind the counter, and the order at the local wholesaler. America has 36 million small businesses. They are 99 percent of all businesses in the country, they employ nearly half of the private-sector workforce, and they generate over 40 percent of U.S. GDP. Small Business Saturday is the one day of the year when all of that is visible on a single street.

Why Small Shops Win on Saturday (and Lose on Friday)

Black Friday belongs to scale. The winners are the retailers who can buy ten thousand televisions and sell them at a loss to fill a store. Our Walmart Black Friday guide tracked $89 AirPods 4 and a $128 50-inch 4K TV — prices no independent shop can match, because no independent shop buys at that volume. A small shop that tries to fight Black Friday on price is fighting a war it has already lost. The shops that win the weekend are the ones that refuse that fight entirely.

Small Business Saturday is winnable because it is fought on different ground. The shopper who walks into a small shop on Saturday is not comparing prices on a phone. She is buying a gift for someone specific, and she wants it to feel chosen rather than discounted. The small shop's advantages are all the things a warehouse store cannot copy: a product the customer cannot find anywhere else, a story behind it, a person who can explain it, and wrapping that makes the gift look like it cost twice what it did. A candle poured in the back room, a scarf knitted two streets away, a toy the owner has actually played with — these are not commodities, and commodities are the only thing a price war can sell.

The 2026 Playbook: Seven Ways Small Shops Actually Earn on Saturday

The shops that earn the most on Small Business Saturday are rarely the ones with the deepest discounts. They are the ones with the best plan. Here is the playbook that works in 2026, built from what the data says shoppers want and what the day actually rewards.

1. Sell the bundle, not the discount

A 20 percent discount on a $40 candle costs the shop $8 and teaches the customer to wait for sales. A $50 gift set — the $40 candle, a $12 soap, and a $10 ornament, sold together for $50 — earns the shop more money, moves three products instead of one, and feels like a gift rather than a bargain. Bundles work because Saturday's shopper is buying for someone else, and a bundle is already a finished gift. Put the bundle in a box, tie it with ribbon, and price it at a round number. The customer who came in for one thing leaves with a present, and the shop's average sale jumps without a single price cut.

2. Make the gift card the easiest product in the store

Gift cards are the quiet goldmine of the day. They cost nothing to stock, they never go out of fashion, and a large share of them are never fully redeemed — pure margin. Yet most small shops hide them behind the counter and mention them only when asked. On Saturday, put them at the front of the store, in every denomination, with a small stand that says the quiet part out loud: not sure what they want? This is the answer. A gift card sold on Saturday is also a customer who walks back through the door in January, the slowest month of the year, when the shop needs foot traffic most.

3. Turn the shop into an event

The most successful Small Business Saturday shops do not just open their doors; they stage a reason to stay. Hot cider at the door. A local musician in the corner. A maker demonstrating the product live. A stamp card that gives a small free gift at the third shop on the street. The event costs almost nothing and does two things a discount cannot: it gives shoppers a reason to bring their children and their friends, and it gives them a photo to post. That photo is free advertising to exactly the audience the American Express study says matters — friends and family, the channel 65 percent of shoppers trust most.

4. Win the search that happens on the sidewalk

Most Saturday shoppers do not plan their route in advance. They search on their phones while standing on the street: "gift shops near me," "bookstore open now," "best coffee downtown." The shop that wins that search is the one whose Google Business Profile is complete and current — correct hours, correct address, recent photos, and a post published this week saying "Open Saturday, 9 to 6, gift wrapping free." That post takes three minutes to write and decides whether a shopper walking fifty meters away turns left toward your door or right toward someone else's. Local search is the small shop's cheapest advertising, and Saturday is the day it pays off most.

5. Sell online before Saturday, deliver the story on Saturday

The smartest small shops treat Saturday as the pickup day, not the selling day. They post their best gift sets on Instagram and in their email newsletter on Wednesday and Thursday, let customers reserve them, and hand them over on Saturday with the wrapping, the card, and the conversation. The sale is already made before the doors open; Saturday becomes a celebration instead of a gamble. This also solves the day's biggest risk, which is the weather: a rainy Saturday can cut foot traffic in half, but a reserved order is a sale whatever the sky does. The shops that earn the most are the ones that do not leave the whole day to chance.

6. Collect the customer, not just the cash

A Saturday sale is worth one transaction. A Saturday customer is worth a year. The shops that understand this put a simple offer at the till: join the email list, get 10 percent off in January. The email list is the only marketing channel a small shop truly owns — no algorithm, no ad platform, no rent. The 80 percent of owners who say the day builds lasting relationships are describing exactly this: the sale is the beginning, not the end.

7. Price for the gift, not for the bargain hunter

Look at what the big stores are actually charging this weekend, and price against the gift, not against them. Target is selling the Dyson V9 at $269.99 and the Nintendo Switch 2 bundle at $499.99; Amazon has the AirPods Pro 3 at $149. No small shop should try to undercut those numbers. The small shop's customer is not the person who wants the cheapest AirPods — she already bought them on Friday. The small shop's customer is the person who wants the gift nobody else will give: the handmade, the local, the personal. Price it like what it is — a one-of-a-kind gift, not a clearance item — and the right customer will pay it gladly.

What Bloggers and Creators Earn From Small Business Saturday

If you earn money from content, Small Business Saturday is the most profitable shopping holiday you are probably ignoring. Every other date on the calendar is crowded: a thousand sites publish Black Friday guides, and a thousand more publish Cyber Monday roundups. Small Business Saturday has a fraction of that competition, and the audience is enormous — 182.7 million people shopping across the weekend, 88 percent of them open to buying small. Low competition plus high intent is the exact combination that makes a post rank, and ranking is what pays.

The content that works is local gift guides. "The 12 Best Gifts From Small Shops in Austin," "Where to Shop Small in Chicago This Saturday," "Small Business Gift Guide for People Who Hate Malls" — these are posts that answer a question people are genuinely typing into Google on Friday night. They also earn in three ways at once. Affiliate links to the products the shops sell online pay per sale. Display ads pay per view, and a local gift guide in November is high-value inventory because advertisers are bidding hardest in the holiday weeks. And the shops themselves become an audience: a shop featured in a guide shares it with its customers, which is free distribution to exactly the local readers an advertiser wants.

There is also the deal content nobody else writes. The big deal sites cover the national chains; nobody covers the independent shop's Saturday offer, because there is no press release and no affiliate feed for it. A blogger who walks down Main Street on Saturday morning, photographs the window displays, and writes up the ten best finds has a post that cannot be copied by a content farm. It is original, it is local, and it is exactly what Google's ranking systems are built to reward. Our own Cyber Monday deals guide from the start of the season showed the same pattern: a specific, price-led, genuinely researched post earns clicks that a generic roundup never will. Small Business Saturday is the same opportunity with a tenth of the competition.

The Mistakes That Kill the Day

The first mistake is the discount war. A small shop that matches a chain's 40 percent off has given away its margin and gained nothing, because the customer who came for the discount will leave for the next discount. The second mistake is staying silent. A shop with no social media post, no email, and no sign in the window on Saturday is invisible on the one day the whole country is looking. The marketing is free — the Shop Small materials, the hashtag, the local press — and the shop that uses none of it has chosen to be invisible. The third mistake is treating Saturday as a one-day event. The shops that earn the most treat it as the launch of their holiday season: the email list grows on Saturday, the gift cards sell on Saturday, and the customers come back in December because Saturday gave them a reason to.

How to Shop Small on Saturday (If You Are the Customer)

If you are reading this as a shopper rather than a seller, the plan is simple. Start with a list, not a wander: three people, three gifts, three shops. Check the shop's hours online before you leave — Saturday hours are often longer than usual, but not always. Bring cash for the smallest shops, where card fees eat a real share of a small sale. Ask the owner what they recommend; the recommendation is the product, and it is free. Buy the gift card for the person you cannot decide on, and buy it at the shop, not online, so the money lands in the till that day. And keep the receipt, because the exchange in January is part of the service a small shop offers and a big website does not.

The Bottom Line

Small Business Saturday 2026 is Saturday, November 28 — the day after Black Friday, the day before the quiet Sunday, two days before Cyber Monday's $15.1 billion online rush. American Express founded it in 2010, the SBA has co-sponsored it since 2011, and shoppers reported spending an estimated $18 billion at small businesses on the day in 2025, part of $241 billion reported since tracking began. This year, 88 percent of Americans say they are likely to shop small this holiday season, the highest since 2012, and 56 percent plan to shop or dine at a small business on the Saturday itself. For the shops, the playbook is simple: bundle instead of discounting, sell the gift card, stage an event, win the search on the sidewalk, take the orders before Saturday, collect the customer, and price the gift like the one-of-a-kind thing it is. For bloggers and creators, the opportunity is just as simple: a huge, high-intent audience, almost no competition, and a story that cannot be faked from a desk. The big stores had their day on Friday. Saturday belongs to the small shop on your street. Go there, buy something real, and keep 68 cents of your dollar at home.
