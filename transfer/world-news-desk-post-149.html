TITLE: Messi's Title Defence Is Over: Columbus Stun Inter Miami 1-0 in the 92nd Minute to Complete a 2-0 Playoff Sweep LABELS: Sports SEARCH DESCRIPTION: Santiago Rodriguez's 92nd-minute goal ended Lionel Messi's title defence as Columbus Crew swept Inter Miami 2-0 in the MLS Cup playoffs. ---BODY---

Lionel Messi in 2026. The 39-year-old scored 26 goals and made 17 assists in the regular season — but could not find a breakthrough in either playoff game. Photo: Wikimedia Commons (public domain).

Lionel Messi's title defence is over, and it ended in the cruellest way a football season can end: two minutes into stoppage time, one goal away from a penalty shootout, with the ball in the back of the net and the season gone. Santiago Rodriguez scored in the 92nd minute on Friday night to give the Columbus Crew a 1-0 victory over Inter Miami at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field, completing a 2-0 sweep of the first-round best-of-three series and eliminating the defending MLS Cup champions from the 2026 playoffs.

Just as the match appeared headed for a penalty shootout, Rodriguez struck, converting a cross from Anass Zaroury to send the home crowd into celebration, as Reuters reported. The goal was the difference between a shootout and an exit, between a deciding Game 3 back in Miami on 2 December and the end of the season. Miami, who had failed to score in either game of the series, went out without a goal to their name in the playoffs. Columbus, the No. 7 seed, who were in last place in the Eastern Conference on 1 September, advanced to the Eastern Conference semi-finals. The defending champions were beaten 4-0 on aggregate by a team that had no business, on paper, beating them at all.

The 92nd minute

The game had been heading, minute by minute, toward penalties. A tightly contested first half offered few clear opportunities, with Messi coming close to breaking the deadlock in the 22nd minute with a free kick, while German Berterame also tested Columbus goalkeeper Patrick Schulte. The second half was the same: tight, tense, and goalless, with Miami unable to find the breakthrough that their season depended on. The match was tied after 90 minutes, and under the playoff rules it would have gone straight to a penalty shootout, with no extra time. Miami, who had scored a league-best 80 goals in the regular season, were two minutes from a lottery they might have won.

Then Rodriguez struck. The marquee summer signing, acquired from Brazil's Botafogo, chested down Zaroury's ball into the box and beat goalkeeper Dayne St. Clair for the dramatic game-winner, as LA Momentum reported. It was Rodriguez's third career postseason goal — he had netted twice in eleven playoff appearances for New York City FC before joining Columbus — and it was the biggest of his career. The home crowd at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field erupted. The Miami players stood still. The season was over.

The goal was cruel in its timing and fitting in its authorship. Rodriguez is the player Columbus signed in the summer to change games like this, and he changed this one in the second minute of stoppage time. Zaroury, who provided the cross, is the winger who had tormented Miami's defence all night. The combination was not luck. It was the product of a team that had won eight straight matches, and that believed, with two minutes left, that one more attack would be enough. It was.

A sweep nobody saw coming

The sweep was stunning because of the gap between the two teams on paper. Inter Miami finished the regular season second in the Eastern Conference, with a record of 16 wins, 12 draws and 6 defeats from 34 games, 60 points, and a league-best 80 goals. Columbus finished seventh, with 14 wins, 5 draws and 15 defeats, 47 points, and a goal difference of just +7. Miami were the defending champions, the No. 2 seed, the team with the greatest player of all time, and the favourites to reach the final. Columbus were the team that had been bottom of the East on 1 September.

Game 1, on Saturday 21 November at Nu Stadium in Miami, was the first shock. Columbus won 3-0, with Gonzalo Tapia scoring twice and Taha Habroune adding a goal, and Inter Miami were reduced to ten men after Gonzalo Lujan received a red card. It was the first MLS Cup Playoffs meeting between the two clubs, and it was a rout. Miami, who had scored 80 goals in the regular season, did not score. Columbus, who had been last in the conference two and a half months earlier, scored three.

Game 2 was supposed to be the correction. Miami needed a win to force a deciding Game 3 back at Nu Stadium on Wednesday 2 December, and they had Messi, Luis Suarez, Rodrigo De Paul, and a team that had scored 80 goals. Instead, they were shut out again. It was the first time since March that Miami had been shut out in consecutive matches. The team that had scored more goals than any other in the league could not score one in 180 minutes of playoff football. The sweep was 2-0 in games, 4-0 in goals, and total in every sense that matters.

Messi's season — and the question it leaves

For Lionel Messi, the exit is a personal disappointment at the end of a remarkable individual season. The 39-year-old led the league with 26 goals and 17 assists in the regular season, securing a second consecutive Golden Boot, and he is the favourite to win an unprecedented third straight Landon Donovan MLS MVP award. He was, by the numbers, the best player in the league again. In the playoffs, he was unable to find a breakthrough, as Reuters put it, and the team that depended on him could not find one either.

It was a second first-round exit in the three MLS Cup playoffs he has contested, according to the Associated Press. The parallels are with 2024, when Miami, also as favourites, were beaten in the first round by Atlanta United. The 2025 title — a 3-1 win over the Vancouver Whitecaps in the MLS Cup final — was supposed to have ended that pattern. It did not. The pattern is now two first-round exits in three years, with a championship in between, and the question it raises is whether a team built around a 39-year-old genius can survive a playoff series in which the genius is stopped.

Messi announced his international retirement in August, ending a legendary run with Argentina that included a World Cup title and two Copa America titles. He is under contract with Inter Miami through 2028, but there is some reason to believe he might opt to call it a career before then, USA Today reported. The question of his future will dominate Miami's off-season, and the answer will shape the club's next three years. If he stays, the club rebuilds around him again. If he goes, the club rebuilds without the player who made it the most famous team in American soccer. Either way, the 2026 season is over, and it ended in Columbus, in the 92nd minute, with a goal he could not prevent.

Columbus: from last place to the semi-finals

If Miami's story is one of failure, Columbus's is one of the great turnarounds in MLS history. The Crew were in last place in the Eastern Conference on 1 September. They won eight of their final nine regular-season games to sneak into the playoffs as the No. 7 seed, and they have now won eight straight matches, and ten of their last twelve. They won their last four games at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field. They beat Miami 3-0 away, and 1-0 at home, and they did it without conceding a goal in the series.

The turnaround has a shape. Under coach Laurent Courtois, Columbus found a defensive solidity they had lacked all season, and a belief that carried them through the final weeks. They were unbeaten against Miami in the 2026 regular season, drawing 2-2 in Miami on 1 August and winning 2-1 at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field on 27 September. The playoff sweep was not a fluke. It was the third time in three months that Columbus had beaten or held the champions, and the second time they had kept them scoreless.

The Crew will now face the New England Revolution (No. 3) or the Philadelphia Union (No. 6) in the Eastern Conference semi-finals, held on 5-6 December. The winner of that series moves one step closer to MLS Cup presented by Audi on 18 December. Columbus last won the championship in 2023, and the team that was last in the East on 1 September is now three wins from another title. From the bottom of the East standings to eliminating Leo Messi's Miami in the playoffs, MLS's own website said, Columbus are starting to look like a team of destiny. Whether their magical late-season run leads all the way to MLS Cup on 18 December is the question of the next three weeks.

The team that could not score

The most striking statistic of the series is not the 4-0 aggregate. It is the zero. Inter Miami, who scored 80 goals in the regular season — more than any other team in the league — scored none in two playoff games. The goals had come from everywhere in the regular season: Messi's 26, the contributions of Suarez and Berterame, the supply from De Paul and Segovia. In the playoffs, the supply stopped. Messi's free kick in the 22nd minute of Game 2 was the closest Miami came. Berterame tested Schulte. Nothing else did.

The reasons are the familiar ones of playoff football. Columbus defended deep and narrow, denied Messi space between the lines, and forced Miami wide. Miami, who had faced key absences through suspension and injury for Game 2, could not find a second way to score. The red card to Lujan in Game 1 had already cost them a defender for the series. The team that had scored 80 goals was, in the end, a team that could only score one way, and Columbus took that way away.

It is also a reminder that the regular season and the playoffs are different competitions. Miami finished second in the East with 60 points, thirteen behind Nashville, who topped the conference with 77. Their 80 goals were the best in the league. Their 59 conceded were the most among the top seven. The defence had been a weakness all season, and the attack had covered it. In the playoffs, the attack was stopped, and the defence — which conceded four goals in two games — could not save them. The season that had promised much, as one report put it, ended with the same flaw that had been visible since February.

Miami's year, in full

The playoff exit was not the only disappointment of Miami's season. The club also suffered exits in the Round of 16 of the Concacaf Champions Cup and in the opening stage of the Leagues Cup. The 2025 MLS Cup title — the club's first championship, won with a 3-1 defeat of Vancouver in the final — had raised expectations that 2026 would be a year of consolidation, and perhaps of a repeat. Instead, it was a year in which the team won the regular-season battles and lost the ones that mattered.

Inter Miami were making their fifth appearance in the MLS Cup playoffs, after previously featuring in 2020, 2022, and 2024 before winning their maiden championship in 2025. The 2024 exit, to Atlanta United in the first round, was the parallel that haunted the club all season — and the 2026 exit, to Columbus in the first round, is the parallel that will haunt it all winter. The ghosts of 2024, as MLS's own website put it, came back to haunt Messi and Inter Miami. The team that was supposed to have learned from 2024 repeated it, against a different opponent, in a different stadium, with the same result.

The off-season now begins, and it begins with questions. What is Messi's future? What happens to the squad around him — Suarez, De Paul, Berterame, the supporting cast who could not score when it mattered? What does the club do about a defence that conceded 59 goals in the regular season and four in two playoff games? And what does it do about the pattern — two first-round exits in three years — that suggests the problem is not the players, but the way the team is built for the playoffs? The answers will come in the transfer window, and in the decisions the club makes about its coach, Cristian "Kily" Gonzalez, and its roster. For now, the season is over, and the answers can wait. The silence in the Miami dressing room on Friday night said enough.

The numbers behind the upset

The scale of the upset is best seen in the numbers, because the numbers are what make it hard to believe. Inter Miami finished the regular season with 60 points from 34 games — 16 wins, 12 draws, 6 defeats — and 80 goals, the most in the league. Columbus finished with 47 points — 14 wins, 5 draws, 15 defeats — and 59 goals. The gap between the two teams was thirteen points, and the gap in goals was twenty-one. Nashville, who topped the Eastern Conference, finished with 77 points, 23 wins, 8 draws, and 3 defeats. Miami were thirteen points behind Nashville and thirteen points ahead of Columbus. On the table, the series should not have been close. It was not close. It went the other way.

The head-to-head record makes it stranger still. Columbus were unbeaten against Miami in the 2026 regular season: a 2-2 draw in Miami on 1 August, and a 2-1 win at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field on 27 September. The playoff sweep was the third meeting in three months in which Columbus had taken something from the champions, and the second in which they had kept them scoreless. The 3-0 win in Game 1 was the first MLS Cup Playoffs meeting between the two clubs, and it was also the most one-sided first-round result of the playoffs. Tapia scored twice, Habroune added the third, and Lujan's red card reduced Miami to ten men. The game was over as a contest before it was over as a match.

The individual numbers are just as stark. Messi's 26 goals and 17 assists led the league in both categories that matter for an attacker, and his second consecutive Golden Boot was secured before the playoffs began. He is the favourite to win a third straight Landon Donovan MLS MVP award, which would be unprecedented. In the two playoff games, he had one free kick that came close in the 22nd minute of Game 2, and nothing else that threatened the goal. The player who had scored or assisted 43 goals in the regular season was involved in none in the playoffs. The team that had scored 80 goals in 34 games scored none in two. The numbers say that Columbus did not just beat Miami. They solved them.

Columbus's numbers tell the other half of the story. Eight straight wins. Ten wins in their last twelve outings. Eight wins in their final nine regular-season games. Four straight home wins at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field. A team that was last in the Eastern Conference on 1 September, and that had won six straight games to climb into the playoffs, kept winning when the playoffs began. The run is the longest winning streak in the club's recent history, and it has carried them from the bottom of the table to the semi-finals. The numbers that matter now are the ones ahead: two more wins to the final, three to the title.

What the exit means for the league

The playoffs will go on without their biggest star, and that matters for more than the result. Messi is the reason millions of people around the world watch MLS, and the reason the league's games are carried on MLS Season Pass on Apple TV to a global audience. Game 2 was also shown in the United States on FS1 and FOX Deportes, and the kickoff — 8 p.m. Eastern on Friday, 1 a.m. Saturday in Britain — was scheduled for a prime-time American audience. The audience that tuned in saw the champions go out in the 92nd minute, and the player they had come to see walk off the field with his season over.

That is the risk of building a league around a single star, and it is a risk MLS has accepted since Messi arrived. The league is bigger, richer, and more watched because of him. It is also more fragile, because the story of its playoffs can end in the first round, in Columbus, on a Friday night in November. The league will point to the Columbus story as the counterweight: the team of destiny, the eight-game winning streak, the semi-final against New England or Philadelphia, the final on 18 December. The counterweight is real. It is also, for the league's broadcasters and sponsors, a smaller story than the one that ended on Friday.

For Miami, the exit raises a question that goes beyond one series. The club was built, in the space of three years, around Messi — his goals, his assists, his presence, and the players signed to support him. The model won a championship in 2025. In 2026 it produced the most goals in the league, a second-place finish, and a first-round exit without a goal. The model is not broken. It is incomplete. A team that can score 80 goals and concede 59 is a team that can win the regular season and lose the playoffs, because the playoffs are decided by the team that concedes less, not the team that scores more. Columbus conceded none in the series. Miami scored none. That is the whole of the series, in two numbers.

The off-season will decide whether the club learns that lesson. The transfer window will bring new players, and the decisions about the coach, the defence, and the squad will shape the 2027 season. Messi's future — whether he stays through 2028, or calls time earlier — will shape it more. The 2026 season is over, and the questions it leaves are the ones the club has been avoiding since 2024: how do you build a team that can win in November, not just in the summer? How do you protect a 39-year-old genius from a series in which the opposition decides to stop him, and succeeds? And how do you win a playoff series when the goals dry up? The answers will come in the winter. The silence on Friday night was the sound of a club that does not yet have them.

What next for the playoffs

The playoffs go on without their biggest star. Columbus advance to the Eastern Conference semi-finals on 5-6 December, against New England or Philadelphia. The MLS Cup final is on 18 December, and the team that lifts the trophy will be the team that navigates three more rounds of knockout football — the kind of football that Columbus have just proved they understand, and that Miami have just proved they do not.

For the neutral, the story of the playoffs is now the Columbus story: the team that was last on 1 September, that won eight straight, that beat the champions 4-0 on aggregate, and that is three wins from a second title in four years. For the Miami fan, the story is the opposite: the team with the greatest player in the world, the most goals in the league, and the highest expectations, going out in the first round without scoring a goal. Both stories are true. They are the same story, told from opposite ends of a 92nd-minute goal.

Friday night in Columbus was the night the 2026 MLS Cup playoffs became a tournament without Lionel Messi. The defending champions are out. The No. 7 seed is through. And the team that was bottom of the East on 1 September is, on 28 November, one of the four teams left standing in the conference — and the team nobody wants to play next.
