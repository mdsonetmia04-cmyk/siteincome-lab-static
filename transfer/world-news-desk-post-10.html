Jayden Daniels with the Washington Commanders in 2025. Photo: All-Pro Reels / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Thirty-nine seconds. Sixty-four yards. One kick. That is all it took for the Washington Commanders to produce the most dramatic finish of the NFL season on Monday night, stunning the Cincinnati Bengals 22-20 on a 26-yard field goal by Drew Stevens as time expired. The win snapped Cincinnati's five-game winning streak, completed a weekend in which every NFC East team emerged victorious, and gave the league a Monday night finish that will be replayed for weeks. And it was only the beginning of a wild Monday around the NFL — a day that also saw the San Francisco 49ers officially release star receiver Brandon Aiyuk, the Arizona Cardinals fire their defensive coordinator, and a quarterback once paid like a franchise player clear waivers and become a free agent.

Week 11 of the 2026 NFL season had already delivered its share of drama before Monday night arrived. The 49ers had thrashed the Minnesota Vikings 35-10 in Mexico City. The Buffalo Bills had improved to 9-1 for the first time since 1991. The Dallas Cowboys had hung 51 points on the Tennessee Titans. The New York Giants had beaten the Jacksonville Jaguars in a statement win. And then Monday night added the exclamation point: a 64-yard drive in 39 seconds, a kick at the gun, and a result that nobody in Cincinnati saw coming. Here is the full story of the week that was — and what it means for the playoff race.

The Monday night stunner: 39 seconds that broke Cincinnati

The Washington Commanders defeated the Cincinnati Bengals 22-20 on Monday Night Football, and the way it happened is the stuff of highlight reels. Trailing late, with 39 seconds on the clock and 64 yards of field between them and the end zone, the Commanders put the ball in the hands of quarterback Jayden Daniels — and Daniels delivered. He led a 64-yard drive in the final 39 seconds, moving Washington into field-goal range with the clock bleeding away, and set up Drew Stevens for a 26-yard field goal as time expired.

The kick was good. The stadium erupted. The Commanders improved to 4-5-1, and the Bengals fell to 7-3 after entering the contest as one of the hottest teams in the NFL. Cincinnati had won five straight. It had looked, for most of the season, like a team on a collision course with the conference championship. And then, in 39 seconds, it all unraveled.

Daniels' drive was a masterclass in late-game quarterbacking. Sixty-four yards in 39 seconds requires precision on every play — no wasted motion, no sacks, no incompletions that stop the clock in the wrong way, no mistakes. Daniels, the second-year quarterback who was named the NFL Offensive Rookie of the Year last season after leading Washington to the NFC Championship Game, showed exactly why the Commanders built their franchise around him. He has taken every Washington snap in the last four games, and on Monday night he played like a quarterback who understands the moment.

The loss was brutal for Cincinnati in ways that go beyond the standings. The Bengals had entered the game as one of the league's hottest teams, winners of five straight, with an offense that had been rolling. Joe Burrow threw for 300 yards and two scores without an interception — a performance that, on most nights, wins the game. But Cincinnati ran for only 31 yards all night, and when the game came down to a final drive, the defense could not get the stop it needed. The Bengals are still 7-3, still very much in the playoff race. But a loss like this one lingers, and it will be remembered when the season's story is told.

For Washington, the win was something more than a single victory. It completed a weekend in which every NFC East team emerged victorious — a clean sweep for a division that has spent the season beating up on itself. And it gave the Commanders, at 4-5-1, a pulse in a season that had threatened to slip away. They are not contenders yet. But on Monday night, with 39 seconds left and 64 yards to go, they played like a team that believes it can be.

The 49ers in Mexico City — and the end of the Aiyuk era

If Monday night belonged to Washington, Sunday night belonged to San Francisco. The 49ers thrashed the Minnesota Vikings 35-10 in Mexico City, in a game that was not close from the moment it started. The 49ers scored four touchdowns in the first half — two by tight end George Kittle — before running out 35-10 winners in front of 85,612 fans at the Azteca Stadium, which was hosting its sixth NFL game and its third involving San Francisco.

The numbers were overwhelming. Quarterback Brock Purdy was outstanding in his first win against Vikings defensive coordinator Brian Flores in three tries, executing coach Kyle Shanahan's game plan perfectly: 21 of 26 for 255 yards with three touchdown passes and no turnovers. San Francisco became the first team to score more than 23 points against the Vikings this season — a defense that had not conceded more than 23 points in a game all year, and that was supposed to be the best in the league. The 49ers outsmarted them completely, and the game was effectively over by halftime.

The victory improved San Francisco to 9-1, giving the 49ers the best record in the NFC and possession of the conference's top seed. They lead the way in the conference, with the Chicago Bears and their divisional rivals the Detroit Lions next best at 8-2. The 49ers have been the league's most complete team this season, and the Mexico City performance was a statement: on a neutral field, in a primetime international slot, against one of the best defenses in football, they were not merely better. They were dominant.

And then, on Monday, the 49ers made the other news of the day. The team officially released receiver Brandon Aiyuk, ending a prolonged and increasingly complicated separation between the organization and one of its former offensive stars. The release followed the dominant victory over Minnesota, and it closed a chapter that had hung over the franchise for months. Aiyuk, once a central piece of the 49ers' offense, is now a free agent. The 49ers, at 9-1, have made it clear that no single player is bigger than the team — and that they intend to chase a championship with the roster they have.

The Mexico City game was also a milestone for the league. It was the last of a record nine international games this season, and the first international game ever played in the primetime slot. Mexico hosted the first regular-season game ever played outside the United States in October 2005, and the NFL returned to the country on Sunday following a four-year absence. The Azteca Stadium, redeveloped for the 2026 World Cup, staged a spectacle — and the 49ers gave the 85,612 fans in attendance a performance worthy of the occasion.

The contenders: Buffalo, Kansas City, Detroit, Denver, Dallas

Week 11 was a week for the contenders, and the contenders mostly did what contenders do. The Buffalo Bills improved to 9-1 for the first time since 1991, beating the Miami Dolphins 26-9 and taking the lead in the AFC. Josh Allen threw for 304 yards and a touchdown — and two interceptions — but Buffalo's defense made it 15-plus quarters without allowing a touchdown, a streak of dominance that is the backbone of the Bills' season. The Bills are 9-1 for just the fifth time in team history, and the first time since a 10-1 start in 1991. They lead the AFC from a pair of divisional rivals, with the Kansas City Chiefs and Denver Broncos both at 8-2.

The Chiefs kept pace with a 34-7 rout of the Arizona Cardinals. Patrick Mahomes threw for 324 yards and three touchdowns, two of them to Travis Kelce, and Kansas City outgained the Cardinals 435-205. The Chiefs made sure they were not caught looking ahead to their prime-time matchup at Buffalo on Thanksgiving night — a game that now looms as one of the biggest of the season, with the AFC's top seed potentially on the line.

The Detroit Lions handled the Tampa Bay Buccaneers 31-21, with Jared Goff throwing for 279 yards and two scores and Jahmyr Gibbs running for two more. The Lions are 8-2, tied with Chicago as the second-best record in the NFC, and they continue to look like a team built for January. The Denver Broncos won their sixth straight game, beating the Las Vegas Raiders 38-24 behind rookie Jonah Coleman, who ran for 111 yards and three touchdowns. The Broncos are 8-2, and they will visit Pittsburgh on Black Friday.

And then there were the Dallas Cowboys, who put up 51 points in a 51-30 win over the Tennessee Titans. Dak Prescott went 28 of 33 for 325 yards and four touchdowns, CeeDee Lamb caught 11 passes for 132 yards, and Dallas gained 480 yards without a turnover or a sack allowed. It was a rollercoaster performance from a 5-6 team that has been brilliant and baffling in equal measure this season — but on Sunday, the brilliance was on full display.

The surprises: the Giants, the Eagles, the Jets, the Panthers

Week 11 also belonged to the teams that were not supposed to win. The New York Giants earned their most impressive victory of the season, beating the Jacksonville Jaguars 23-18 in a game that featured two teams with winning records — one of only two such matchups on the entire weekend slate. The Jaguars had entered the game with the best regular-season record over the past 12 months, having lost only twice, and had scored more than 30 points in each of their past four games. The Giants stifled them for three quarters, led 20-6 heading into a dramatic final quarter, and held on for the win.

It was the first time the NFC East leaders have beaten a team with a winning record this season, and it improved the Giants to 6-3-1, while the 7-3 Jaguars remain in charge of the AFC South. In his first season as head coach, John Harbaugh could lead the Giants to their first division title since 2011, when Big Blue went on to claim their fourth Super Bowl. They remain clear of the 5-6 Cowboys, and they look increasingly like a team that belongs in the conversation.

The Philadelphia Eagles ended a seven-game losing streak with a 19-16 win over the Pittsburgh Steelers, a result that surprised everyone who had written the Eagles off. The defense gave Aaron Rodgers trouble all game, sacking him five times. Rodgers completed 30 of 44 passes for only 227 yards, and the Eagles — at 3-7 — finally have something to build on. The Carolina Panthers beat the Baltimore Ravens 35-33 in a shootout, with the Ravens gaining 421 yards but committing four turnovers in the loss. And the New York Jets beat the Los Angeles Chargers 27-24 on the road, with Geno Smith going 17 of 21 for three touchdowns and the Jets converting 9 of 11 third downs. The Chargers fell to 1-9, with Justin Herbert sacked six times.

The rest of the weekend filled in the picture. The Houston Texans beat the Indianapolis Colts 26-3 on Thursday night, with C.J. Stroud throwing three touchdowns and the defense holding the Colts to 167 yards. The Chicago Bears beat the New Orleans Saints 21-13, with Jeffery Simmons recording three of Chicago's six sacks and Caleb Williams rebounding from a four-interception game with an efficient outing — 15 of 23 for 181 yards. The Bears are 8-2, and they have won two straight despite playing well below their best.

The firings, the injuries, and the free agent

Monday was also a day of hard decisions around the league. The Arizona Cardinals announced that defensive coordinator Nick Rallis has been dismissed, with senior defensive assistant Teryl Austin taking over on an interim basis. The decision followed Sunday's 34-7 defeat against Kansas City, which dropped the Cardinals to 3-8. It was a lopsided loss, and the organization decided that a change was necessary.

The New York Giants lost cornerback Greg Newsome to a high ankle sprain, with head coach John Harbaugh confirming on Monday that Newsome will miss multiple weeks. The injury occurred during Sunday's 23-18 victory over Jacksonville, and it is a blow to a defense that has been the foundation of the Giants' season. And Daniel Jones, the quarterback once paid like a franchise player, cleared waivers on Monday and became a free agent — the final chapter in a fall from grace that has been one of the league's most-watched stories.

These are the moves that define a season as much as the games do. A fired coordinator, an injured starter, a released star, a quarterback on waivers — each one is a team making a judgment about its future, and each one will shape the weeks ahead.

What Week 11 means for the playoff race

With six weeks left in the regular season, the playoff picture is coming into focus. In the NFC, the 49ers lead at 9-1, with the Bears and Lions at 8-2, the Bengals at 7-3, and the Giants at 6-3-1. The Vikings, who started 6-0, have now lost three of four and sit at 7-3, with Kirk Cousins throwing a critical pick-six in his second start since joining Minnesota at the trade deadline. The race for the NFC's top seed — and home-field advantage throughout the playoffs — runs through San Francisco, and the 49ers have the inside track.

In the AFC, the Bills lead at 9-1, with the Chiefs and Broncos at 8-2, the Bengals at 7-3, and the Jaguars at 7-3 atop the AFC South. The Thanksgiving night matchup between the Chiefs and the Bills will go a long way toward deciding the conference's top seed. The 49ers face another test of their Super Bowl credentials next Sunday at home against the defending champion Seattle Seahawks, who are 6-4 and were one of six teams on their bye this week.

The most remarkable fact of the week may be the simplest one: all four teams favored by double digits handled their business, avoiding letdowns. The favorites won. The contenders contended. And the playoff race, with six weeks to go, is as tight and as compelling as it has been in years.

Thanksgiving week: what comes next

Week 12 starts with three Thanksgiving games, and the schedule is a gift for football fans. On Thursday, the Bears visit the Lions, the Eagles visit the Cowboys, and the Chiefs visit the Bills in prime time. Every team plays in Week 12, with no byes, and the week opens on Wednesday night with Green Bay at the Rams — two 6-4 teams coming off their bye weeks.

The Thanksgiving games are the centerpiece. Chicago at Detroit is a divisional rivalry with playoff implications. Philadelphia at Dallas is a rivalry that needs no stakes to matter. And Kansas City at Buffalo, under the lights on Thanksgiving night, is the game of the week — perhaps the game of the season so far — with the AFC's top seed potentially on the line.

For the Commanders, the win over Cincinnati is a foundation to build on. For the Bengals, it is a loss to learn from. For the 49ers, the Mexico City win and the Aiyuk release are a statement of intent. And for the league, Week 11 was a reminder of why the NFL dominates American sports: because on any given Sunday — or Monday night — 39 seconds is enough to change everything.

The bottom line

Jayden Daniels drove Washington 64 yards in 39 seconds. Drew Stevens kicked the winner as time expired. The Commanders stunned the Bengals 22-20, snapping a five-game winning streak and completing an NFC East sweep. The 49ers thrashed the Vikings 35-10 in Mexico City, improved to 9-1, and then released Brandon Aiyuk. The Bills reached 9-1 for the first time since 1991. The Chiefs, Lions, Broncos, and Cowboys all won. The Giants made a statement. The Eagles ended a seven-game skid. And Thanksgiving week arrives with the Chiefs at the Bills in prime time.

Week 11 had everything — a walk-off field goal, a blowout in Mexico City, a star released, a coordinator fired, and a playoff race tighter than ever. Week 12, with three Thanksgiving games and every team in action, promises more of the same. The season is entering its final stretch, and the games are only getting bigger.
