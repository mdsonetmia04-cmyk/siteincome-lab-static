Marvel has just broken its own record — again. The final trailer for Avengers: Doomsday, released on Monday, November 23, 2026, drew 563 million views in its first 24 hours, making it the most-viewed trailer in Disney history and the second most-viewed movie trailer of all time. The figure was reported by Deadline and carried across trade and entertainment outlets on Tuesday, November 25, and it lands with just over three weeks to go before the film opens in theatres on December 18, 2026.

The number is staggering on its own, but what makes it remarkable is what it beat. The previous Disney record belonged to the very same film: the first Avengers: Doomsday teaser, released in July 2026, drew 503 million views in its first 24 hours. The final trailer cleared that bar by 60 million views — and it did so as a follow-up trailer, the kind of asset that almost always draws fewer views than the first look. The only trailer in movie history that has ever drawn more in a single day is Spider-Man: Brand New Day, which pulled in 718.6 million views in March 2026. That leaves Doomsday 155.6 million views behind the all-time record — and, for the first time, within realistic striking distance of it.

This is not a small story about a big number. Trailer views have become the film industry's earliest and most public test of demand, and a 563-million-view day is the loudest possible signal that Avengers: Doomsday is not just the most anticipated film of December — it may be the most anticipated film of the decade. The trailer ranked number one, two and three on YouTube's Movie Trending charts within a day of release, according to The Hollywood Reporter India, and across the whole promotional campaign the film has now accumulated approximately 3.3 billion views and 4.8 million social media mentions, per the Times of India. This is the full story of the record: the numbers, the all-time chart it now sits on, what the three-minute-fifteen-second trailer actually shows, the cast it assembles from three different universes, the Robert Downey Jr. gamble at its centre, and what 563 million views in a day means for a film that opens on December 18.

The Record in Numbers: 563 Million Views in One Day

Start with the headline figure, because it is the one that will be quoted for years. According to Deadline, the final trailer for Avengers: Doomsday was watched 563 million times in the 24 hours after its release on Monday, November 23, 2026. That makes it the single most-viewed trailer Disney has ever released, across every film in the studio's history, and the second most-viewed movie trailer ever released by any studio.

The comparison that matters most is the one inside Disney's own catalogue. The previous record holder was the first Avengers: Doomsday teaser, which drew 503 million views in its first day when it arrived in July 2026. The final trailer beat it by 60 million views — a margin of roughly 12 percent. Before that, the Disney record belonged to the Super Bowl teaser for Deadpool & Wolverine, which drew 365 million views in 24 hours in 2024, including the television broadcast audience. And before that, the record was held by Spider-Man: No Way Home, with 355.5 million views in August 2021. Avengers: Endgame, the biggest film of all time at the box office, drew 289 million views for its first trailer in December 2018 — barely more than half of what the Doomsday final trailer managed in a day.

Put another way, the Doomsday final trailer drew almost twice as many first-day views as the trailer for Avengers: Endgame, the film it is most directly following. The scale of trailer launches has grown enormously in eight years, but even against that growth, 563 million is an outlier. It is also worth noting how the record was set: the trailer was released as a surprise drop, without the kind of global promotional build-up that accompanied the Spider-Man: Brand New Day launch. The Times of India noted that the two trailers had different release strategies — Brand New Day arrived as part of a global promotional campaign, while the Doomsday final trailer was released without a similar build-up — and still came within 156 million views of the all-time record.

The response was immediate and measurable beyond the view count. The trailer took the top three spots on YouTube's Movie Trending charts on Tuesday, November 24, just a day after release, according to The Hollywood Reporter India. The campaign as a whole has generated around 4.8 million social media mentions, and the final trailer ranked as the second most-discussed movie trailer since 2023 — behind only the film's own earlier teaser, which recorded approximately one million mentions. The numbers describe a film that is not merely anticipated but actively dominating the conversation, three weeks before it opens.

The All-Time Trailer Chart: Only One Film Stands Above Doomsday

The 563-million-view day puts Avengers: Doomsday at number two on the all-time list of most-viewed movie trailers in their first 24 hours, and the list it now sits on is a short history of modern blockbuster marketing. Here is how the top of that chart looks, according to the figures reported by Deadline and compiled by the Khaleej Times.

At number one is Spider-Man: Brand New Day, with 718.6 million views in 24 hours. Tom Holland's fourth outing as the web-slinger broke the record when its trailer arrived in March 2026, and it did so fast — the trailer had already become the most-watched ever within eight hours of launch, reaching 373 million views worldwide in that window. The film went on to open to a domestic box-office record of 360 million dollars, according to The Hollywood Reporter India, which makes the trailer record look less like a curiosity and more like a genuine leading indicator of demand.

At number two is the Avengers: Doomsday final trailer, with 563 million views. At number three is the film's own first teaser, with 503 million views from July 2026 — which means Marvel now holds two of the top three spots on the all-time chart with the same film. At number four is the Deadpool & Wolverine Super Bowl teaser, with 365 million views in 24 hours in 2024, including broadcast viewing — a figure that stood as the Disney record for more than two years. At number five is Spider-Man: No Way Home, with 355.5 million views in August 2021. And at number six is Avengers: Endgame, with 289 million views in December 2018.

Two details in that chart deserve attention. The first is that every film on it is a Marvel film, which tells you something about where the biggest trailer audiences in the world now live. The second is the gap between the top two and the rest: the Doomsday final trailer drew nearly 200 million more first-day views than the Deadpool & Wolverine teaser that held the Disney record before it. The chart has not just been topped — it has been rearranged.

There is one more comparison that flatters Doomsday even further. The final trailer for Spider-Man: Brand New Day — the follow-up trailer for the record holder — drew only 131 million views in its first 24 hours. The Doomsday final trailer drew more than three times that number, and more than four times it by some counts. In the final-trailer race, which is the fairest like-for-like comparison, Doomsday is not second. It is first, by an enormous margin.

Why a Final Trailer Beating the Teaser Is So Unusual

The strangest thing about the 563-million-view day is not the size of the number. It is the kind of trailer that produced it. In the normal life cycle of a blockbuster marketing campaign, the first trailer is the event. It carries the reveal, the surprise, the first look at the thing fans have been waiting years to see. Every trailer after that is a reminder — a little more footage, a little more story, aimed at people who are already interested. Follow-up trailers almost always draw fewer views than the first, because the novelty is gone and the audience has already decided whether it cares.

Avengers: Doomsday has now broken that rule twice. The first teaser drew 503 million views in July. The final trailer, released four months later, drew 563 million — 60 million more. That is not supposed to happen, and it has not happened at this scale before. The usual explanation for a late trailer outperforming an early one is that the later trailer contains something the earlier one did not — a reveal big enough to pull back the people who had already made up their minds. In this case, the trailer's contents explain the surge, and they explain why the conversation around the film changed overnight.

The three-minute-fifteen-second trailer is the longest look at the film Marvel has released, and it is the first time the film has had a story rather than just a cast list. It opens not with a battle but with a family. Steve Rogers, played by Chris Evans, and Peggy Carter, played by Hayley Atwell, are shown living the quiet life Steve chose at the end of Avengers: Endgame — and raising a teenage son. The boy, whose name is revealed as Steven Grant Rogers, asks his father whether he was a superhero. Steve and Peggy argue about what to tell him. The trailer then cuts to the Fantastic Four arriving at Steve's home, travelling back in time to bring him into the present, where he is expected to lead the Avengers in their battle against Doctor Doom.

That is the emotional core of the trailer, and it is the reason the view count exploded. For seven years, since Endgame ended with Steve Rogers choosing a life with Peggy, fans have wondered what that life looked like. The final trailer answers the question in the most dramatic way possible: he has a son, the son does not know who his father was, and the past is coming to take him back. The trailer also carries the film's thesis in a single line from Doctor Doom, played by Robert Downey Jr.: "All of you have lived stolen lives. But now you must give them back." The idea is that the Avengers' choices in Endgame — the time mission that undid the Snap — had consequences, and Doom has come to collect.

The action in the trailer delivers the spectacle the numbers promise. Doctor Doom and his Sentinels face the Avengers and the X-Men in a battle that brings together heroes from three different universes. Cyclops, played by James Marsden, delivers the line X-Men fans have waited decades to hear in live action: "To me, my X-Men!" Nightcrawler teleports Gambit, Mystique and Beast into the battle. Mister Fantastic, Shang-Chi, Thor and Yelena Belova are all shown joining the fight. Thor, according to ComicBook.com, is getting a massive upgrade, and the trailer also teases the Young Avengers potentially joining the Marvel Cinematic Universe — a detail that sent fan speculation into overdrive within hours of release.

The threat itself is existential. The film centres on incursions — collisions between universes that could destroy one or both worlds — and the trailer frames the Avengers themselves as partly responsible for what is happening. As Steve Rogers explains in the footage, the heroes did what was needed to make the world right years ago, but they did not know their choices would have consequences. That framing gives the film a moral weight that the first teaser, which was mostly a cast parade, did not have. It also gives audiences a reason to watch a trailer they had already, in a sense, seen — because this one finally tells them what the film is about.

Three Universes, One Film: The Cast the Trailer Assembles

The scale of the cast is the other reason the trailer travelled so far. Marvel says the film assembles the Avengers, the X-Men and the Fantastic Four on screen for the first time in Marvel's cinematic history, with heroes from three different Earths who must band together or risk extinction. The final trailer is the first footage to show that assembly actually happening, and the roll call is the longest in the history of the franchise.

The returning Avengers are led by Chris Evans as Steve Rogers, back as Captain America after the Endgame farewell that was supposed to be final, and Chris Hemsworth as Thor, who in the trailer admits he does not know who half of the assembled heroes are. Paul Rudd returns as Ant-Man, Letitia Wright as Black Panther, Sebastian Stan as the Winter Soldier, Tom Hiddleston as Loki and Simu Liu as Shang-Chi. Anthony Mackie returns as Sam Wilson, the new Captain America, and the Wakandan delegation also includes M'Baku, played by Winston Duke, and Namor, played by Tenoch Huerta Mejia.

The Fantastic Four arrive as a complete unit, with Pedro Pascal as Mister Fantastic, Vanessa Kirby as the Invisible Woman, Joseph Quinn as the Human Torch and Ebon Moss-Bachrach as the Thing — the cast of The Fantastic Four: First Steps, returning to face their nemesis. The trailer shows them travelling back in time to recruit Steve Rogers, which makes them the film's bridge between the old Avengers and the new threat.

The X-Men contingent is the one that carries the most nostalgia, and the trailer knows it. Patrick Stewart returns as Professor Charles Xavier, Ian McKellen as Magneto, James Marsden as Cyclops, Kelsey Grammer as Beast, Rebecca Romijn as Mystique, Alan Cumming as Nightcrawler and Channing Tatum as Gambit — actors from 20th Century Fox's X-Men films, some of whom have not played these characters in more than a decade. The sight of Marsden's Cyclops shouting the team's battle cry in live action is the trailer's single most-shared moment, and it is the clearest sign that Marvel is treating this film as a reunion as much as a sequel.

The New Avengers, also known as the Thunderbolts, complete the line-up. Florence Pugh returns as Yelena Belova, alongside her Thunderbolts co-stars David Harbour as Red Guardian, Lewis Pullman as Sentry, Wyatt Russell as U.S. Agent and Hannah John-Kamen as Ghost. The trailer also introduces new cast members, including Kate Bishop, played by Hailee Steinfeld, expanding on the character-focused teasers Marvel released earlier in the campaign.

The film is directed by Anthony and Joe Russo, the brothers behind Avengers: Infinity War and Avengers: Endgame, and produced by Kevin Feige, Louis D'Esposito and Jonathan Schwartz. It runs for roughly 150 minutes, according to co-director Joe Russo — almost exactly the length of Infinity War, which ran 149 minutes, and well short of Endgame's 181 minutes. That runtime matters, because it suggests a film that is epic in scope but disciplined in shape, built to be watched in a single sitting rather than endured.

Robert Downey Jr. as Doctor Doom: The Gamble at the Centre of the Film

No single decision explains the 563-million-view day better than the casting at the film's centre. Robert Downey Jr., the actor who built the Marvel Cinematic Universe as Tony Stark, returns to the franchise as its villain — Doctor Doom. The character is reportedly a variant of Tony Stark from another universe, which sets up a confrontation unlike anything Marvel has attempted: the hero who defined the franchise, played by the same actor, now standing on the other side.

The casting was the single biggest talking point of the first teaser in July, and it remains the single biggest talking point of the final trailer. Downey's Doom delivers the trailer's thesis line — "All of you have lived stolen lives. But now you must give them back" — and the footage shows him commanding Sentinels against the combined forces of the Avengers and the X-Men. The trailer does not show his face in full, and it does not explain how a Tony Stark variant became Doctor Doom, but it does not need to. The idea alone is enough to drive conversation, and the conversation is what drove the view count.

The gamble is enormous. Downey's Tony Stark died in Endgame in one of the most emotional scenes in blockbuster history, and bringing the actor back as a different character risks cheapening that farewell. The trailer's answer to that risk is to make the return the point: Doom's accusation is that the Avengers' lives are stolen, that their happy endings were bought at a cost, and that the bill has come due. If the film can make that argument land, the casting becomes the masterstroke of the decade. If it cannot, it becomes the most expensive nostalgia play ever attempted.

The early signs favour the optimists. The trailer's reception has been overwhelmingly positive, the view count is a record, and the discussion around the film has shifted from whether the casting works to what it means. The film's premiere is set for December 14 at the Intuit Dome in Los Angeles, four days before the theatrical release, and the first reactions from that premiere will be the next major test. Until then, the trailer is the only evidence anyone has — and 563 million views is a lot of evidence.

The Campaign So Far: 3.3 Billion Views and a Pre-Sales Record

The final trailer's record does not stand alone. It is the latest peak in a marketing campaign that has been breaking records since it began, and the campaign's own numbers help explain why the trailer travelled so far.

Marvel kicked off the marketing last December with the launch of four teaser trailers, and the official trailer launched on July 20, 2026. That first full look set its own enormous bar, drawing 503 million views in its first 24 hours, and Disney opened a separate ticket sale to coincide with it — a sale that took 16.5 million dollars on its first day, according to Comic Basics. The first trailer also bagged what The Hollywood Reporter India described as a historic, record-breaking pre-sales run within just 12 hours of its July release. The demand was already there in July. The final trailer proved it had only grown.

The demand is visible in India, too, where the film has already made trade headlines. Advance booking for Avengers: Doomsday in India crossed 10 crore rupees in just 24 hours, according to trade reports carried earlier this month — a figure this blog covered when the bookings opened. The film is slated to release in India on December 17, a day before the worldwide release on December 18, and the Indian market has become one of the most important battlegrounds for Marvel's biggest films.

Across the entire promotional campaign so far, the film has accumulated approximately 3.3 billion views and 4.8 million social media mentions, according to the Times of India. That total covers every trailer, teaser and piece of footage released since the campaign began, and it makes Doomsday one of the most-discussed films in the history of social media measurement. The final trailer alone accounts for more than a sixth of that total, in a single day.

The campaign's success is also visible in what happened around it. The theatrical re-release of Avengers: Endgame, retitled Avengers: Endgame Encore, opened at number one at the domestic box office with 26 million dollars, according to Asianet News — a remarkable result for a seven-year-old film, and a sign that audiences were using the re-release as a warm-up for Doomsday. The re-release also carried the newly added scene featuring Steve Rogers and Peggy Carter that the final trailer picks up from, which means the trailer's biggest reveal was seeded in cinemas weeks before it arrived online.

What 563 Million Views Means for the Box Office

Trailer views are not box-office grosses, and it is important to say so plainly. A view is free, a ticket is not, and the history of Hollywood is full of trailers that were watched by hundreds of millions of people for films that then underperformed. But at this scale, the correlation is hard to ignore, and the trade press is already treating the record as a box-office signal.

Deadline, which first reported the 563-million figure, believes the Russo brothers' superhero epic is bound for some sort of box-office opening record, according to Yardbarker's account of the report. That is not a prediction of a specific number, but it is a meaningful statement from the outlet that tracks the industry's numbers most closely. The reasoning is straightforward: the trailer for Spider-Man: Brand New Day drew 718.6 million views and the film opened to a domestic record of 360 million dollars. The Doomsday final trailer drew 563 million views — the second-highest total ever — for a film that is the first Avengers movie since Endgame, the biggest film of all time. If the trailer-to-ticket relationship holds even loosely, the opening weekend will be historic.

The context supports that reading. Avengers: Endgame opened to 357 million dollars domestically in 2019, a record that stood until Brand New Day passed it this year. Doomsday is the first Avengers film in seven years, it reunites the original cast with the Fantastic Four and the X-Men, and it carries the biggest marketing campaign Marvel has ever run. The pre-sales run in July, the 16.5-million-dollar first day of ticket sales, the 10-crore Indian advance booking in 24 hours and the 26-million-dollar Endgame Encore re-release all point in the same direction: demand that is not just high but unusually broad, spanning the core Marvel audience and the casual viewers who only turn up for the biggest events.

There are risks, and they are worth naming. The film's reported budget has not been officially confirmed, and the break-even point for a film of this scale will be enormous — well over a billion dollars worldwide by any reasonable estimate. A record trailer does not guarantee a record opening, and a record opening does not guarantee a profitable run. The film also opens in a crowded December, and its legs will depend on whether audiences who loved the trailer love the film itself. But the trailer has done its job. It has made Avengers: Doomsday the film that everyone is talking about three weeks before release, and in the modern box office, that is half the battle.

What Happens Next: Premiere, Release and the Road to Secret Wars

The next three weeks will be the final stretch of the campaign, and the calendar is already set. The film premieres on December 14 at the Intuit Dome in Los Angeles, where the first reactions from critics and fans will land within hours. The theatrical release follows on December 18, 2026 worldwide, with India getting the film a day earlier on December 17. The film is the first Avengers movie since Endgame in 2019, and the first chapter of a two-part story that concludes with Avengers: Secret Wars, scheduled for December 17, 2027.

The opening weekend will answer the question the trailer has raised: whether 563 million views in a day converts into the biggest opening in Marvel history. The advance booking numbers in India, the pre-sales run in July and the Endgame Encore re-release all suggest the demand is real, but the opening weekend is the only number that counts. If the film opens anywhere near the Brand New Day record of 360 million dollars domestically, it will confirm that the trailer record was a genuine signal. If it opens well below that, the industry will have a new case study in the limits of trailer views as a predictor.

Either way, the trailer has already changed the conversation around the film. Before Monday, Avengers: Doomsday was a film with a huge cast and a famous villain. After Monday, it is a film with a story — a father, a son, a stolen life, a bill come due — and a record that puts it second only to Spider-Man in the history of movie marketing. The final trailer did what final trailers are not supposed to do: it made the film bigger than its first look. That is why 563 million people watched it in a day, and why the next three weeks will be the most closely watched countdown in years.

The Bottom Line

The 563-million-view day is a record, but it is also a message. It says that the audience for Avengers: Doomsday is not just the Marvel faithful who watched the first teaser in July — it is bigger now than it was then, and it is still growing three weeks before release. It says that a final trailer can outperform a first look when it finally gives people a story to care about. And it says that the film's central gamble — Robert Downey Jr. as Doctor Doom, Chris Evans as a father with a secret, and the Avengers, the Fantastic Four and the X-Men on screen together for the first time — has landed with the audience it was made for.

Whether that audience turns up on December 18 is the only question left, and it is the one that no trailer, however record-breaking, can answer in advance. But if the opening weekend looks anything like the first 24 hours of the final trailer, the box office is about to see something it has never seen before. For now, the scoreboard reads: Spider-Man 718.6 million, Doomsday 563 million, and everything else a long way behind.
