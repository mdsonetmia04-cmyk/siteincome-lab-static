TITLE: Isak Scores Twice as Liverpool Win 3-0 — Now 1 Game From Salah's Away Record LABELS: Sports SEARCH DESCRIPTION: Alexander Isak scored twice as Liverpool beat Club Brugge 3-0 in the Champions League, moving one away game from Mohamed Salah's club record ---BODY---

Alexander Isak in action for Sweden. The striker scored twice as Liverpool beat Club Brugge 3-0 in the Champions League, taking his season tally to 10 goals. Photo: Rolandhino1 via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0.

Alexander Isak scored twice as Liverpool beat Club Brugge 3-0 in the Champions League on Wednesday night, November 25, 2026, and in doing so moved to within one away game of a club record that has belonged to Mohamed Salah for five years. The Swedish striker's brace at the Jan Breydelstadion in Bruges took his tally for the season to 10 goals and extended his extraordinary run of scoring in six consecutive away matches for Liverpool — a feat that, in the Premier League era, only Salah has ever bettered. One more goal on the road, and Isak will stand level with the Egyptian. Two more, and the record will be his alone.

It was a night that belonged to Isak, but it was also a night that belonged to Liverpool. The 3-0 victory — sealed by goals from Isak in the 13th and 82nd minutes, either side of a superb strike from Florian Wirtz in the 79th — moved the Reds up to fifth in the 36-team Champions League table, with 12 points from five matches and four wins from five in the league phase. It was their third straight win in all competitions, it followed Sunday's 4-0 thrashing of Manchester United, and it was achieved with a performance that combined ruthlessness in front of goal with a defensive solidity that has not always been present this season. Liverpool also became the first team to shut out Club Brugge in more than nine months, and kept their first clean sheet in the Champions League this season.

For a club that had lost to Fenerbahce in their last European encounter, the victory in Belgium was a statement of intent. Liverpool are now in an excellent position to finish in the top eight of the league phase and go straight through to the last 16, without the need for a play-off round in February. And they did it with their star striker in the form of his life, their German playmaker scoring for the third game in a row, and their goalkeeper making history of his own.

The goals: a gift, a beauty, and a chip

The opening goal arrived in the 13th minute, and it was a gift. Club Brugge goalkeeper Yann Sommer — the experienced Swiss international — played a sloppy pass out from the back, and Bradley Barcola intercepted it. Barcola played Dominik Szoboszlai in down the left, and the Hungarian squared the ball for Isak to fire under Sommer from close range. It was a goal born of pressing, of punishing a mistake, and of the kind of sharpness in the final third that Liverpool have shown in abundance during this run of form. For Isak, it was his sixth successive away game with a goal, and it set the tone for everything that followed.

For long stretches after that, the game was tighter than the scoreline would eventually suggest. Club Brugge, playing at home in front of their own supporters, created chances of their own. Szoboszlai was needed at the other end to clear Nicolo Tresoldi's header to safety, and Felix Lemarechal nodded straight at Alisson Becker at the back post moments later. Liverpool, for their part, could not find the second goal that would have killed the game: Rio Ngumoha, who came on to replace the Spaniard, almost doubled the lead early in the second half, but after skipping beyond two Brugge defenders, he fired just wide. A free-flowing move saw Wirtz's brilliant pass laid off by Isak and into the path of Szoboszlai, but with just Sommer to beat, the stand-in Reds captain fired straight at the goalkeeper. Alisson then had to be alert at his near post to keep out Lemarechal's stinging drive.

It was not until 11 minutes from the end of normal time that Liverpool could breathe easily. Wirtz, whose brilliant run of form has been one of the stories of Liverpool's season, doubled the lead in the 79th minute with a classy bending strike, capping a move that showcased everything the German brings to this team: the movement, the technique, the composure in front of goal. It was Wirtz's third consecutive game with a goal — the first time he has done so as a Liverpool player, and the first time at club level since September 2024, when he was still at Bayer Leverkusen.

Three minutes later, Isak had the final say. Alisson Becker sent a long ball forward from his own penalty area, and when the Brugge defender Brandon Mechele misjudged it, Isak raced clear of the goalkeeper and lifted a sublime chipped finish over Sommer. It was a goal of rare quality — the kind of finish that separates the very best strikers from the merely good — and it put the game beyond doubt. It was also, in its own way, a historic goal: it took Isak to 10 for the season, it completed a brace that had begun with a tap-in and ended with a chip, and it left him one away game from Salah's record.

One game from Salah

The record that Isak is chasing is a specific and revealing one. The 27-year-old has now scored in six consecutive away matches for Liverpool, a run that dates back to his winner in the 1-0 victory at Bournemouth on September 20. In the Premier League era, the only other Liverpool player to score in six successive away games is Mohamed Salah — and Salah went one better, scoring in seven consecutive away matches between August and October 2021, when he scored seven times in a row on the road.

That puts Isak's achievement in context. Salah's 2021 run is remembered as one of the great individual hot streaks in Premier League history: a period in which the Egyptian was, by common consent, the best player in the world, scoring goals of extraordinary quality week after week, home and away. To be mentioned in the same breath as that run — to have matched the kind of away form that usually belongs to a long, sustained peak rather than a single autumn — is a measure of how well Isak is playing.

The numbers behind the run are striking. Among all Premier League players, Isak has now scored the joint-most away goals across all competitions this season — eight, level with Erling Haaland. His brace in Bruges took his tally for the season to 10 goals, and his display was rated at 9.74 by one statistical service — a reflection not just of the two goals, but of the quality of his all-round play. As one report put it, Isak's display did more than settle the match: it took him to 10 for the season and left him with six consecutive away games scored in for Liverpool, one short of Salah's club benchmark.

There is an important nuance to the record. Salah's seven away games were all in a single run in 2021; Isak's six have come across the autumn of 2026, in a mixture of league and European fixtures. The comparison is not exact, and it does not need to be: what matters is that a Liverpool striker is scoring away from home with a consistency that the club has seen only once before in the modern era, and that the man who set the standard is still at the club, still scoring, and still — for now — one game ahead.

Isak's own journey to this point has been a remarkable one. The Swedish international, who joined Liverpool amid enormous expectation, has had to adapt to a new league, a new team and a new level of scrutiny. His early weeks at Anfield were promising without being spectacular; his recent form has been something else entirely. The winner at Bournemouth on September 20 started the run. The goals since then — in the league, in Europe, home and away — have turned a promising signing into the focal point of Liverpool's attack. And the brace in Bruges, scored with the composure of a striker at the very top of his game, suggested that the run is not about to end.

Alisson's assist: a goalkeeper makes history

Isak's second goal had a second author, and his contribution deserves its own paragraph — because Alisson Becker achieved something on Wednesday night that no Liverpool goalkeeper had ever done before. The Brazilian became the first Liverpool goalkeeper to record an assist in the Champions League, when his long pass up the pitch set up Isak's second goal and completed the scoring in the 3-0 victory away at Club Brugge.

That long ball took Alisson's assists total for the Reds to four in all competitions, a figure that puts him level with Pepe Reina and Bruce Grobbelaar — two of the greatest goalkeepers in Liverpool's history. His record began on January 19, 2020, when he set up Mohamed Salah against Manchester United in the Premier League. The pass became one of the memorable moments of Liverpool's season, and it demonstrated that Alisson could influence the attack as well as protect his goal. He provided another assist for Salah on February 19, 2022, this time against Norwich City. His third assist came on October 16, 2022, again for Salah, in a Premier League meeting with Manchester City. The latest assist, for Isak against Club Brugge, completed a landmark sequence and added a European dimension to Alisson's record.

It is a record that says something important about the modern game — and about Liverpool's way of playing it. The goalkeeper is no longer just the last line of defence; he is the first line of attack. Alisson's ability to pick out a pass over 60 or 70 yards, to turn defence into attack in a single kick, is a weapon that few teams in world football possess, and it was on full display in Bruges. That the pass led directly to a goal — and to a piece of club history — was fitting. It was also, as the club itself noted, the first time a Liverpool goalkeeper had ever assisted a goal in the Champions League, a competition the club has won six times.

Wirtz, Szoboszlai and the team around Isak

A night built around one player can be dismissed as a hot streak, but this was a 3-0 Champions League away win with Wirtz on the scoresheet and Szoboszlai supplying the opener. Liverpool left Bruges with three goals, three points and a striker who is now one away game from another club mark — and with a team performance that suggested the whole side is coming into form at the right time.

Florian Wirtz's contribution deserves emphasis. The German playmaker, signed amid huge expectation, has taken time to settle in England, as many overseas signings do. But his recent form — a goal against Manchester United on Sunday, followed by another sensational display against Club Brugge — suggests that the adaptation is complete. His 79th-minute strike in Bruges was his third goal in three consecutive appearances, and it was the kind of goal that explains why Liverpool paid so much for him: a bending, precise finish from a player who makes the difficult look easy. After enjoying a fruitful international break with Germany, Wirtz has followed up his display against United with another goal, and he is now scoring with the regularity that Liverpool hoped for when they signed him.

Dominik Szoboszlai, the stand-in captain, was central to the victory in a different way. It was his pass that set up Isak's opener, after Barcola's interception, and his presence — clearing Tresoldi's header at one end, driving forward at the other — embodied the work rate that has become a hallmark of this Liverpool side. That he fired straight at Sommer when clean through will frustrate him, but his overall contribution was that of a captain: everywhere, involved in everything, setting the tempo for the team.

Andoni Iraola's men, as one report described them, backed up their 4-0 thrashing of Manchester United last Sunday with an assured performance in Belgium. The reference to Iraola is a reminder of how much has changed at Liverpool: the manager who has built this team, who has integrated Isak and Wirtz into a side that can win 3-0 away in the Champions League and 4-0 at home against Manchester United in the same week, is overseeing a team that is beginning to look like a genuine force in Europe again.

What the win means for the Champions League

The victory moved Liverpool up to fifth in the Champions League table, with 12 points from five matches — level with Como, Barcelona and Real Betis, and firmly in the mix for a top-eight finish. In the new Champions League format, the top eight teams in the 36-team league phase go straight through to the last 16, while the teams finishing ninth to 24th must play a two-legged play-off in February. Finishing in the top eight is therefore worth a great deal: it means fewer fixtures, more rest, and a kinder draw.

With four wins from five league phase games, Liverpool are in an excellent spot to achieve that. They have three matches remaining in the league phase, and their form — three straight wins in all competitions, 10 goals scored in their last two matches, a clean sheet in Bruges — suggests they will take some stopping. The defeat to Fenerbahce in their last European encounter before Bruges was a reminder that this competition punishes complacency, but the response since then has been emphatic.

Club Brugge, for their part, will reflect on a night when they were competitive for long stretches but ultimately outclassed. They were the first team to be shut out by Liverpool in the Champions League this season, and the first team to fail to score against the Belgians in more than nine months — a statistic that, turned around, says as much about Brugge's own attacking record at home as it does about Liverpool's defence. Sommer's error for the opening goal will haunt him, but the Belgian champions created enough chances to have made the game closer than it was, and they will take encouragement from the spells in which they troubled Liverpool's back line.

What comes next — and why the record matters

The question now is not whether Isak can keep scoring, but where the run will take him — and Liverpool. The next away fixture is the one every Liverpool supporter will have circled, because a goal there would draw Isak level with Salah on seven consecutive away games, and a goal in the away game after that would give him a record of his own. Records of this kind matter more than they might seem. They are not just numbers in a table. They are markers of consistency, of a striker who delivers not once or twice but week after week, in different stadiums, against different defences, under different kinds of pressure. A player who scores in six straight away games is not lucky. He is reliable. And reliability, away from home, is the quality that wins leagues and decides European ties.

For Liverpool, the timing of Isak's form could hardly be better. The season is entering its most demanding phase: the league phase of the Champions League has three matches remaining, the domestic fixture list is congested, and the winter months are when squads are tested to their limits. A striker in the form of his life is the difference between a team that competes and a team that wins. Isak's 10 goals this season have come at a rate that puts him among the most productive forwards in Europe, and his eight away goals — level with Haaland among Premier League players — underline that he does his best work in the hardest places.

There is also the matter of what this run does for the player himself. Confidence is the most valuable currency a striker can hold, and Isak is currently rich in it. The chipped finish over Sommer in the 82nd minute was not the finish of a player hoping for the best; it was the finish of a player who knew, before the ball left his foot, exactly where it was going. That is the difference between a striker in form and a striker out of it, and it is the difference that Liverpool will be relying on in the weeks ahead. The record chase adds pressure, of course — every away game now carries the weight of expectation — but it also adds motivation, and Isak has shown no sign of being burdened by it.

For Salah, watching from the same dressing room, the situation is a curious one. His record, set between August and October 2021, has stood for five years as a marker of his own extraordinary peak. Now a teammate is one game away from equalling it. There is no rivalry here — Salah's own assists record, and his place in Liverpool's history, are secure — but there is a quiet symmetry to it: the record that Isak is chasing was set by the man whose goals have defined Liverpool's modern era, and the fact that the next man to approach it wears the same shirt is a measure of the standards the club now sets for itself. If Isak does equal or break the record, it will be a moment for both men: for Salah, a reminder of what he achieved; for Isak, proof that he belongs in the same conversation.

A striker, a record, and a season taking shape

For Liverpool, the significance of Wednesday night goes beyond three points and a place in the top eight. It is about the emergence of a striker who is scoring at a rate that the club has rarely seen, about a playmaker who is finding his feet, about a goalkeeper who is rewriting the record books, and about a team that is beginning to believe in itself again.

Isak's run — six consecutive away games with a goal, one short of Salah's seven — is the headline, and rightly so. But it is also a symptom of something larger: a Liverpool side that is creating chances for its striker, that is pressing high and winning the ball in dangerous areas, that is defending well enough to keep clean sheets in Europe, and that is winning matches in different ways — by four goals at home against Manchester United, by three goals away in Bruges, by grinding out a 1-0 win at Bournemouth that started it all.

The next away game will be watched with particular interest. If Isak scores, he will equal Salah's record of seven consecutive away games — a record that has stood since October 2021. If he scores in the one after that, the record will be his, and the comparison with Salah will take on a new dimension: not just a striker matching a great, but a striker surpassing him. For now, though, the record sits one away match away, and the pressure is on — not just on Isak, but on the defences that must try to stop him.

As one report put it after the match: a night built around one player can be dismissed as a hot streak, but this was a 3-0 Champions League away win with Wirtz on the scoresheet and Szoboszlai supplying the opener. Liverpool left Club Brugge with three goals, three points and a striker who is now one away game from another club mark. It was a good night for Liverpool. For Alexander Isak, it was the night when a record that seemed untouchable began to look reachable — and for the rest of Europe, it was a warning that Liverpool, and their Swedish striker, are coming into form at exactly the right time.
