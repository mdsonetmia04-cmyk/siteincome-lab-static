TITLE: Hasina Plans December 16 Return to Bangladesh — Government Rules Out Biman Flight LABELS: Politics, National ---BODY---

Bangladesh's politics was jolted on Tuesday by the biggest story of the day: ousted former prime minister Sheikh Hasina plans to return to the country on December 16, Victory Day, and has asked the government to arrange her return on a Biman Bangladesh Airlines flight. The government has responded with a firm refusal, saying there is no possibility of sending an aircraft to bring her back and that if she returns, she will have to return in handcuffs.

From Tuesday morning, the story led the headlines of nearly every television channel and online news outlet in the country. Channel 24, Jamuna Television, Desh TV and Channel One all carried it as their top story throughout the day. On one side is Hasina's announcement of a return; on the other, the government's tough stand. Together, they have created fresh tension in the country's politics around December 16.

What the spokesperson said

Hasina's spokesperson told the news agency Reuters on Tuesday that the former prime minister plans to return to Bangladesh on December 16, 2026. The date is the country's Victory Day. She has requested the government to arrange a flight on the national carrier, Biman Bangladesh Airlines, to bring her back to Dhaka. The English news outlet Banglanews24 carried the same information in its report.

Sheikh Hasina is currently in India. She left the country for India on August 5 last year after her government fell in a student-led mass uprising. She has remained there since. Several cases have been filed against her in Bangladesh, and the government describes her as a convicted person. Against this backdrop, her announcement of a return has added a new dimension to the country's politics.

Questions arose as soon as the spokesperson's announcement became public. Will she really return on December 16? What action will be taken against her if she does? Will the government allow her to return? The answers are not yet clear. But the government's initial reaction makes one thing plain: her path back will not be easy.

The government's firm reply

Since Hasina's announcement, the government has issued a series of tough statements. State Minister for Foreign Affairs Humayun Kabir said clearly that there is no scope for sending an aircraft to bring the convicted Sheikh Hasina back. He said that if Hasina returns to the country, she will have to return in handcuffs. He added that she will be arrested if she goes to any Indian mission.

Dr. Zahed Ur Rahman, the prime minister's adviser for information and broadcasting, described the announcement of a return on Victory Day as political deception. In his words, the request for a state aircraft is backed by evil forces. He also said that announcements will keep coming and there is nothing to be afraid of. In other words, the government does not see the announcement as something to fear, but as a political tactic.

The government has also briefed foreign diplomats, explaining its position to them. The government briefed foreign representatives about the sabotage carried out by the Awami League, whose activities are banned. The briefing said that more than 200 acts of sabotage by the Awami League have taken place so far in November. Foreign diplomats responded positively to the government's steps, according to reports.

The state minister for foreign affairs added that no terrorist will ever be allowed to return without extradition. His statement is a clear signal that the government views Hasina's case through a legal process and is not prepared to offer any special facility.

How political parties reacted

The country's main political parties have also reacted to Hasina's announcement. The BNP said that a meeting of fugitive Awami League leaders in India hints at a conspiracy. The party's leader Rizvi alleged that a meeting of four Awami League leaders at the border is part of a dangerous blueprint. In his words, these meetings are being held to create instability in the country. The BNP claims that more than 200 acts of sabotage by the Awami League have taken place so far in this month of November.

The National Citizen Party (NCP) reacted in even stronger language. The party warned that if Hasina returns to the country, a situation like August 5 will be created again. In other words, they are saying that people will once again take to the streets. The NCP's statement has added further heat to the situation.

Jamaat-e-Islami, on the other hand, said that if it receives a letter for dialogue, it will take part with conditions. The party's position shows that it wants to keep the path of dialogue open, but not without its own conditions. Discussion is also continuing over the implementation of the referendum verdict. ATM Azhar said that implementing the referendum verdict is essential to building unity among the people. In his view, without implementing the verdict of the referendum, lasting peace will not return to the country.

Taken together, Hasina's announcement has opened a new debate among the country's political parties. On one side the government and rival political parties are taking a tough stand; on the other, efforts are continuing from the Awami League side to return. Observers believe this tug of war may grow in the coming days.

The picture of sabotage in November

Alongside the announcement of Hasina's return, the picture of sabotage across the country in November also came into discussion today. According to government figures, at least 121 crude bomb explosions and 99 incidents of arson targeting vehicles have taken place across the country so far in November. The prime minister's information and broadcasting adviser, Dr. Zahed Ur Rahman, presented these figures at a press briefing on Tuesday.

A total of 101 platoons of the Border Guard Bangladesh (BGB) have been deployed in Dhaka and various districts to maintain law and order. In the capital's Mirpur-1 area, a passenger bus of Basumati Paribahan was set on fire in front of Sony Cinema Hall. Reports of vehicle arson and crude bomb explosions have also come from different parts of the country.

Law enforcement agencies said terrorists are under surveillance around December. After the arrest of Sweden Aslam, six members of the "Four Star" group have now been arrested. Maximum alert has been issued at the Maulvibazar border amid fears of infiltration. Round-the-clock surveillance is being carried out there with night-vision cameras, CCTV and thermal drones.

The Dhaka Metropolitan Detective Police (DB) arrested Md. Azizul Islam, former member of parliament from Jashore-6, from the capital. A court also granted a further five-day remand for Mohammad Sayeed Khokon, former mayor of Dhaka South City Corporation. The remand was granted in the case of the killing of a teenager named Abdul Motaleb (14) in the capital's Dhanmondi during the July movement last year.

Trial of Ziaul Ahsan at the tribunal

Another important story of the day came from the International Crimes Tribunal. The prosecution has found information linking former military officer Ziaul Ahsan to the killing of at least 115 people. The prosecution presented a grim account of his enforced disappearances and killings before the tribunal. The state has sought the maximum punishment — the death penalty — along with confiscation of his assets.

This case is seen as one of the most important trials in the country's history. The case is part of the trial process for the killings and crimes against humanity committed during the July movement. The prosecution claims Ziaul Ahsan was directly involved in these killings. It is hoped that when the trial process ends, the people of the country will see justice done.

Separately, the first-ever joint military exercise of the three armed forces was held at Swarnadweep. The prime minister visited Swarnadweep, inspected the exercise and watched the skills of the para commandos and naval SWADS. The exercise underlined that the armed forces are ready to face any challenge.

Other major stories of the day

Garment workers' wages: owners offer Tk 15,700, workers demand Tk 22,000

Amid the political tension, another important story of the day concerns the wages of garment workers. The Minimum Wage Board sat again today. This was the board's fourth meeting. At the meeting, garment factory owners proposed a minimum monthly wage of Tk 15,700 for Grade-4 workers. Workers' representatives, on the other hand, lowered their demand to Tk 22,000. The gap between the two sides' proposals is still more than Tk 6,000.

The chairman of the Wage Board, Syed Azad Subhani, disclosed the updated proposals at a press briefing at around 4:30pm. In Gazipur today, garment workers again tried to block roads and protest. Police dispersed them with tear gas and sound grenades. The workers say a wage hike is urgent in line with current market prices and the cost of living. Owners say that raising wages too much would make it difficult to run factories and reduce competitiveness in export markets. Millions of garment workers are now waiting to see whether this gap will narrow at the next meeting.

Nine more die with measles symptoms

The measles outbreak in the country has not yet eased. In the past 24 hours, nine more people died with suspected measles symptoms. In the 24 hours to 8am, 1,283 suspected measles cases were reported across the country. Dhaka and Sylhet divisions reported the highest number of deaths. The information came in a bulletin from the health department.

Health experts say measles is a contagious disease. Children who have not been vaccinated are at the highest risk. They are stressing the need to raise vaccination rates before the winter season begins. Parents have been urged to check whether their children have received the measles vaccine. The health department is also considering special vaccination programmes in the areas where the outbreak is worst.

Gold and oil prices fall

World markets saw fresh pressure on gold prices. Fears that the US Federal Reserve may raise interest rates at its December meeting have unsettled the international gold market. Investors have taken a cautious position. The precious metal remains under sustained pressure. The effect has reached the domestic market too. Gold was selling at higher prices in the country today, but if the downward trend in the world market continues, the local market may change in the coming days.

Oil prices in the world market have fallen sharply, to their lowest in three months. The fall in fuel prices may bring some relief for an oil-importing country like Bangladesh. But analysts warn that volatility in the international market can turn at any time.

3,000 electric buses for Dhaka

The government has taken a major plan for the capital's public transport and to reduce air pollution. A total of 3,000 modern electric buses will be introduced in Dhaka in phases. Once these buses are running, the city's air quality is expected to improve and commuting will become more comfortable for passengers. Dhaka's traffic congestion and pollution are long-standing problems. Urban planners believe electric buses can partly solve them.

Canada to continue trade preferences until 2032

Bangladesh and Canada have reaffirmed their commitment to strengthening bilateral ties. Ottawa has expressed its intention to continue preferential market access for Bangladeshi products until 2032. The two countries concluded their sixth bilateral consultations in Dhaka on Tuesday. The meeting discussed trade, investment, development cooperation and regional and global issues.

Canada is an important market for Bangladesh's garment exports. If this facility continues, the country's exporters will benefit. Economists say Canada's decision is positive news for Bangladesh's economy.

Tk 1,055 crore pledged at climate summit

Bangladesh has received pledges of Tk 1,055 crore at the climate summit. The money will be spent on tackling the harmful effects of climate change. Bangladesh is one of the countries worst affected by climate change. Rising sea levels, cyclones and floods threaten the lives of millions of people in the coastal regions. If the pledges are implemented, the affected people will get some relief.

EC dialogue on UP elections from November 29

To make the upcoming Union Parishad elections fair and acceptable, the Election Commission (EC) will hold dialogue with political parties. The dialogue will take place on November 29 and 30 and December 1. The Election Commission said fair elections will be arranged after taking the views of all parties.

Biman suspends Riyadh flights; actor Sohel Khan dies

Biman Bangladesh Airlines has extended the suspension of its regular flights on the Dhaka-Riyadh-Dhaka route until December 16 amid security concerns following recent airstrikes in Saudi Arabia. Popular actor Sohel Khan has died. His death has cast a shadow of grief over the film world. The weather department has issued a fresh message about rain due to a low-pressure area over the Bay of Bengal.

Background: from August 5 to today

To understand today's announcement, the background must be known. In July 2024, the quota reform movement that began with students turned into a mass uprising in early August. On August 5, Sheikh Hasina's government fell and she left the country for India the same day. She has remained in India since. An interim government was formed in the country, and one case after another was filed against her.

For more than a year, the activities of the Awami League have remained banned. Many of its leaders have fled the country; many have been arrested. Even so, announcements keep coming from the party from time to time. Hasina herself has addressed supporters through audio messages and statements. But this is the first time a specific date has been given for a return, and that is why today's announcement carries special weight.

The interim government has always said Hasina will be brought back and made to face trial. The trial process for the killings during the July movement is under way. Hearings in several cases are continuing at the International Crimes Tribunal. The government says the families of those killed during the movement will get justice.

Meanwhile, allegations of sabotage against Awami League leaders and activists are growing. According to the government, more than 200 acts of sabotage have taken place in November alone. Crude bomb explosions, vehicle arson and road blockades have been reported from different parts of the country. Law enforcement agencies say the fugitive Awami League leaders are directing these incidents. Reports of meetings of fugitive leaders in India are also gaining importance in this context.

The legal process against Hasina

Several cases have been filed against Sheikh Hasina in the country. The government describes her as a convicted person. She faces charges over the killings during the July movement. Cases of crimes against humanity are also under way at the International Crimes Tribunal.

Legal experts say the extradition process must be followed to bring back a convicted person. The state minister for foreign affairs said the same thing — no one will be allowed to return without extradition. Bangladesh has an extradition treaty with India. But bringing someone back under this treaty is complicated, both diplomatically and legally. The Indian government has not yet said anything officially on the matter. But the issue is being discussed in diplomatic circles, according to reports.

The government's position is clear — if Hasina returns, she will have to face the law. She will not be given any special facility. By rejecting the request for a state aircraft, the government has sent exactly that message. What remains to be seen is what she does on December 16 — whether she tries to return on her own initiative, or whether the announcement remains only a tactic to create political pressure.

How today's news affects ordinary people

Beyond Hasina's announcement, today's other stories are directly linked to ordinary people's lives. The wage board's decision will affect the lives of around four million garment workers. Owners have proposed Tk 15,700 and workers Tk 22,000. The gap is more than Tk 6,000. If the gap is not reduced, workers' discontent may grow. But if wages rise too much, small factories will come under pressure. The whole garment sector is waiting for the wage board's decision.

The measles outbreak is also a cause of concern for ordinary families. Nine more people died in the past 24 hours. Parents need to be more aware about vaccinating their children. The health department says special vaccination programmes will be run in the worst-affected areas.

Fluctuations in gold prices are linked to the savings and wedding plans of countless families in the country. Prices rose in the local market today even as world prices fell. Buyers and traders are watching which way prices will move in the coming days. On the other hand, the arrival of 3,000 electric buses in Dhaka is expected to make daily commuting much easier for city residents. Less air pollution will also improve people's health.

Major world news today

Alongside the country's news, several important events took place around the world today. Tensions in the Middle East continue. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson said Iran will use all its tools to confront the US naval blockade. Yesterday, US Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio met separately with Qatar's prime minister at the White House. These meetings are seen as part of diplomatic efforts around the Iran situation.

In Gaza, Israeli airstrikes killed the director of Hamas' internal security apparatus, Naeem Abu Nahel, along with six others. Two police officers were among the dead. A staff member of the UN agency for Palestinian refugees was also killed in an earlier strike, according to reports. Concern is growing in the international community over the situation in Gaza.

The Russia-Ukraine war also claimed lives today. An overnight Russian attack on the city of Zaporizhzhia killed two people and wounded 40, including four children. An apartment building was struck. A new analysis claims more than 700,000 Russian soldiers have been killed in the war, now nearly five years old. NATO chief Mark Rutte said the alliance will defend against any threats in space after reports that Russia is preparing to launch a nuclear-armed satellite.

The COP31 climate talks collapsed with no deal. The European Union and other countries rejected a final text that made no mention of transitioning away from fossil fuels. The world's divisions over tackling climate change have once again become clear. A magnitude 5.4 earthquake struck Nepal's Koshi Province; tremors were felt in India, Bhutan and Bangladesh. In the United States, Democrats won their biggest House majority in decades, which is expected to give them the power to block President Trump's agenda.
